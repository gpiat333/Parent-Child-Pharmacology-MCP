## Metformin: adult label warnings compared with breastfed-infant findings

### Adult medicine-taker label

The retrieved DailyMed label is for **METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]**. The product/formulation match to a particular patient’s medicine was not confirmed. Its **Warnings** section describes lactic acidosis as a rare, serious complication and discusses contexts including renal dysfunction, hypoperfusion or hypoxemia, certain heart-failure contexts, older age, hepatic disease, dehydration, sepsis, and excessive alcohol intake. The retrieved warning text is truncated. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The label’s **Adverse Reactions** section reports gastrointestinal reactions—including diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, indigestion, and abdominal discomfort—in adults with type 2 diabetes. It also lists other reported reactions, including hypoglycemia, dyspnea, rash, and headache. These are findings in the medicine-taker population, not breastfed infants. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

In its lactation section, the label states that animal studies found metformin excreted into milk, that comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers, and that potential hypoglycemia in nursing infants was a concern. This is a label warning or uncertainty statement, not an observed infant outcome. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### Breastfed-infant evidence

LactMed reports measured milk and infant-level concentrations and separately describes observed infant outcomes. [S23](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

**Measured exposure**

- **Milk concentrations:** average **270 mcg/L** in one group; approximately **380 mcg/L** in one sample collected about 4–5 hours after administration.
- **Milk concentrations:** averages of **130 and 270 mcg/L** in two women over the following 72 hours.
- **Milk concentrations:** averages of **130–270 mcg/L** in three mothers.
- **Milk concentrations:** average **410 mcg/L** when trough and peak milk samples were collected 4–17 days postpartum.
- **Infant serum:** measurements were retrieved for four breastfed infants. Two were undetectable at **<10 mcg/L**; two had levels of **50 and 80 mcg/L** at the reported sampling times.
- **Infant serum:** metformin was undetectable at **<5 mcg/L** in two additional infants whose levels were measured.

The retrieved LactMed record does not provide the ages or extent of nursing for some infants with measured levels. These concentrations are research measurements, not medication doses or treatment instructions. [S23](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

**Observed infant outcomes**

- LactMed reports seven infants aged 5–25 months judged healthy, with growth and development progressing as expected; two also had normal Denver developmental screening results.
- Three infants had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin.
- In three breastfed infants 10–11 days postpartum, none had low blood glucose, and their mothers reported no adverse reactions.
- In a study involving 111 infants—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—no differences were found in reported 3- and 6-month height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates between the groups.

These are the outcomes reported in the cited LactMed summaries; they do not convert the adult label warning into an infant outcome. The LactMed extract does not establish a total study count. [S23](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/) [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737874/)
