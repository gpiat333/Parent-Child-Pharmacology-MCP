## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Evidence scope:** Lamotrigine identity was verified. The retrieved evidence consists of one selected DailyMed product label plus a bounded PubMed search; it is not a systematic review. The DailyMed label is for one product/formulation, with effective date **October 12, 2026** and publication date **November 9, 2026**. Its pregnancy text was truncated in one field, although the structured PLLR 8.1 sections were returned. DailyMed states that in-use labeling may differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The label states that most lamotrigine pregnancy data come from women with **epilepsy**. It separately describes pregnancy-associated risks reported with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-drug exposure, including preeclampsia, preterm labor, hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. These are presented as **underlying-disease and treatment-context risks**, not as lamotrigine-specific adverse effects. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also reports that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is source labeling for clinician discussion, not an individualized medication plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A 2023 review of epilepsy in pregnancy and lactation states that pregnancy safety evidence in this population relies substantially on pregnancy registries, cohort studies, case-control studies, and prospective cohorts because large randomized safety trials have not been conducted. The source population is **pregnant women with epilepsy and lactation**, and the publication type is **review**. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)

### Fetal or infant exposure

No numeric human fetal concentration was retrieved.

A 2023 study of **60 mothers**, with source populations including **pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children**, measured psychotropic-medication concentrations in serum and breastmilk at multiple time points and followed exposed children during the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high breastmilk concentration-to-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but does not provide a lamotrigine-specific milk concentration, fetal concentration, or infant-level concentration in the retrieved text. The study design label was not stated in the returned abstract metadata. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

The label reports animal findings, including decreased fetal folate concentrations in rats and developmental findings in animals. These are **animal data**, not measured human fetal exposure. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

For **lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester**, the label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558** exposed infants; 95% CI **1.6%–3.1%**.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0%** of **1,562** exposed infants.
- EURAP registry: major birth defects in **2.9%** of **2,514** exposures; 95% CI **2.3%–3.7%**.

The label states that the frequency of major congenital malformations was similar to general-population estimates. The underlying registry populations and comparator methods differ, so these figures should not be treated as a single pooled estimate. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Isolated oral clefts:** The NAAED Pregnancy Registry reported oral clefts in **3.2 per 1,000** among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy; 95% CI **1.4–6.3**, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**; 95% CI **0.8–2.63**. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age outcomes, and neurodevelopmental delay. It also states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions. The retrieved extract does not establish a lamotrigine-specific effect estimate for each of these outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between its in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborn groups and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. The abstract does not establish the lamotrigine-specific subgroup size or outcome estimates, so this finding should not be generalized to all lamotrigine-exposed pregnancies. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

- Registry findings concern specific populations, exposure periods, and comparator groups; they should not be merged into one estimate. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- Monotherapy findings should not be generalized to polytherapy or unreported co-exposures. The retrieved label data specifically describe monotherapy for the registry percentages. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
