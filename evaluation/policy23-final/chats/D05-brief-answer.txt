## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Identity:** The lookup matched sertraline exactly. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved, but the MCP result is partial and is not a safety verdict. LactMed’s Drug Levels and Effects in Breastfed Infants sections were truncated; the record should not be treated as complete. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### 1. Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed reports galactorrhea case reports in nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients and cites a pharmacovigilance analysis that did not find an increased hyperprolactinemia signal compared with other medicines. These findings do not directly establish an effect during established lactation. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A report described six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; administered regimens were not reported. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed label states that data on sertraline’s effects on milk production are unavailable. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### 2. Measured exposure

Retrieved milk-concentration measurements included:

- Sertraline **45 mcg/L**; range **7–207 mcg/L** in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In 26 women, sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L**; range **11–938 mcg/L**. Norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L**; range **20–1,498 mcg/L**. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- At two months postpartum, mean milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline in four mothers. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a case involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline; sampling times were random. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Foremilk and hindmilk concentrations at four weeks postpartum, 13 hours after exposure, were **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One milk measurement was **<5 mcg/L**; another study reported milk concentrations of **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In six women, the median milk concentration was **11.3 mcg/L**, with a range of **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**; the source notes that sampling timing might affect accuracy. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In 15 mothers, the mean milk concentration was **87 mcg/L**, range **12–288 mcg/L**. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The returned LactMed exposure extract does not include a complete infant-level concentration section. A PubMed abstract reports detectable concentrations of sertraline in three nursing infants and desmethylsertraline in six; it describes the specimen as infant serum. [S52](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

These are research measurements, not dosing instructions. Milk concentrations do not establish safety.

### 3. Observed infant outcomes

The retrieved extract reports:

- One four-month-old infant had benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study of 26 infants reported no detectable acute adverse reactions. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other small reports described no clinically noted adverse reactions or no adverse reactions requiring medical attention in the reported infants. These reports do not establish that unaddressed outcomes were absent. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study involving 30 infants reported blunted pain responses among SSRI-exposed infants; four were exposed to sertraline. The source states that there was no control group of depressed, nonmedicated mothers, so the contribution of maternal depression-related behavior was not resolved. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)[S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled survey of 930 mothers reported infant drug-discontinuation symptoms in about 10% of infants; the extract does not make this finding sertraline-specific. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20807106/)[S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A case involving a preterm infant born at 33 weeks reported hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation abnormalities, and high-pitched crying. The source also reports infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite and describes the authors’ attribution to sertraline exposure through breastmilk in that case. This is a single case report, not a population estimate. [S30](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)[S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Label context

The selected DailyMed label describes low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk and reports no observed adverse reactions in a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs. This is one selected product label, not all sertraline formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
