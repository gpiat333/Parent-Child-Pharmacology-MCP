### 1. Effects on the pregnant person

The retrieved material did not provide a lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-outcome estimate. The DailyMed Clinical Considerations section discusses risks associated with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, including several obstetric and maternal outcomes; the returned extract does not establish that these outcomes were caused by lamotrigine. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The reviews describe pregnancy management as balancing maternal seizure control with fetal considerations. These are review-level discussions, not a lamotrigine-specific maternal outcome study. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)

**Evidence gap:** Maternal lamotrigine-specific outcomes were not established in the retrieved extract. This is not evidence that such outcomes were absent or that the medicine is safe.

### 2. Maternal pharmacokinetics

#### Clinical Considerations

The selected label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The label presents this as clinician-management information; no individualized dose or adjustment plan is provided here. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Literature

- A pregnancy review states that physiological changes during pregnancy affect antiseizure-medication levels and seizure risk. The abstract does not provide numerical lamotrigine pharmacokinetic measurements. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A review specifically covering lamotrigine and other antiseizure medicines lists pharmacokinetics as a topic, but its retrieved abstract does not report numerical lamotrigine pregnancy pharmacokinetic results. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)
- A naturalistic study of 60 mothers measured psychopharmacologic medications in serum and breastmilk at different time points during pregnancy and lactation and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high concentration-by-dose ratio in breastmilk for lamotrigine, but does not provide a lamotrigine-specific maternal serum concentration, clearance estimate, or fetal concentration. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

**Interpretation limit:** Candidate pharmacokinetic passages were retrieved, but human lamotrigine pregnancy pharmacokinetic data were not confirmed as a complete, drug-specific dataset in this extract. No administered regimen, dose estimate, or personal monitoring plan is provided.

### 3. Fetal exposure

No direct fetal lamotrigine concentration measurement was retrieved. The available material therefore describes maternal concentration changes and a breastmilk concentration ratio in a mixed-medication study, but does not quantify fetal exposure. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4) [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

A recruiting observational study is investigating antiseizure-medication clearance, physiological factors affecting blood concentrations in pregnant adults with epilepsy, and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. Its registration and recruitment status indicate an investigation in progress, not posted fetal-exposure results. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### 4. Fetal and infant outcomes

#### Human registry and epidemiologic findings

The selected label reports the following first-trimester lamotrigine-monotherapy registry findings:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558** exposed infants, with a reported 95% confidence interval of 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0%** of **1,562** exposed infants.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9%** of **2,514** first-trimester exposures, with a reported 95% confidence interval of 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that these registry data did not show an overall increased frequency or consistent pattern of major malformations, while also noting that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit interpretation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

For isolated oral clefts, the NAAED registry reported **3.2 per 1,000** among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.8–2.63. These findings are not uniform across the cited sources. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports that the available data did not suggest increased risk of these outcomes with lamotrigine monotherapy, but explicitly limits the conclusions because outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups differed. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A naturalistic mixed-medication study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. The abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific outcome estimate, so this finding should not be treated as a standalone lamotrigine result. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

#### Animal findings

The label separately reports developmental findings in pregnant animals, including reduced fetal body weight, skeletal variations, neurobehavioral abnormalities, offspring mortality, and reduced fetal folate concentrations in rats. These are animal findings and are not fetal human outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### 6. Gaps that remain visible

- No direct fetal lamotrigine concentration was retrieved.
- No numerical, lamotrigine-specific maternal pregnancy pharmacokinetic dataset was retrieved in the abstracts.
- No lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-outcome estimate was retrieved.
