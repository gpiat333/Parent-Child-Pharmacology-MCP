## Sertraline during lactation: research summary

Sertraline was identity-matched as the ingredient sertraline. The lactation evidence below comes primarily from the retrieved LactMed record, revised August 15, 2026; its **Drug Levels** and **Effects in Breastfed Infants** sections were truncated, so this is not a complete record. [S45](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

These are **milk measurements**, not infant outcomes or treatment instructions:

- In pooled data from **15 mothers**, mean breastmilk sertraline concentration was **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**). [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of **26 women** averaging 15.8 weeks postpartum, mean milk concentrations were **129 mcg/L** for sertraline (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and **258 mcg/L** for norsertraline (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**), using extensive sampling over 24 hours. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In four mothers at 2 months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline; samples were collected at random times. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In one report involving a preterm infant, three random milk samples averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Foremilk and hindmilk collected 13 hours after administration contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In nine women providing trough samples during the first postpartum week, sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one woman; the other eight had concentrations of **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**. LactMed notes that the timing of collection could affect these values. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In six women providing random samples, the median concentration was **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**); LactMed notes that these values might be inaccurate because of sample timing. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In 15 mothers providing repeated samples, mean milk concentration was **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**). [S42](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38841362/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

**Infant-level measurements:** The retrieved LactMed extract states that norsertraline is often detectable at low levels in **infant serum**, but the detailed infant-level measurement passage was not returned. Thus, infant specimen results are incomplete in this extract. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

The LactMed record reports:

- In **26 infants** in one study, no acute adverse reactions were detected during the study. The source population was breastfed infants of mothers receiving sertraline; the design label was not stated in the returned passage. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of **14 mother–infant pairs**, no adverse effects were reported, including among six exclusively breastfed infants younger than 3 months. Infant whole-blood serotonin measurements were also reported; this was a measured biological outcome, not a milk concentration. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11578995/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In **25 infants** followed through 6 months, reported weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In **six infants aged 5–34 weeks**, no clinical adverse reactions were noted at the time of the study. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367050/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In **seven 4-week-old infants**, no adverse effects were reported. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367055/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One **4-month-old infant** had benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of infants of depressed mothers exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy alone or during pregnancy and breastfeeding, blunted pain responses were reported compared with infants of nondepressed, unexposed mothers. Four of 30 exposed infants received sertraline. The returned passage states that there was no control group of depressed, nonmedicated mothers, so an effect related to maternal depression or behavior could not be excluded. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled online survey of **930 mothers** using antidepressants reported discontinuation-type symptoms in about 10% of infants; the finding was not sertraline-specific and included pregnancy as well as breastfeeding exposure. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20807106/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a telephone study of mothers taking benzodiazepines while nursing, one mother also taking sertraline and other medicines reported sedation in her breastfed infant. Because of the co-exposures and the limited information returned, attribution to sertraline is not established. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22504099/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

#### Separate case report

LactMed reports one case involving a **preterm infant born at 33 weeks**:

- The mother used sertraline during gestation, at delivery, and postpartum while exclusively breastfeeding.
- The infant developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation problems, and high-pitched crying during the first 24 hours; symptoms worsened on day 4 while breastfeeding continued.
- Breastfeeding was discontinued on day 9; the source reports that symptoms then dissipated and infant drug concentrations decreased.
- The infant was later found to have genetically intermediate metabolism of two CYP450 enzymes.
- The authors attributed the presentation to serotonergic overstimulation associated with persistent exposure through breastmilk and reduced metabolism. This is a single case report, not an incidence estimate. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Lactation and milk-production findings

These findings concern the lactating person or breastfeeding process, not direct infant toxicity:

- A report involving six people described decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; the returned passage does not provide administered amounts, and the report was observational.
- A small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor exposure included only one sertraline-exposed participant and reported delayed onset of lactogenesis compared with controls; the returned passage states that there was no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day 4.
- Other cited studies examined SSRI or antidepressant exposure during pregnancy or postpartum and breastfeeding outcomes, but several were not sertraline-specific and may be influenced by the underlying maternal condition. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19965920/) [S24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22344850/) [S32](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28984528/) [S34](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29522259/) [S41](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35170756/) [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The selected DailyMed label states that there are no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production. The retrieved product was **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**; the exact product match was not confirmed. The label’s lactation section reports a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which no adverse reactions were observed, but this is label evidence and should not be treated as a complete review of all lactation studies. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Evidence limitations and context

- The retrieved LactMed record does not establish a total study count; its reference count is not a study count. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The returned material includes small pharmacokinetic studies, observational reports, and case reports; the design label was not stated for every study.
- Infant age, prematurity, breastfeeding extent, co-exposures, sampling timing, and follow-up varied or were sometimes unspecified.
- The LactMed concentration and infant-outcome sections were truncated; omitted information is missing from the retrieved extract, not evidence that no additional findings exist.
- PubMed abstracts were not separately retrieved in this lookup. Findings above are therefore reported as findings summarized in the LactMed record; the absence of an abstract here is a retrieval limitation, not evidence that an article lacks an abstract.
- Milk concentrations, infant-level measurements, observed symptoms, and source-attributed interpretations are different types of evidence. A measured concentration does not itself establish a clinical outcome or safety.
