**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (2022)  
**PMID:** 34131926 [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

**Study design/methods:** A formal design label was not stated in the abstract. The reported methods involved breastfeeding women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam who completed a questionnaire. Researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples from pre-dose through 12 hours post-dose and in one pre-dose maternal serum sample. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

**Population:** 20 breastfeeding women and 21 infants; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed. Ages, prematurity, and other detailed population characteristics were not provided in the abstract. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

**Drug exposure:** The trough breast-milk/serum levetiracetam ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**. The abstract also reports correlations among maternal levetiracetam dose, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk AUC values. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

**Observed infant outcomes:** Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding. The abstract reports that all infants gained weight according to age. It does not establish that levetiracetam caused the somnolence, and it does not provide longer-term infant outcomes. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
