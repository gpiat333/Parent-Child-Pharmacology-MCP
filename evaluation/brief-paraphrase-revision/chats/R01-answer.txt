LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin in breastfed infants**:

### Observed symptoms
- **Diarrhea:** reported in 3 of 25 infants in one telephone follow-up and in 2 of 40 infants in another prospective study.
- **Rash:** reported in 1 of those 40 infants.
- **Rash and somnolence:** reported in a comparison study; adverse reactions occurred in 8.3% of amoxicillin-exposed infants, a rate similar to the comparison group.
- **One case of generalized urticaria (hives):** a 2-month-old infant developed hives after breastfeeding while the mother was taking amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin. The hives recurred after another breastfeeding exposure later that day. The responsible drug could not be determined; the report considered the antibiotic combination, most likely amoxicillin/clavulanate, the probable cause.
- In a study of 25 infants, **no adverse effects were reported**.

### Advice to monitor—not observed symptoms
A controlled study asked mothers to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were monitoring targets, not reported symptoms in that study; no statistically significant differences were found between the comparison groups.

### Uncertainty
LactMed states that rash and gastrointestinal-flora disruption, potentially resulting in diarrhea or thrush, have been reported but **have not been adequately evaluated**. The reports do not consistently establish causation: some involved other medicines or combination products, and one infant’s exposure to amoxicillin alone was not isolated. Infant blood-level information was not found as of the LactMed revision date. The extract does not establish a study count; the number of cited references is not a study count.
