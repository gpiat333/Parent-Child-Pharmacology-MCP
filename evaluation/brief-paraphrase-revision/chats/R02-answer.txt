Yes—these are different kinds of evidence.

**Measured in breast milk (drug-exposure evidence):**
- Sumatriptan was measured in milk from small groups of breastfeeding women after subcutaneous, nasal, and oral use.
- In one group of five women, the average peak milk concentration was 87.2 mcg/L; in another group of seven women, the average peak was 59.3 mcg/L.
- LactMed reports calculated infant exposure estimates from these milk concentrations.
- **Published infant blood/drug levels were not found** in the LactMed record. Milk concentration therefore indicates potential exposure; it is not a measurement of the infant’s blood level or a clinical effect. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

**Observed in infants (clinical-outcome evidence):**
- LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence concerning three women who used sumatriptan while breastfeeding; none reported adverse effects in their infants.
- The dose and route were unspecified, and this was not presented as a controlled study. It is a small, limited observation—not proof that no breastfed infant can experience an effect. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

So, the milk data answer **“Was drug present in milk, and in what amount?”** The infant reports answer **“What was observed clinically in some infants?”** These should not be treated as interchangeable: measured exposure is pharmacokinetic evidence, whereas reported infant effects are clinical-outcome evidence.
