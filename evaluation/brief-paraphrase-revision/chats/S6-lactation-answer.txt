## Sertraline during lactation: balanced evidence summary

**Identity:** The retrieved record matched sertraline as the ingredient (RxNorm RxCUI 36437). It is distinct from any complete product or combination-drug field. [S9](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Measured exposure: milk and infant serum

LactMed reports that sertraline is measurable in breast milk, while infant serum concentrations are often undetectable or low; norsertraline, its metabolite, may be detectable. These are **exposure measurements**, not clinical outcomes. Milk concentration alone does not establish harm, and an undetectable infant level does not prove that adverse effects are impossible. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

Examples of reported exposure studies:

- In **30 breastfed infants**, 22 had undetectable infant-serum sertraline and 8 had detectable levels. The population included infants aged 6–13 weeks; 19 were exclusively breastfed and 11 were breastfed at least half the time. The study type is observational exposure measurement. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In **22 breastfed infants**, 4 had detectable sertraline and 11 had detectable norsertraline. Most were exclusively breastfed; the infants’ mean age was about 17 weeks. This was an observational pharmacokinetic study of mothers being treated for severe depression. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other small lactation groups reported undetectable infant serum concentrations, sometimes with detectable norsertraline. The returned record does not establish a single overall study count. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes and adverse findings

- **Case report:** LactMed reports benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation in one breastfed infant. The returned extract does not provide enough detail to establish causation. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Preterm case report:** A 33-week breastfed infant had symptoms described as similar to neonatal-abstinence symptoms, with measurable sertraline and norsertraline in serum. The source also reported genetically intermediate metabolism. This was a premature-infant case and should not be generalized to term infants. The returned extract is truncated, so the full chronology and clinical details are unavailable. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Observational group of 26 breastfed infants:** No acute adverse reactions were detected during the study. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Observational group of 25 infants:** Reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Small study of 30 infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy and/or through breast milk:** Blunted pain responses were reported compared with infants of nondepressed, unexposed mothers; only 4 infants had sertraline exposure. Because there was no control group of depressed mothers who were not taking medication, the study could not separate medication exposure from effects associated with maternal depression or related factors. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Uncontrolled survey of 930 nursing mothers using antidepressants:** About 10% reported infant discontinuation-type symptoms, but the result was not sertraline-specific and relied on self-report. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Mixed-exposure report:** One infant’s sedation was reported in the context of sertraline plus other sedating medicines. The contribution of sertraline alone cannot be determined from that report. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These findings describe observed outcomes; they do not by themselves establish that sertraline caused the reported events. Source recommendations or general cautions should not be treated as evidence that a symptom occurred.

### Relevant context and limitations

- The retrieved LactMed record concerns **lactation**. It also discusses prenatal SSRI exposure, but prenatal findings should not be treated as effects caused by breast milk alone. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
