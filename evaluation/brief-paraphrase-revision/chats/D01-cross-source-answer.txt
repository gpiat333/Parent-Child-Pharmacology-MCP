## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured milk and infant exposure

LactMed reports low sertraline concentrations in breast milk. Norsertraline, an active metabolite, was also detected in milk and was often detectable at low levels in infant serum. In most reported infant samples, sertraline was undetectable; norsertraline was detected more often. These are measured-exposure findings, not clinical outcomes or a personal safety determination. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

One reported case involved a preterm infant with symptoms resembling neonatal abstinence and measurable infant serum concentrations of sertraline and norsertraline. The source also reported genetically intermediate metabolism in that infant. This is a case-specific temporal association; it does not establish that sertraline caused the symptoms in all infants. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

The returned LactMed extract describes:

- No acute adverse effects observed in several small observational groups.
- No abnormal effects reported by mothers in one small group followed through 6 months, with normal reported weight gain.
- One report of benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation.
- No developmental adverse effects identified in infants followed up to 5 years in the cited evidence.
- A study of SSRI-exposed infants reported altered pain responses, but only a small number had sertraline exposure and maternal depression could not be excluded as a contributing factor. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These findings come from mixed source populations and study types, including observational reports, case reports, and a meta-analysis; the returned metadata does not provide a study count. The LactMed extract is incomplete: the infant-effects section is truncated, and the “Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk” section was omitted by the retrieval limit. Therefore, this is not a complete LactMed record, and no-effects findings should not be interpreted as proof of safety or as evidence that sertraline is well studied. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
