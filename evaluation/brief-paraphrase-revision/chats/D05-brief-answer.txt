## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Identity:** The lookup matched sertraline as the ingredient (RxCUI 36437). [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Maternal effects

- The selected DailyMed record is **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, SPL version 13, effective date 2026-11-05. It is one product label, not all formulations. Its lactation section reports low milk levels of sertraline and metabolites but states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- A retrieved postpartum-depression review discusses sertraline in relation to breastfeeding, but the returned metadata gives its source population as **pregnancy**, not confirmed lactation. It should not be generalized as direct lactation evidence. [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38811474/)

### Measured infant exposure

- In a study of **12 lactating women** and **11 mother–infant pairs**—source population labels: **pregnancy, lactation, neonate**; study type: journal article—sertraline and desmethylsertraline were measured in milk and infant serum. Sertraline was detected in three infants and desmethylsertraline in six. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed describes additional small lactation studies in which infant serum sertraline was often undetectable, while norsertraline was sometimes detectable. These are exposure measurements, not a clinical safety verdict. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- The retrieved LactMed extract reports one infant with benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation, while other small groups reported no observed acute adverse reactions, normal reported growth, or no abnormal effects. These observations do not establish causation or prove safety. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study involving **30 infants**, of whom **4 were exposed to sertraline**, reported blunted pain responses among SSRI-exposed infants. The source lacked a depressed, nonmedicated comparison group, so attribution to sertraline or breastmilk exposure was uncertain. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One report involved a preterm infant and concurrent prenatal, delivery, and lactational exposure; the retrieved extract is truncated, so the complete outcome chronology is unavailable. Do not treat this partial record as a complete case report. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
