## Pregnancy evidence brief: lamotrigine

**Evidence status:** Lamotrigine identity was an exact match in RxNorm. The retrieved evidence is not a safety verdict and is not clinically validated. It consists mainly of pregnancy registries, observational studies, reviews, and product labeling. Pregnancy evidence is not exhaustive. [S26](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)

### Effects on the pregnant person

- The selected DailyMed product label reports that pregnancy-related physiological changes may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It describes reported decreases in concentrations during pregnancy and return toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is labeling context for clinician discussion, not an individualized treatment plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- Reviews of pregnant people with epilepsy describe the clinical importance of maintaining seizure control while considering fetal outcomes. These are review-level statements, not individualized predictions. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)
- The retrieved label discusses risks associated with epilepsy itself, including obstetric and maternal complications. The extract does not provide a separate lamotrigine-specific analysis of those maternal outcomes; disease-associated risks and medication-associated risks should therefore remain distinct. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Fetal exposure

- The human pregnancy registry evidence measures or classifies pregnancies by maternal lamotrigine exposure, especially first-trimester monotherapy exposure. The retrieved material does not provide a complete quantitative profile of fetal lamotrigine concentrations.
- A naturalistic study of 60 mothers measured medication concentrations during pregnancy and lactation and followed exposed children for the first year. The abstract does not establish the lamotrigine-specific pregnancy sample size or provide a complete fetal-concentration analysis. Its lamotrigine-specific concentration statement concerns breastmilk, not measured fetal exposure. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- A current recruiting observational study examines antiseizure-medication clearance, maternal blood concentrations, and exposure to unborn children in pregnant adults with epilepsy. It is **RECRUITING**, has no posted results, and its registry status does not establish safety or benefit. The returned study population is pregnant adults with epilepsy; exact individual eligibility was not confirmed. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

**Gap:** Direct measured fetal lamotrigine exposure was not clearly reported in the retrieved abstracts or label extract. The absence of such a result in this bounded retrieval is not proof that no such evidence exists elsewhere.

### Fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

For first-trimester lamotrigine **monotherapy**, the selected label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: 2.2% among 1,558 exposed infants.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: 2.0% among 1,562 exposed infants.
- EURAP: 2.9% among 2,514 exposures.

The label states that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These findings come from pregnancy-registry populations, predominantly people with epilepsy, and should not automatically be generalized to polytherapy, other indications, or every formulation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

- The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy, compared with unexposed healthy controls.
- The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries.
- A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45, with a confidence interval that included no association.

These findings are inconsistent across sources and do not establish causation or a consistent lamotrigine effect. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Other fetal and child outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while also stating that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This is a source-reported limitation, not proof that the outcomes are absent or that the population is fully studied. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant developmental differences during the first year. However, the abstract does not provide enough lamotrigine-specific sample or comparator detail to attribute all findings specifically to lamotrigine. The returned source population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children; the exact lamotrigine-specific population was missing in the returned metadata. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

#### Animal evidence

The selected label reports developmental toxicity findings in animals, including fetal growth and skeletal findings, offspring neurobehavioral findings, increased offspring mortality in one rat study, and decreased fetal folate concentrations. These are animal findings and should not be treated as direct estimates of human fetal risk. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Maternal pharmacokinetic evidence

**Clinical Considerations:** The label reports pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations and restoration toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. It identifies possible effects on therapeutic response, but does not provide an individualized monitoring or adjustment plan in this brief. **Literature:**

The source population was pregnancy, with age details missing in the returned metadata. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- The naturalistic study measured serum and breastmilk concentrations over pregnancy and lactation in 60 mothers, but the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific maternal pharmacokinetic results sufficient to quantify clearance or concentration changes. - A review explicitly covering lamotrigine pharmacokinetics in pregnancy was retrieved, but only its abstract was available in this lookup. **Interpretation:** The retrieved evidence supports that maternal lamotrigine exposure can change during pregnancy and around delivery, but the magnitude, timing, individual variability, and relationship to clinical outcomes were not fully established here. This is an evidence limitation, not evidence of safety or harm.

### Additional limitations

### Questions for a clinician or pharmacist

1. Does this evidence apply to the exact lamotrigine product, formulation, indication, and any other active ingredients?
