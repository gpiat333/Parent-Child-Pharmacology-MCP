## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Overall:** Human evidence is mainly from pregnancy registries, epidemiologic studies, reviews, and a practice guideline; no large randomized pregnancy safety trials were identified in the retrieved material. The evidence is not a safety verdict.

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

- The retrieved DailyMed product label is for **“LAMOTRIGINE KIT … LAMOTRIGINE TABLET [OWP Pharmaceuticals, Inc.]”**, effective **October 12, 2026**. Its pregnancy section was truncated in the returned extract, and the label represents one product rather than all formulations. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- Most pregnancy exposure data in the label are from **pregnant women with epilepsy**. The label describes adverse pregnancy outcomes associated with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, including hypertensive disorders, preterm labor, bleeding, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. These are underlying-disease context, not findings shown to be caused by lamotrigine. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A review of **pregnancy populations with epilepsy** states that pregnancy evidence commonly comes from registries, cohort studies, and case-control studies because large randomized safety trials are lacking. It describes lamotrigine as having a relatively lower risk profile than some antiseizure medicines, but this is review-level interpretation rather than a drug-specific causal finding. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)
- A separate narrative review concerns **pregnant and postpartum people with bipolar disorder**, not epilepsy. Its population is therefore not interchangeable with the epilepsy evidence. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Maternal pharmacokinetics and measured exposure

- The label reports that pregnancy-related physiological changes can affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reported concentration changes during pregnancy and after delivery. This is source-label pharmacokinetic context, not an individualized dosing instruction. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers**, with a mixed source population of **pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children**, measured psychotropic-drug concentrations in serum and breast milk and followed children for the first 12 months. The abstract reports lamotrigine milk-concentration findings, but the returned abstract does not establish a lamotrigine-specific fetal concentration result. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- **NCT05450978** is a **recruiting observational study** of pregnant **adult women with epilepsy**; its summary concerns antiseizure-medication clearance and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. The returned record reports no posted results, and eligibility was not confirmed. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### Fetal and infant outcomes

**Major congenital malformations**

For **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy** exposure, the label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2% among 1,558 infants**.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: **2.0% among 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: **2.9% among 2,514 exposures**.

The label states that overall frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These populations were pregnancy-registry populations, primarily involving epilepsy; they should not automatically be generalized to polytherapy, other indications, or every formulation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Oral clefts**

- The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts in **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants** exposed early in pregnancy, described in the label as higher than in unexposed healthy controls.
- The label states that this signal was not observed in other large international registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 with a confidence interval compatible with no association.

This is an inconsistent signal across human studies, not a settled causal conclusion. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Other outcomes**

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while also stating that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation. This is a limitation of the evidence, not proof that those outcomes cannot occur. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or development during the first 12 months between its exposed and nonexposed groups, but the returned abstract included multiple psychotropic medicines and did not establish lamotrigine-specific results for every outcome. Its source population was mixed: pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

**Animal findings**

The label separately reports developmental toxicity findings in animal studies, including fetal growth and skeletal changes, neurobehavioral findings, fetal-folate changes, and later-gestation/postnatal mortality. These animal findings should not be presented as observed human outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
