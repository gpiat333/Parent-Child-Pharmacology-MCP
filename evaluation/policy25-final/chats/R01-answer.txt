LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin**:

**Observed infant symptoms**
- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, 3 mothers reported diarrhea in their infants; no rash or candidiasis was reported in that study. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8498418/)
- In a prospective study of 40 infants exposed to amoxicillin in breast milk, 2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16044108/)
- In a study comparing amoxicillin with a macrolide antibiotic, reported reactions among amoxicillin-exposed infants occurred in 8.3% and included rash and somnolence. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19366316/)
- A case report described a 2-month-old who developed generalized urticaria about 20 minutes after breastfeeding following the mother’s first exposure to a combination of amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin. The hives recurred after a later breastfeeding episode. The report attributed the reaction probably to antibiotics in breast milk, but the responsible drug could not be determined; amoxicillin/clavulanate was considered most likely. [S2](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1472-8206.2009.00689.x)
- Another report described mild diarrhea in an 11-month-old whose mother was taking amoxicillin and acetaminophen plus codeine; the relationship was rated possible or probable. [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40183105/)
- In a study of 25 mothers whose infants were older than 4 weeks, no infant adverse effects were reported. [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41582095/)

**Advice to monitor**
- LactMed describes a study in which mothers were asked to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, changes in stool frequency or consistency, diaper rash, and skin rash; weight and jaundice were also recorded. These were study-assessment items, not evidence that those symptoms occurred or a separate general monitoring recommendation. [S1](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.1991.tb05523.x)
- The retrieved LactMed text does not provide a distinct general recommendation to monitor for diarrhea, rash, thrush, or somnolence.
