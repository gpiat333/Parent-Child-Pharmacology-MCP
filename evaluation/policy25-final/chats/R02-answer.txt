### Measured exposure

LactMed reports these **sumatriptan concentrations in breast milk**:

- Peak milk concentration: **87.2 mcg/L** (range **62–113 mcg/L**), measured in five breastfeeding women; sampling context was approximately **2.5 hours** after administration. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8866921/)
- In a separate group of seven breastfeeding women, peak milk concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** (range **24.6–112.8 mcg/L**), occurring **2–4 hours** after administration. The average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L** (range **7.7–50.6 mcg/L**). [S2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33730376/)

**Infant-level measurements:** LactMed states that relevant published information on infant levels was **not found as of the record’s revision date**. The retrieved information therefore concerns milk concentrations, not measured infant concentrations. [S7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence stating that, among **three women** known to the manufacturer who used sumatriptan while breastfeeding, **none reported adverse effects in their infants**. The exposure amount and route were unspecified. [S7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

These are different evidence types: milk concentrations measure **drug exposure in milk**; the three-person report describes **reported infant observations**. The milk measurements do not themselves establish an infant outcome, and the reported infant observations do not provide measured infant drug levels. The retrieved extract does not establish a study count and is not a safety verdict.
