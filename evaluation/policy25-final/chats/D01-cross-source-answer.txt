## Sertraline during breastfeeding

The tool confirmed the ingredient as **sertraline**. The retrieved evidence does not determine an individual treatment plan.

### Measured exposure

LactMed reports the following **milk concentrations**:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers, breast-milk sertraline averaged **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**). [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/)
- In a study of 26 women with extensive 24-hour sampling, sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**), and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). Milk concentrations were higher in hindmilk than foremilk. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In a case involving a preterm infant, breast-milk concentrations at random times averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)

Therefore, infant-level information is missing from the returned exposure extract; no infant specimen type should be inferred. The LactMed summary states that sertraline is usually not detected in infant serum and that norsertraline is often detectable at low levels in infant serum. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports:

- One 4-month-old infant had benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and transient agitation. [S44](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9108810/)
- In a study of 26 infants, no acute adverse reactions were detected during the study. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In 14 mother–infant pairs, infant whole-blood serotonin values were similar before and after maternal treatment; no adverse effects were reported in those infants. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11578995/)
- In a follow-up of 25 infants, reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- No clinical adverse reactions were noted in six infants aged 5–34 weeks in one study. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367050/)
- One case report involving a preterm infant described hyperthermia, altered muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying. The report also described infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite and stated that the symptoms dissipated after breastfeeding was discontinued. The case authors attributed the reaction to sertraline exposure through breast milk. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- A small study reported blunted pain responses in infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy, with or without breastfeeding exposure; four of the 30 infants had sertraline exposure. The returned LactMed text states that the study lacked a control group of depressed, unmedicated mothers, so the contribution of maternal depression could not be ruled out. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)
