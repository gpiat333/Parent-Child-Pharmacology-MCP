# Pregnancy evidence brief: lamotrigine

**Identity:** Lamotrigine was matched exactly in RxNorm (RxCUI 28439). [S26](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved, but this is a bounded source review rather than a systematic review or clinical validation. The retrieved evidence does not determine an individual treatment plan.

## 1. Effects on the pregnant person

### Lamotrigine-specific maternal effects

**No lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-outcome findings were retrieved in the reviewed pregnancy material.** This is not evidence that maternal effects are absent. The selected DailyMed label’s standalone adult adverse-reaction and warning sections were missing from the retrieval, so those sections cannot be assessed here. The selected product was:

- **LAMOTRIGINE KIT (LAMOTRIGINE) KIT LAMOTRIGINE TABLET [OWP Pharmaceuticals, Inc.]**
- SPL version 15
- Effective date: October 12, 2026

DailyMed states that this is one selected product label rather than all lamotrigine formulations, and that its in-use labeling can differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Underlying-condition context

For pregnancy in people with epilepsy, the label describes disease-associated maternal and embryofetal risks, including obstetric complications and risks associated with untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures. These are not lamotrigine-specific adverse effects. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A pregnancy-and-lactation review states that antiseizure treatment may be required to protect pregnant people with epilepsy from seizures; its abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific maternal outcome estimate. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)

## 2. Maternal pharmacokinetic evidence

### Clinical Considerations

The label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The label frames possible treatment adjustment as a clinician consideration; it does not provide an individualized plan. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Literature

- A pregnancy review states that physiological changes during pregnancy affect antiseizure-medication levels and seizure risk, and discusses serum-concentration monitoring. The abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific quantitative PK result. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A review of lamotrigine and other antiseizure medicines in pregnancy and breastfeeding explicitly covers clinical pharmacokinetics, but the retrieved abstract provides only the review scope; detailed lamotrigine PK findings were not retrieved. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** measured psychopharmacologic medication concentrations in serum and breastmilk at different time points and followed exposed children during the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high concentration-by-dose ratio in breastmilk for lamotrigine, but it does not provide a verified numeric maternal pregnancy concentration or lamotrigine-specific pregnancy PK trajectory. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

**PK gap:** No verified numeric lamotrigine maternal clearance, concentration, exposure, or area-under-the-curve result was retrieved in the reviewed passages. The MCP-generated interpretation is that the available PK passages require inspection of the underlying study population and design; no personalized dose adjustment was inferred.

## 3. Fetal exposure

No quantitative fetal lamotrigine concentration or fetal-to-maternal concentration ratio was retrieved.

The retrieved pregnancy label reports fetal outcomes among exposed pregnancies but does not provide measured fetal concentrations. A currently listed recruiting study concerns antiseizure-medication clearance, maternal blood concentrations, and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants; its registration and planned measurements are not observed outcome findings. [S8](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

The retrieved breastmilk concentration statement from the 60-mother study concerns lactation exposure, not fetal exposure, and should not be used as a fetal concentration estimate. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

## 4. Observed fetal and infant outcomes

### Human pregnancy-registry findings reported in the label

For **lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester**, the label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558** exposed infants; reported 95% CI **1.6%–3.1%**.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0%** of **1,562** exposed infants.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9%** of **2,514** first-trimester exposures; reported 95% CI **2.3%–3.7%**.

The label states that overall malformation frequency was similar to general-population estimates. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also reports that the NAAED Pregnancy Registry observed isolated oral clefts among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy, with a reported frequency of **3.2 per 1,000** and a reported 95% CI of **1.4–6.3**, described in that registry analysis as threefold higher than unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. It also reports a European case-control study with an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45** and a 95% CI of **0.8–2.63**. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It also states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions. Underlying study counts and detailed subgroup results were not provided in the retrieved passage. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Mixed-medication observational findings

A naturalistic study of 60 mothers reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. The returned abstract concerns multiple psychotropic medicines and does not establish that these findings were specific to lamotrigine. The source population labels were mixed: pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Guideline abstract

The AAN/AES/SMFM practice-guideline abstract addresses congenital, perinatal, and neurodevelopmental outcomes after antiseizure-medication exposure among children born to people with epilepsy of childbearing potential. The abstract includes lamotrigine among the medicines considered, but it does not provide lamotrigine-specific outcome estimates beyond the label findings summarized above. [S24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)

### Animal findings

The label reports developmental findings in pregnant mice, rats, and rabbits, including fetal body-weight changes, skeletal variations, rat offspring neurobehavioral findings, and decreased fetal folate concentrations. These are animal findings and are not human fetal outcomes. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- All **8 requested PubMed records had abstract text retrieved**; none were reported as lacking an abstract, unreturned, or failed in the abstract-coverage field. This does not make the literature search exhaustive.
- No verified numeric fetal lamotrigine concentration was retrieved.
- No verified numeric maternal pregnancy PK result specific to lamotrigine was retrieved.
- Maternal adverse-reaction and adult warning sections were missing for the selected DailyMed label.
- Study populations, gestational timing, co-medications, epilepsy severity, and comparator details were not consistently available in the retrieved abstracts.
- A curated federal pregnancy counterpart to LactMed was not identified in this source review. The MCP identifies this as a coverage limitation, not proof that no other resource exists.
