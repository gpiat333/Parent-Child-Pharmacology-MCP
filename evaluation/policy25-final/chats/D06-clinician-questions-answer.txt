## Questions to ask your clinician

The evidence brief suggests asking:

1. Does this evidence apply to my exact sertraline product, formulation, and any other medicines or supplements I use?
2. How closely do the study populations match my situation and my infant’s situation, including infant age, prematurity, health conditions, and degree of breastfeeding?
3. Which findings measure drug exposure, and which report observed maternal, milk-production, or infant outcomes?
4. What is known about sertraline and norsertraline measured in breast milk and in infant specimens?
5. Which findings come from small studies or case reports, and which come from larger studies?
6. How should the reported breastfeeding and milk-supply findings be interpreted when some studies involved antidepressants or SSRIs generally rather than sertraline specifically?
7. What evidence is missing for premature infants, newborns, or longer-term development?
8. What follow-up would you recommend for my infant in my circumstances?
9. How should my clinical need for sertraline and the risks of changing treatment be considered alongside the breastfeeding evidence?
10. Would input from a pharmacist, pediatric clinician, or lactation specialist be useful?

### Measured exposure reported by LactMed

LactMed reported breast-milk concentrations in different study contexts, including:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**) in a pooled analysis.
- In a study with repeated sampling: sertraline **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- At two months postpartum in one report: sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L**.
- In one preterm-infant case, breast-milk sertraline and norsertraline averaged **201 mcg/L** and **358 mcg/L**, respectively, at random sampling times.
- Foremilk and hindmilk concentrations collected 13 hours after administration were **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively.
- Other reported milk measurements included **less than 5 mcg/L**, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**), and **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).

The returned LactMed extract did **not** include a complete infant-level concentration section; infant-level information was retrieved separately in the PubMed abstract for one study, which stated that sertraline was detected in three nursing infants and desmethylsertraline in six. These are research measurements, not individualized dosing guidance. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) [S52](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
