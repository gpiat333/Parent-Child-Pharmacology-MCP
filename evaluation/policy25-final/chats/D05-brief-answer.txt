## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Identity:** The evidence brief matched the ingredient **sertraline** exactly. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed reports six patients who observed decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; administered regimens were not reported. It also describes a small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitor exposure in which lactogenesis II was delayed by an average of 16.7 hours, while feeding difficulties after day 4 did not differ statistically between groups. The study included one sertraline-exposed participant, so the finding was not specific to sertraline. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed product label states that published data demonstrate low levels of sertraline and its metabolites in human milk, and that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. This label applies to one selected product, **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured exposure

Retrieved LactMed milk concentrations included:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L**, range **7–207 mcg/L**, in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a 26-woman milk-sampling study: sertraline average **129 mcg/L**, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline average **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1,498 mcg/L**. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other reported sertraline milk concentrations included **26.4 mcg/L**, **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, **26 mcg/L** in foremilk and **46 mcg/L** in hindmilk, **<5 mcg/L** in one sample, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** with a range of **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**, and **87 mcg/L** with a range of **12–288 mcg/L**. Sampling times varied; one passage states that some values might be inaccurate because of sample timing. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The returned LactMed concentration extract did not include a complete infant-level measurement section. Separately, the abstract for one study states that detectable concentrations were found in three nursing infants for sertraline and six for desmethylsertraline, with infant serum concentrations below the detection limit of most commercial laboratories. The source’s specimen terminology is “infant serum.” [S52](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

These are research measurements, not individualized exposure estimates or treatment instructions.

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in one 4-month-old infant and spontaneously resolving agitation in a case report. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In several small groups described by LactMed, the source reports no observed acute adverse reactions, no clinically noted adverse reactions at the time of study, or no adverse effects in the specific groups studied. These observations were from small, differing populations and do not establish the absence of effects in all breastfed infants. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate abstract involving 12 mothers states that no adverse effects of exposure were observed in the infants studied. [S52](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed describes one preterm infant born at 33 weeks who developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation disorders, and high-pitched crying. The case report states that infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite were measured and that symptoms dissipated after breastfeeding was discontinued; this is a single case report and the returned extract does not establish how often such an event occurs. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) [S30](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- A study of infants exposed to an SSRI prenatally alone or prenatally and through breastmilk reported blunted pain responses compared with controls; four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline. The returned passage states that no control group of depressed, nonmedicated mothers was included, and the relevant contribution of maternal depression was therefore not established in that study. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)
