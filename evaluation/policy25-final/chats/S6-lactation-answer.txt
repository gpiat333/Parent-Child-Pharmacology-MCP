## Sertraline during lactation

### Measured exposure

The retrieved LactMed record reports these **milk concentrations**:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers: sertraline **45 mcg/L** on average, range **7–207 mcg/L**. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/)
- In a study of 26 women with extensive 24-hour milk sampling: sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). The source states that concentrations were higher in hindmilk than foremilk. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In four mothers at two months postpartum: sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L** at random sampling times. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In one mother, foremilk and hindmilk collected 13 hours after an exposure contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In nine women providing first-week postpartum trough samples, sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one woman and **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in the other eight; the source notes that timing could affect these values. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In six women providing random samples, the median sertraline concentration was **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**); the source notes that the sampling timing might make these values inaccurate. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In 15 mothers whose milk was sampled at prespecified times over two days, the average concentration was **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**). [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These are research measurements, not administered amounts or individualized exposure estimates. The retrieved LactMed extract does **not** include a complete infant-level concentration passage: its infant-level information is missing from the returned Drug Levels extract, which was truncated. ### Observed infant outcomes

- One **4-month-old infant** had benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation in a case report. The retrieved passage does not establish that sertraline caused either finding. [S44](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9108810/)
- In a study of **26 infants**, no acute adverse reactions attributed to sertraline in milk were detected during the study. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In a study of **14 mother–infant pairs**, infant whole-blood serotonin values were measured before and after maternal treatment; the report states that none of the infants experienced adverse effects. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11578995/)
- In a report involving **25 infants**, the authors reported normal six-month weight gain and no abnormal effects reported by the mothers. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- A case report describes a **preterm infant born at 33 weeks** who developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation disorders, and high-pitched crying during the first days after birth while exposed during pregnancy and breastfeeding. The report also described infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite, genetic findings affecting metabolism, and symptom improvement after breastfeeding was discontinued. Because prenatal exposure, prematurity, and infant metabolism were also present, the contribution of each factor cannot be separated from this retrieved passage. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- An uncontrolled survey of **930 mothers** reported infant discontinuation-type symptoms in about 10% of infants exposed to antidepressants, but the result was not sertraline-specific and did not establish causation. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20807106/)
- One report described infant sedation when the mother was also taking other medicines; the retrieved evidence does not establish that sertraline alone caused the sedation. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22504099/)

### Lactating-person findings and context

LactMed includes a report from six patients who described decreased milk supply after starting sertraline. It also describes a small study of eight mothers taking serotonin-reuptake inhibitors, including one sertraline user, in which onset of milk secretory activation was delayed compared with 423 mothers who were not taking an inhibitor. These findings are not sertraline-specific and do not establish causation. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The selected DailyMed record states that published lactation data show low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk, reports no adverse reactions in a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs, and states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable in that label. The selected product is **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**; the lookup did not confirm that it matches every sertraline product or formulation. The label’s retrieved adverse-reaction section concerns the medicine-taker label population and is not automatically evidence of infant outcomes. Several expected label sections were not returned, including warnings, boxed warning, and drug-interactions sections. ### Evidence limitations

- The retrieved LactMed Drug Levels and Effects in Breastfed Infants sections are truncated; the record should not be treated as complete. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The returned evidence includes small studies, case reports, observational reports, an uncontrolled survey, and biomarker measurements; a definitive study count was not established in the retrieved record. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Study details vary in infant age, prematurity, breastfeeding extent, prenatal exposure, co-medications, sampling timing, and follow-up.
- A finding that no adverse outcome was observed in a small study does not establish that adverse outcomes cannot occur.
- The LactMed record was revised August 15, 2026, and the retrieved package was published October 22, 2026; the MCP advises checking the linked record for later updates. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
