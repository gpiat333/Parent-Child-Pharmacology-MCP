## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Scope and label context.** The evidence brief identified lamotrigine exactly (RxCUI 28439). It used one selected DailyMed product label—**LAMOTRIGINE KIT, OWP Pharmaceuticals, Inc.**, SPL version 15, effective October 12, 2026—not all formulations. The pregnancy section is truncated in the broader label extract; the returned PLLR pregnancy subsections include the key human-data passages. DailyMed states that its in-use labeling can differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The pregnancy label states that most lamotrigine pregnancy data come from women with **epilepsy**. It also describes pregnancy-associated risks reported with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, including preeclampsia, preterm labor, hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. These passages describe underlying-disease and treatment-context risks; they do not establish that lamotrigine caused each outcome. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label reports that physiologic pregnancy changes may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, including reported decreases during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is label pharmacokinetic context, not an individualized medication plan. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

No maternal lamotrigine adverse-effect outcome was provided in the retrieved pregnancy-specific evidence axes. The selected label’s standalone adult adverse-reaction, warnings, and precautions sections were missing in the extract; this is a retrieval limitation, not evidence that those sections contain no information. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Fetal or infant exposure

No quantitative fetal lamotrigine concentration was retrieved.

A mixed psychotropic-medication study of **60 mothers** measured maternal serum and breastmilk at different time points and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high breastmilk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but it does not provide a lamotrigine-specific numeric concentration in the returned text. The study’s population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children, and its findings covered multiple medications rather than lamotrigine alone. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

A recruiting ClinicalTrials.gov study, **NCT05450978**, is investigating antiseizure-medication clearance and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants in pregnant adult women with epilepsy. It has no posted results; the planned investigation is not evidence that an exposure or outcome has been demonstrated. [S8](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

The DailyMed label reports first-trimester **lamotrigine-monotherapy** registry findings:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 infants**; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that the frequency of major congenital malformations was similar to general-population estimates. The returned extract does not provide a complete account of underlying disease, co-medications, or all comparator details for each registry. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

For isolated oral clefts, the label reports that the NAAED registry observed **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants**, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label also states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**, with 95% CI 0.8–2.63. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It further states that the available data did not suggest increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while differences in outcome definition, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limited the conclusions. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The mixed psychotropic-medication study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. The returned abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific outcome counts, so this finding should not be treated as a lamotrigine-only result. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

The label also reports developmental findings in pregnant animals, including reduced fetal body weight, skeletal variations, neurobehavioral abnormalities, offspring mortality, and decreased fetal folate concentrations. These are animal findings, not human outcome estimates. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
