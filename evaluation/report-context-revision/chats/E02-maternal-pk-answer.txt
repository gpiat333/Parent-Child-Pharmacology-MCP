### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The retrieved DailyMed label reports first-trimester **lamotrigine monotherapy** registry findings:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2%** major congenital malformations among **1,558** exposed infants; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: **2.0%** among **1,562** exposed infants.
- EURAP: **2.9%** among **2,514** exposures; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The source states that these frequencies were similar to general-population estimates, while also noting that outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation. These populations were primarily women with epilepsy and should not automatically be generalized to polytherapy, other indications, later-pregnancy exposure, or an individual pregnancy. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Isolated oral clefts

The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy at **3.2 per 1,000**; 95% CI 1.4–6.3. The label describes this as higher than the unexposed healthy-control comparison. The finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**; 95% CI 0.8–2.63. These results are not consistent across the reported datasets. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Other fetal and child outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age status, and neurodevelopmental delay, but that differences in outcome definition, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit conclusions. The retrieved text does not provide outcome-specific quantitative estimates for these endpoints. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A naturalistic observational study with a mixed medication population reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Because the returned population included multiple psychotropic medications and did not provide a lamotrigine-only estimate, this finding should not be generalized specifically to lamotrigine. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

Animal findings in the label—including developmental toxicity and reduced fetal folate concentrations in rats—are animal findings, not observed human fetal outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
