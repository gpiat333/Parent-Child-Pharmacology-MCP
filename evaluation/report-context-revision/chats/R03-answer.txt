### Findings in breastfed infants

LactMed reports the following for lactating women and their breastfed infants:

- **Measured exposure:** Reported metformin concentrations in milk included **270 mcg/L** averaged over 24 hours, approximately **380 mcg/L** at about 4–5 hours in one mother, **130–270 mcg/L** in another small group, and an average of **410 mcg/L** in samples collected 4–17 days postpartum. These are measured concentrations, not administered doses. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Infant serum exposure:** Infant serum measurements were retrieved. In one group of four infants, two results were below **10 mcg/L** and two were **50 and 80 mcg/L**; in another group, two results were below **5 mcg/L**. Some infant ages and the extent of nursing were not stated. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Observed infant outcomes:**  
  - Seven infants aged 5–25 months were described as healthy, with expected growth and development; two had normal Denver developmental screening results.  
  - Three infants aged 2, 5, and 14 months had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin.  
  - Three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days had no reported low blood-glucose findings, and their mothers reported no adverse reactions.  
  - A prospective, nonrandomized study followed **111 infants**: 61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed. It found no differences at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates between the groups. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

These are reported observations in the stated study populations. They do not establish that the label’s potential concern is impossible, and the retrieved LactMed extract does not establish a definitive study count.
