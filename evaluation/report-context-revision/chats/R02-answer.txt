**Measured exposure in milk (LactMed)**  
- In a report of **five breastfeeding women**, peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L** (range **62–113 mcg/L**), measured about **2.5 hours** after exposure.  
- In another report of **seven women**, peak milk concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** (range **24.6–112.8 mcg/L**) at **2–4 hours**; the average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L** (range **7.7–50.6 mcg/L**).  
- These are measurements of **sumatriptan in milk**, not measurements of infant health. The retrieved record states that relevant published **infant-level** information was not found. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

**Observed infant outcomes**  
- LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence concerning **three women** who used sumatriptan while breastfeeding; **none reported adverse effects in their infants**. The infants’ ages, exposure details, and assessment methods were not specified. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Thus, milk concentrations describe **potential infant exposure**, whereas reported infant outcomes describe **what was observed clinically**. Finding drug in milk does not itself establish an infant adverse effect, and no reported adverse effects in this small, incompletely described group do not establish that adverse effects cannot occur.

LactMed also describes breast or nipple pain and occasional decreased milk production in lactating people; those are **lactation-related maternal observations**, not infant outcomes. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/) The retrieved DailyMed label was a sumatriptan succinate tablet label effective November 10, 2026; its lactation section was missing in the returned extract, so it adds no infant-outcome evidence here.
