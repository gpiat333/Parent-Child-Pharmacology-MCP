# Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Identity:** Sertraline matched exactly to RxNorm concept 36437. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

## Evidence status and scope

Evidence was retrieved, but it is **not a safety verdict**. The search was bounded rather than systematic. LactMed’s **Drug Levels** and **Effects in Breastfed Infants** sections were truncated, so the returned record is incomplete. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) The LactMed resource is lactation-focused; pregnancy findings from the selected label are not substitutes for lactation evidence. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### 1. Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed reports that six patients observed by a midwife reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; doses were not reported. One person reported increased supply after stopping and decreased supply after restarting. This is a temporal report, not proof of causation. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitors—not sertraline alone—reported delayed onset of lactogenesis II. The returned text also reports no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day 4 postpartum. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Some observational studies found lower breastfeeding rates among women using antidepressants or SSRIs. Several did not identify the specific antidepressant, and the returned text notes that underlying depression may contribute to these associations. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed label states that there are **no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production**. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- The same selected label’s adult adverse-reaction section lists hypersensitivity, QTc prolongation/ventricular arrhythmias with pimozide, suicidal thoughts and behaviors, serotonin syndrome, increased bleeding, mania/hypomania activation, discontinuation syndrome, seizures, angle-closure glaucoma, hyponatremia, and sexual dysfunction. However, the returned label did **not** include the expected standalone sections for warnings, warnings and precautions, boxed warning, or drug interactions; their absence is an extraction limitation, not proof that the information does not exist elsewhere. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### 2. Measured infant exposure

These are research measurements, not administered doses or recommendations:

- Breastmilk sertraline: **45 mcg/L** average, range **7–207 mcg/L**, in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- In 26 breastfeeding women, milk sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**); norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- Other returned milk measurements included sertraline **26.4 mcg/L**, **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, foremilk **26 mcg/L**, hindmilk **46 mcg/L**, **<5 mcg/L** in one sample, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, median **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**), and average **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).
- In one preterm-infant case, randomly timed breastmilk averages were **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- A PubMed study of 12 women reported detectable sertraline in three nursing infants and detectable desmethylsertraline in six. Infant serum concentrations were below the detection limit of most commercial laboratories. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

The returned LactMed extract did not provide a complete infant-serum measurement section, and the Drug Levels section was truncated. These measurements do not establish safety or an individual infant’s exposure. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### 3. Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in one 4-month-old infant and spontaneously resolving agitation in the same case description; the returned text does not establish that both findings had identical timing or cause. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a group of 26 infants, no detectable acute adverse reactions were observed. Other small groups also reported no observed adverse reactions, but study sizes and populations were limited. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study of prenatal and/or breastfeeding SSRI exposure reported blunted pain responses compared with controls. Only four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline, and the comparison group did not control adequately for maternal depression, so the finding cannot be attributed specifically to breastfeeding exposure to sertraline. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled survey of antidepressant exposure reported infant discontinuation symptoms in about 10% of infants; the returned evidence was not sertraline-specific. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Case report chronology:** A preterm infant born at 33 weeks developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation disorders, and high-pitched crying during the first 24 hours after birth. The symptoms worsened on day 4 while breastfeeding continued. On day 5, infant serum concentrations were reported in the adult therapeutic range. Breastfeeding was discontinued on day 9; the source then reports that symptoms dissipated and serum levels decreased. The source attributed the episode to sertraline exposure and reduced metabolism, but this is a single case report and does not establish risk for other infants. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
