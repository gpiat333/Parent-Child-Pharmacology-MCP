### Measured fetal exposure

- No measured human fetal lamotrigine concentration or fetal-to-maternal concentration ratio was retrieved.
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** included pregnancy and lactation, measured medication concentrations in maternal serum and breast milk, and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its source population labels included pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child; the abstract reported a high concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine in breast milk. This is lactation exposure evidence, not a measured fetal concentration, and the abstract did not provide a lamotrigine-specific sample size. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- Animal-label text described reduced fetal folate concentrations in rats. This is animal evidence, not measured human fetal exposure. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Gap:** The retrieved material does not establish the magnitude or clinical significance of human fetal lamotrigine exposure.

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The selected label reports first-trimester lamotrigine-monotherapy registry findings in infants:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2% among 1,558 infants**.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: **2.0% among 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: **2.9% among 2,514 exposures**.

The label states that overall frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These are registry findings in predominantly pregnant people with epilepsy and should not automatically be generalized to other indications, combination therapy, or different populations. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts in **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 early-pregnancy exposures**, described in the label as higher than an unexposed healthy-control group. The label also states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries; a European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 with a confidence interval that included no association. These are conflicting findings, not a settled causal conclusion. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT registry record reported no statistically significant increase in oral clefts compared with other defects. The record status was **COMPLETED**, with **no posted results**; the registry record is therefore not equivalent to a full published outcome report. The study population was congenital-anomaly registry data, not all pregnancies. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

#### Other infant outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It also states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This means the evidence gap remains; it does not establish absence of risk. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Because the abstract covered multiple psychotropic medicines and did not provide lamotrigine-specific outcome numbers, this cannot be treated as a definitive lamotrigine-only result. The source population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Maternal pharmacokinetic evidence

#### Clinical Considerations in the label

The selected label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reported decreases during pregnancy and restoration toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is source-label information for clinician discussion, not an individualized dose plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Literature

- A pregnancy/epilepsy review states that physiological changes affect antiseizure-medication levels, but the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific quantitative pharmacokinetic results. Its source population was pregnancy. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A review specifically covering lamotrigine pharmacokinetics in pregnancy was retrieved, but its abstract did not provide quantitative lamotrigine pharmacokinetic results. Its returned source populations were pregnancy and lactation. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)
