## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Bottom line:** Human pregnancy-registry data for **lamotrigine monotherapy**, largely among pregnant people with epilepsy, have not shown an overall increase in major congenital malformations compared with general-population estimates. A possible signal for isolated oral clefts appeared in one registry but was not consistently replicated. This is not a determination of safety for an individual pregnancy or a treatment recommendation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

- The selected DailyMed label states that most lamotrigine pregnancy data come from people with **epilepsy**. It separately describes epilepsy and poorly controlled convulsive seizures as sources of maternal and fetal risk, including obstetric complications and injury. Those disease-related risks should not be attributed automatically to lamotrigine. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The retrieved evidence did **not** provide a complete lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-effect profile. The selected label’s adult adverse-reaction and warnings sections were missing in the returned extract; missing sections do not establish absence of adverse effects.
- A 2025 narrative review addressed **pregnancy and postpartum in people with bipolar disorder** and discussed lamotrigine among several treatments, but the returned abstract did not provide lamotrigine-specific pregnancy outcome estimates. The source population was people with bipolar disorder in the perinatal period, not an epilepsy cohort. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Fetal or infant exposure

- The pregnancy registries measured exposure through reported maternal lamotrigine use, primarily **first-trimester monotherapy**; the returned label did not provide a quantitative fetal concentration.
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children, and measured medication concentrations in maternal serum and breast milk while assessing children during the first 12 months. The abstract mentions lamotrigine in breast milk, but does not establish the lamotrigine-specific subgroup size or a fetal concentration. This is lactation-exposure evidence, not a fetal-exposure measurement. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- The label reports pregnancy-related changes in maternal lamotrigine concentrations. That is maternal pharmacokinetic evidence, not evidence of fetal exposure or an individualized dosing plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

- **Major congenital malformations:** The label reports:
  - International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2%** among **1,558** infants exposed to first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy.
  - North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry: **2.0%** among **1,562** infants.
  - EURAP, an international registry: **2.9%** among **2,514** first-trimester exposures.
  
  The label states that overall frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These are observational registry findings, not randomized comparisons. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- **Isolated oral clefts:** The North American registry reported **3.2 per 1,000** among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy, described in the label as approximately three times the rate in unexposed healthy controls. The label also states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries; a European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**, with a 95% confidence interval of **0.8–2.63**. The signal is therefore inconsistent across sources and does not establish causation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- **Other outcomes:** The label reports that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It states that available data did not suggest increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while noting that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This is a qualified evidence statement, not proof that risk is absent. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- The 2023 naturalistic study abstract reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or first-year development between its in-utero-exposed and nonexposed groups. Because the abstract included multiple psychotropic medicines and did not provide a lamotrigine-specific subgroup size, the result should not be treated as a definitive lamotrigine-specific estimate. The returned source population was mixed: pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Limitations

- The PubMed retrieval was a bounded relevance search: **690 records** matched the query, but only a small subset and abstracts were returned; it was not a systematic review. - The selected DailyMed record is one formulation-specific label, with an effective date of **October 12, 2026**; it is not all lamotrigine labeling. Its broader pregnancy extract was truncated, although structured pregnancy fields were returned. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- Registry estimates mainly concern **monotherapy and first-trimester exposure** and may not generalize to polytherapy, later gestation, different underlying conditions, or different formulations.
