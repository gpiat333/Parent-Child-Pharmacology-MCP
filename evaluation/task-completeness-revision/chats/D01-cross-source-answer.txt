## Sertraline during breastfeeding

Sertraline identity was confirmed as the single ingredient sertraline. [S9](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Measured milk and infant exposure

**LactMed reports:**

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers, average breast-milk sertraline concentration was **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**).
- In a 24-hour sampling study of 26 breastfeeding women, average milk concentrations were **129 mcg/L** for sertraline (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and **258 mcg/L** for norsertraline (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- In a separate study of four mothers, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- LactMed states that sertraline is usually not detected in infant serum, while low levels of norsertraline are often detectable.
- The product label reports a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which exclusively breastfed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to **2% of maternal serum levels**, with a range of **0–15%**. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

These are measured research findings, not dosing instructions. The retrieved LactMed **Drug Levels** section was truncated, so additional exposure information may be missing. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports:

- No detectable acute adverse reactions in a study of 26 infants.
- No adverse effects observed in several small groups of infants, including a group with normal six-month weight gain.
- One 4-month-old infant with benign sleep myoclonus and self-resolving agitation.
- One preterm infant with hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying. The report also described elevated infant serum concentrations and genetically intermediate metabolism of relevant enzymes; the authors attributed the reaction probably to sertraline. This was a single case involving a preterm infant, so it should not be generalized to all breastfed infants.
- A small mixed-SSRI study reported blunted pain responses in exposed infants; only four infants were exposed to sertraline, and the study could not separate medication exposure from effects related to maternal depression.
- An uncontrolled survey of antidepressant exposure reported discontinuation-type symptoms in some infants, but it did not provide sertraline-specific findings.

The retrieved LactMed **Effects in Breastfed Infants** section was truncated; therefore, this is not a complete record. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
