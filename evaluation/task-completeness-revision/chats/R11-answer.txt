**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (2022), PMID 34131926. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

- **Study design:** A study of breast-milk excretion and its relationship to maternal oral dose and serum concentrations. Participants completed a questionnaire; researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples from before dosing through 12 hours afterward and in one pre-dose maternal serum sample. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
- **Population:** The returned metadata lists the source population as **lactation and infant**. The study included 20 breastfeeding women with epilepsy and 21 infants; one woman had twins, 16 infants were fully breastfed, and five were partially breastfed. Infant ages, gestational ages, and prematurity status were not provided in the abstract. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
- **Measured drug exposure:** The mean trough breast-milk/serum ratio was 0.98 ± 0.20. The abstract reported higher estimated infant exposure and relative infant exposure among fully breastfed infants than partially breastfed infants. Maternal dose, maternal serum and breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk AUC values were substantially correlated. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
- **Observed infant outcomes:** Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding. All infants gained weight according to age. The authors described the somnolence as potentially related to breast-milk levetiracetam exposure, but this was an interpretation of an observed association, not proof of causation. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
