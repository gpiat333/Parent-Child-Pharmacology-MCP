## Sertraline during lactation: balanced research summary

Sertraline was exactly identified as the ingredient sertraline. The main lactation source is LactMed. Its returned extract is **truncated** in the “Drug Levels” and “Effects in Breastfed Infants” sections, and the number of underlying studies is **not established from this extract**. Therefore, this is not a complete evidence record. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure: milk and infant serum

- LactMed describes generally low sertraline concentrations in human milk. Infant serum sertraline is often undetectable, while the metabolite norsertraline is more often detectable at low levels. These are **exposure measurements, not clinical outcomes**. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Reported studies included small groups such as 15 mothers in a pooled analysis and 26 mothers in an intensive milk-sampling study. Milk concentrations varied substantially across participants and with sampling factors such as foremilk versus hindmilk and timing of collection. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The DailyMed label reports a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which exclusively breastfed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to 2% of maternal levels, with a reported range of 0%–15%; no adverse reactions were observed in that analysis. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Observed infant outcomes

**No adverse outcome observed in particular studies**

- In a study of **26 infants**, no detectable acute adverse reactions were reported.
- In a study involving **14 mother–infant pairs**, no breastmilk-related adverse effects were reported; six infants were exclusively breastfed and younger than three months.
- In another group of **25 infants**, reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects.
- Smaller groups of six and seven infants also had no clinically noted adverse reactions during the reported study periods. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These findings are limited by small sample sizes, short or variable follow-up, observational designs, and—in some studies—the absence of an unexposed comparison group. The evidence does not establish that rare or long-term effects cannot occur.

**Reported adverse or potentially concerning findings**

- **One 4-month-old infant:** benign sleep myoclonus and agitation that resolved spontaneously were reported. The source does not establish that sertraline caused these events. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **One preterm infant:** the mother had sertraline exposure during pregnancy and postpartum while exclusively breastfeeding. The infant, born at 33 weeks, developed hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying during the first days of life. Sertraline and norsertraline were measured in the infant’s serum. The report also identified genetically intermediate metabolism of relevant enzymes. Breastfeeding was stopped on day 9, after which symptoms dissipated and serum concentrations decreased. This is a single case report involving prematurity, prenatal exposure, and unusual metabolism; it cannot estimate frequency or be generalized to full-term infants. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Sedation report:** one mother reported sedation in her breastfed infant while also taking sertraline and other medicines, including sedating agents. The contribution of sertraline alone is therefore uncertain. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Pain-response study:** infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy, with or without breastfeeding exposure, had blunted pain responses compared with infants of nondepressed, unexposed mothers; only four infants in the exposed group had sertraline exposure. Because there was no depressed, untreated comparison group, the effect of maternal illness or caregiving could not be separated from medication exposure. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled online survey of 930 mothers taking antidepressants while nursing reported infant “discontinuation” symptoms in about 10% overall. This was not sertraline-specific and relied on parent reports, so it cannot establish a sertraline-specific rate or causal relationship. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Effects on the lactating person and milk production

These findings should not be confused with drug concentrations in milk:

- LactMed reports a small observational account of six mothers who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; this does not establish causation.
- A small mixed-SSRI study included only one sertraline-treated mother. It reported delayed onset of milk secretory activation in the broader SSRI group, but the study was too limited to determine a sertraline-specific effect.
- Broader observational studies found lower breastfeeding rates among some antidepressant-exposed groups, but the source notes that underlying psychiatric illness may contribute. These studies did not consistently identify sertraline separately. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The retrieved DailyMed lactation section states that data on sertraline’s effects on milk production are unavailable. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Relevant source context and limitations

- LactMed’s summary says that authoritative reviewers consider sertraline a preferred antidepressant during breastfeeding; this is a source characterization, not a personalized treatment recommendation. It also notes that rare accumulation may be possible in preterm infants with impaired metabolic activity. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The retrieved DailyMed label is for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective November 5, 2026. The tool did not confirm that this is the exact product or formulation in use. The lactation section was retrieved, but the label extract was missing the warnings, warnings-and-precautions, boxed-warning, and drug-interaction sections; missing sections are not evidence that no such information exists. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- Adult label adverse reactions apply to the person taking the medicine and are not automatically infant outcomes. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
