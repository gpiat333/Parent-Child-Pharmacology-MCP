## Pregnancy evidence brief: lamotrigine

**Identity:** The lookup matched the ingredient **lamotrigine** exactly. [S26](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)

### Overall evidence

Human pregnancy-registry data summarized in the selected DailyMed label did not identify an increased overall frequency of major congenital malformations with first-trimester **lamotrigine monotherapy**:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2%** among 1,558 exposed infants  
- North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry: **2.0%** among 1,562 exposed infants  
- EURAP: **2.9%** among 2,514 exposures  

The label reports that these frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. The source context is primarily pregnancies involving people with epilepsy. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A signal for isolated oral clefts was reported in one NAAED analysis: **3.2 per 1,000** among 2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy, described in that analysis as approximately three times the rate in unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries; a European case-control analysis also did not show a statistically clear association. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

These findings are population-level observations, not an individualized risk estimate.

This is source-label information, not an individualized dosing plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### 2. Fetal exposure

The pregnancy evidence retrieved through registries reported pregnancy exposure and infant outcomes, but did not provide fetal concentration measurements.

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** examined serum and breast-milk concentrations during pregnancy and lactation and followed exposed children through the first 12 months. Its population field includes **pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child**; the abstract does not establish a lamotrigine-specific fetal concentration result. It reported a high lamotrigine concentration-by-dose ratio in breast milk, which is lactation evidence and should not be treated as fetal-exposure evidence. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### 3. Observed fetal or infant outcomes
