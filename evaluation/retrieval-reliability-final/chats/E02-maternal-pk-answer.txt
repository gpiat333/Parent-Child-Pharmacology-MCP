### Fetal exposure

No verified numeric fetal or cord-blood lamotrigine concentrations were retrieved.

The label states that pregnancy-related physiological changes may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reported decreases during pregnancy and return toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is maternal pharmacokinetic evidence, not a direct measurement of fetal exposure. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** measured medication concentrations in maternal serum and breast milk and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high breast-milk concentration-to-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but this is lactation evidence—not fetal exposure evidence—and no lamotrigine-specific numeric pregnancy serum values were provided in the retrieved abstract. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Fetal and infant outcomes

The selected label summarizes pregnancy-registry data, primarily from people with epilepsy and lamotrigine monotherapy:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 infants** exposed during the first trimester.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 infants** exposed during the first trimester.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 first-trimester exposures**.
- The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts at **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 early-pregnancy exposures** versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries. A European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45 (95% CI 0.8–2.63)**. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It notes that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation; no individualized risk estimate can be derived from this extract. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT record reports no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with other defects after in-utero lamotrigine exposure. This is a study-record summary, not a substitute for the full publication. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

The abstract of the 2023 naturalistic study reports no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Because the study included multiple psychotropic medicines, the retrieved abstract does not establish that these findings were specific to lamotrigine. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
