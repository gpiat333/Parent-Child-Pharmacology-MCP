## Sertraline evidence

### Measured milk exposure

LactMed reports these research measurements in human milk:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L** average; range **7–207 mcg/L**.
- In 24-hour sampling: sertraline **129 mcg/L** average; range **11–938 mcg/L**. Norsertraline: **258 mcg/L** average; range **20–1,498 mcg/L**.
- At randomly timed samples 2 months postpartum: sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L**.
- In a case involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L sertraline** and **358 mcg/L norsertraline** at unspecified sampling times.
- Foremilk and hindmilk sampled 13 hours after administration contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively.
- Early-postpartum trough samples: sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one woman and **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight others.
- Randomly timed samples: median sertraline concentration **11.3 mcg/L**, range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**.
- Another study reported an average milk concentration of **87 mcg/L**, range **12–288 mcg/L**.

These are milk concentrations, not administered amounts or individualized dosing guidance. The retrieved LactMed extract did not include a complete infant-level concentration section; it states that sertraline is usually not detected in infant serum and that norsertraline is often detectable at low levels in infant serum. ### Observed infant outcomes

Reported LactMed observations include:

- One 4-month-old infant had benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation.
- In one group of 26 infants, no acute adverse reactions were detected.
- In 14 mother–infant pairs, infant whole-blood serotonin measurements were similar before and after maternal treatment; no adverse effects were reported in those infants.
- In a follow-up group of 25 infants, reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal effects.
- No clinical adverse reactions were noted in six infants aged 5–34 weeks in one study, and no adverse effects were reported in seven 4-week-old infants in another.
- One report described sedation in an infant whose mother was also taking other medicines; the report does not establish that sertraline alone caused it.
- A separate case involved a **preterm infant** with elevated infant concentrations and symptoms including hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying. The report attributed the reaction to sertraline in the context of prematurity and reduced metabolism. This single case should not be generalized to all infants.

The “Effects in Breastfed Infants” section was truncated, so additional findings may be missing. Milk concentration is measured exposure, not an infant clinical outcome. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
