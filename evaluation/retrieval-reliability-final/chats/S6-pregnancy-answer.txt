### Maternal effects

**Direct maternal lamotrigine adverse outcomes:** No specific maternal clinical outcome attributable to lamotrigine was retrieved in the evidence brief. The maternal-effects row is therefore **limited/missing in the retrieved extract**, not evidence that no such effects exist.

The label emphasizes an important underlying-disease context: pregnancy in people with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, has been associated with preeclampsia, preterm labor, hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. The label identifies untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures as a relevant risk context. These are disease-associated risks described in the label, not evidence that lamotrigine caused those outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

Most pregnancy exposure data in the label come from people with **epilepsy**. Evidence for bipolar disorder is represented by a separate narrative review of women with bipolar disorder in pregnancy and postpartum; its abstract discusses relapse and perinatal medication management broadly, not a lamotrigine-specific comparative outcome estimate. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Maternal pharmacokinetic context

The label reports pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations, including reported decreases during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is label context for clinician discussion, not an individualized dosing instruction. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A retrieved naturalistic study included **60 mothers** with pregnancy/lactation exposure to multiple psychotropic medicines and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its abstract reported a high milk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but did not provide a lamotrigine-specific numeric concentration in the retrieved text. Because multiple medicines were studied, this result should not be treated as a lamotrigine-only estimate. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Fetal or infant exposure

No fetal lamotrigine concentration was retrieved.

The mixed-medication study measured maternal serum and breast milk at multiple time points; its abstract provides the qualitative milk concentration-by-dose finding above, but no verified lamotrigine-specific numeric concentration. Breast-milk transfer is not the same as fetal exposure. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

Animal findings in the label—including developmental toxicity and reduced fetal folate concentrations in rats—should remain separate from human pregnancy observations. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

For **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy exposures**, the selected label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 infants**.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**.

The label reports that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These are registry findings in the source populations described above, predominantly people with epilepsy; they should not be generalized to every indication, formulation, exposure pattern, or pregnancy context. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The NAAED registry also reported isolated oral clefts in **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 early-pregnancy exposures**, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries. It also reports a European case-control estimate with an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45 (95% CI 0.8–2.63)**. Thus, the retrieved sources show an inconsistent oral-cleft signal rather than a single settled estimate. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT registry record states in its study summary that it found no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with other defects, but the record reports **no posted results**. The registry summary is therefore not equivalent to a full published outcome report. Its returned source population is a European congenital-anomaly-registry case-control study of pregnancy exposure, with the study’s comparison restricted to malformed infants rather than all births. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

The label also discusses fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It states that available meta-analyses did not suggest increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while noting differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups. This limitation does not establish that those outcomes were absent. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Its source population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children and involved multiple psychotropic medicines; the abstract does not establish a lamotrigine-specific sample size or estimate. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
