### What the adult medicine-taker label says

- **Warnings:** The label describes lactic acidosis as a rare but serious complication associated with metformin accumulation. It discusses increased risk in settings including renal dysfunction, hypoperfusion or hypoxemia, unstable or acute heart failure, older age, hepatic disease, dehydration, sepsis, and excessive alcohol intake. The retrieved warnings text is truncated. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- **Adverse reactions:** In a placebo-controlled study of adults with type 2 diabetes, the reported reactions more common with metformin included diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, asthenia, indigestion, abdominal discomfort, headache, and other listed reactions. This is adult study evidence, not evidence of infant effects. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- **Lactation label section:** The label states that animal studies found metformin in milk and that comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers. It raises a potential risk of hypoglycemia in nursing infants. That is a label precaution, not an observed report that an infant developed hypoglycemia. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### What LactMed reports in breastfed infants

- **Measured exposure:** Reported metformin concentrations in milk included averages of **270 mcg/L**, approximately **380 mcg/L** at one sampling time, **130–270 mcg/L** in another group, and an average of **410 mcg/L** in women sampled postpartum. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Infant-level measurements:** The returned LactMed text reports **infant serum** measurements: metformin was undetectable at **<10 mcg/L** in two infants, while levels of **50 and 80 mcg/L** were reported in two others. In another report, infant serum levels were undetectable at **<5 mcg/L** in two infants. Ages, extent of nursing, and some sampling details were not stated. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Observed infant outcomes:** LactMed reports that seven infants aged 5–25 months were judged healthy with expected growth and development; three infants had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin; and three recently postpartum breastfed infants had no low blood-glucose levels reported. It also describes a 6-month, nonrandomized study of 111 infants—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—in which no differences were found in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates at the reported follow-up points. These are findings from the specified study populations, not a conclusion about all breastfed infants. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
