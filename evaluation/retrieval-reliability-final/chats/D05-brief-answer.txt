# Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Evidence status:** Sertraline identity was matched exactly in the lookup. The retrieved evidence is partial and is **not a safety verdict**. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

## Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed reports an observation of decreased milk supply in six patients after starting sertraline; dosing and other clinical details were not reported.
- A small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor exposure—only one participant took sertraline—reported delayed onset of lactogenesis II compared with controls. The extract also reported no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after postpartum day 4.
- Several observational findings concerned antidepressants or SSRIs as groups rather than sertraline specifically; they should not be treated as sertraline-specific effects.
- The selected DailyMed label states that published data show low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk and that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. The selected product was **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**; effective date: **2026-11-05**. This is one product label, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32) [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

## Measured exposure

The retrieved LactMed extract contains the following **research measurements in milk**:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L**, range **7–207 mcg/L**.
- In one 26-woman study, sertraline: **129 mcg/L**, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline: **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1,498 mcg/L**.
- In another study, sertraline: **26.4 mcg/L**; norsertraline: **29 mcg/L**.
- In a case involving a preterm infant, breastmilk sertraline and norsertraline averaged **201 mcg/L** and **358 mcg/L**, respectively, at random sampling times.
- Foremilk and hindmilk concentrations collected at a specified sampling time were **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively.
- One sample was below quantification at **<5 mcg/L**; another group had milk levels of **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**.
- Another study reported median milk sertraline **11.3 mcg/L**, range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**; the source notes that sampling timing might affect accuracy.
- Another study reported average milk sertraline **87 mcg/L**, range **12–288 mcg/L**.

The returned LactMed drug-level excerpt did **not** include a complete infant-level concentration section. A PubMed abstract describing 12 women and 11 mother–infant pairs reported detectable sertraline in three infants and detectable desmethylsertraline in six; it described the detected infant serum concentrations as below the detection limit of most commercial laboratories. This small study does not establish an individual infant’s exposure or outcome. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

## Observed infant outcomes

- One report described benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in a 4-month-old infant and agitation that resolved spontaneously.
- In a study of **26 infants**, no detectable acute adverse reactions were observed.
- Other small reports described no clinically noted adverse reactions, no adverse reactions requiring medical attention, or normal six-month weight gain.
- A separate case report involved a **preterm infant born at 33 weeks’ gestation** with hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying. The source described high infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite and attributed the episode to sertraline in the context of prematurity and reduced metabolism. This is a single case report; it does not establish how broadly the finding applies. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study involving prenatal and/or breastfeeding SSRI exposure reported blunted infant pain responses compared with an unexposed control group. Only four infants were exposed to sertraline, and there was no control group of depressed, nonmedicated mothers, so the specific contribution of sertraline exposure through breastfeeding was not established. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The LactMed infant-outcomes section was truncated; additional outcome details may be missing.

- How closely do these study populations match my infant’s age, prematurity status, feeding pattern, and medical history?
- Which findings are measured milk or infant exposure, and which are observed clinical outcomes?
- Does the evidence about milk supply apply to my postpartum timing and lactation status?
- What follow-up, if any, is appropriate for my infant’s specific circumstances?
- Could other medicines, supplements, or sedating agents affect interpretation of any infant symptoms?
