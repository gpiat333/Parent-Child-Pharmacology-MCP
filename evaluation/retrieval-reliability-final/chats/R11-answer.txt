**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* — PubMed PMID 34131926 [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design
A journal article reporting an observational study of breastfeeding women with epilepsy. Participants completed a questionnaire; researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples from before administration through 12 hours afterward and in a single pre-administration maternal serum sample. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Population
The study included **20 breastfeeding women and 21 infants**—16 fully breastfed and 5 partially breastfed infants; one woman had twins. The retrieved abstract does not provide infant ages or prematurity status. The source population is labeled **lactation and infant**. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Drug exposure
The study measured levetiracetam in breast milk and maternal serum. The reported trough breast-milk/maternal-serum ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**. The abstract also reports relative infant exposure estimates of **13.8 ± 3.1%** among fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** among partially breastfed infants. It reports correlations among levetiracetam exposure measures, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk concentrations, and breast-milk area-under-the-curve values. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Observed infant outcomes
Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding. This temporal association does not establish causation. The abstract also states that all infants gained weight according to age. No other infant clinical outcomes were reported in the retrieved abstract. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
