LactMed reports the following for breastfed infants exposed to **amoxicillin**:

**Observed symptoms**
- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, **3 infants had diarrhea**; no rash or candidiasis was reported.
- In a prospective study of 40 infants exposed through breast milk, **2 had diarrhea and 1 had a rash**.
- One comparison study reported reactions in **8.3%** of amoxicillin-exposed infants, including **rash and sleepiness**; this was similar to the rate in infants exposed to a macrolide.
- One case involved **recurrent generalized hives** after breastfeeding. The mother had received **amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin**. The report attributed the reaction probably to antibiotics in breast milk, but could not determine which drug was responsible; the combination product was considered most likely.
- Another report described **mild diarrhea** in an 11-month-old whose mother was taking amoxicillin plus acetaminophen and codeine.
- In a study of 25 mothers and infants older than 4 weeks, **no infant adverse effects were reported**.

**Advice to monitor—not observed symptoms**
A controlled study asked mothers to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were monitoring targets, not reports that the symptoms occurred. No statistical differences were found between the groups, although the comparison involved related antibiotics—ampicillin or ampicillin/clavulanate—rather than amoxicillin alone.

**Uncertainty**
LactMed states that rash, diarrhea, and thrush have been reported but **have not been adequately evaluated**. Some reports involved other medicines or combination products, so causation cannot always be assigned to amoxicillin. Infant blood-level information was not found in the record. The extract’s study count is **missing in the exported data**; its reference count is not a study count. Thus, the record describes mostly limited observational evidence and does not establish either that symptoms are caused by amoxicillin or that they cannot occur.
