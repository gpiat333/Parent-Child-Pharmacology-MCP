**Milk exposure and infant observations are different kinds of evidence.**

- **Measured in milk:** In breastfeeding women, sumatriptan concentrations were measured in breastmilk over time. One group had an average peak milk concentration of **87.2 micrograms/L**; another had an average peak of **59.3 micrograms/L**. Researchers used these milk concentrations to **estimate potential infant intake**. No published measurements of sumatriptan in infant blood were found in the retrieved LactMed record. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
- **Actually observed in infants:** A manufacturer report, relayed through an author, described **3 women** who used sumatriptan while breastfeeding; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The doses, routes, infant details, and observation methods were unspecified. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
- **Interpretation:** Milk concentrations are measurements of **drug exposure through milk**; the report about 3 infants is an **observational outcome report**. Neither should be treated as the other: detecting drug in milk does not demonstrate an infant effect, and no reported infant effects does not prove absence of exposure or establish safety. The LactMed extract does not establish the total study count, so the evidence should not be regarded as comprehensively studied. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Reports of breast or nipple pain and occasional reduced milk production concerned the **lactating person**, not observed infant outcomes. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
