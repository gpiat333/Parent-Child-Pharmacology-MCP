## Pregnancy evidence brief: lamotrigine

**Identity:** The retrieved record matched lamotrigine exactly as an ingredient. Evidence for combination products or interactions was not established. [S26](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)

**Coverage:** This is a bounded label-and-literature review, not a systematic review. The selected DailyMed record is one lamotrigine product label, not all formulations. Its general pregnancy section was truncated, although structured pregnancy fields were returned. The label’s effective date was October 12, 2026; DailyMed notes that in-use labeling may differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### 1. Effects on the pregnant person

- The label describes pregnancy risks associated with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, including hypertensive complications, preterm labor, bleeding, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. These are **disease/seizure-context findings**, not evidence that lamotrigine caused them. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The retrieved pregnancy-specific maternal-adverse-reaction subsection was missing in the exported data. Therefore, no pregnancy-specific maternal adverse-effect estimate was established from this lookup.
- A 2024 AAN/AES/SMFM practice guideline—covering people with epilepsy of childbearing potential and neonatal outcomes—frames seizure control and fetal outcomes as competing considerations. It is a guideline based on a systematic review, not a lamotrigine-specific new outcome study. [S24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)

### 2. Maternal pharmacokinetics

**Clinical Considerations:** The selected label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is source information for clinician discussion, not an individualized treatment plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Literature:** The retrieved pharmacokinetic PubMed search returned 109 records; the MCP retrieved three abstracts. The most relevant abstract described a naturalistic study of **60 mothers** with serum and breastmilk measurements at multiple time points and child follow-up through 12 months. Its abstract did not establish a lamotrigine-specific maternal pregnancy sample size or detailed lamotrigine-specific clearance or exposure estimates. [S12](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29+AND+%28%22Pharmacokinetics%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Drug+Elimination+Routes%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pharmacokinetic%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+clearance%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+%22area+under+the+curve%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+%22dose+adjustment%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=3&sort=relevance&retmode=json) [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

A pregnancy-and-epilepsy review states that physiological changes affect antiseizure-medication levels and discusses serum-concentration monitoring, but the retrieved abstract did not provide a primary lamotrigine-specific PK estimate. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)

**PK gap:** Detailed human lamotrigine pregnancy PK—such as trimester-specific clearance or exposure changes—was not established in the retrieved primary-study abstracts. A recruiting observational study is examining antiseizure-medication clearance and exposure in pregnant adults with epilepsy, including lamotrigine, but its record has no posted results. **Status: RECRUITING; results not posted.** [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### 3. Fetal exposure

- No measured human fetal lamotrigine concentration was reported in the retrieved label or abstracts.
- The recruiting study above is designed to examine exposure to unborn children, but registration and recruitment are not outcome findings. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)
- Animal label findings involving reduced fetal folate concentrations in rats are **animal data**, not measured human fetal exposure. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

Thus, measured human fetal exposure remains a gap in this retrieval.

### 4. Fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The label reports first-trimester **lamotrigine-monotherapy** registry findings:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: 2.2% among 1,558 exposed infants.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: 2.0% among 1,562 exposed infants.
- EURAP, an international registry: 2.9% among 2,514 exposures.

The label states that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates, but the populations, comparators, exposure definitions, and monotherapy status differ. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts in 3.2 per 1,000 exposed infants, described in the label as higher than in unexposed healthy controls. The same label states that this signal was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries, and that a European case-control study did not show a statistically clear association. This remains inconsistent evidence, not a settled causal conclusion. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT registry record states that its study found no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with other defects, but the record has no posted results. **Status: COMPLETED; results not posted.** [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

#### Other outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy but says that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This is an evidence limitation, not proof that these outcomes cannot occur. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The retrieved naturalistic study abstract reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or development during the first 12 months between exposed and nonexposed newborns. However, the returned abstract included multiple psychotropic medicines and did not establish a lamotrigine-specific sample size, so this finding should not be generalized as a lamotrigine-only result. **Source population labels:** pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child; the exact lamotrigine subgroup was missing in the returned metadata. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### 5. Important gaps

- No detailed lamotrigine-specific maternal PK estimates were retrieved from a primary pregnancy study.
- No measured human fetal lamotrigine concentrations were retrieved.
- Maternal pregnancy-specific adverse-event data were missing in the exported label fields.
- Evidence on isolated oral clefts is inconsistent across registries and study designs.
- Longer-term neurodevelopmental evidence remains limited and outcome definitions vary.
- The PubMed search was bounded and not systematic; absence from the retrieved subset does not show absence of evidence elsewhere.
- Evidence largely concerns pregnancy exposures among people with epilepsy; applicability to bipolar disorder or other indications may differ. The returned source population for the bipolar-disorder review was pregnancy, but it did not provide lamotrigine-specific outcome estimates. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

- Which findings apply to the exact lamotrigine formulation and any other active ingredients?
- Does my indication and underlying condition match the populations in these studies?
- Which monitoring information, if any, is relevant to pregnancy-related concentration changes and the postpartum period?
- How should the uncertainty around the oral-cleft signal be interpreted alongside the larger registry findings?
- What fetal or pregnancy follow-up is appropriate for my gestational stage?
- Which evidence applies to monotherapy versus use with other medicines?
- What is the plan for continuity of care during pregnancy and after delivery?
