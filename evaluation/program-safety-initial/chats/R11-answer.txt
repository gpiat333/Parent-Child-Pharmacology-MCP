### Paper: *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

- **Study type:** Observational pharmacokinetic study with questionnaires.
- **Source population:** Lactation and infants; 20 breastfeeding women with epilepsy and 21 infants, including 16 fully and 5 partially breastfed infants. Infant ages and prematurity status are not provided in the abstract.
- **Measured drug exposure:** Levetiracetam was measured in serial breast-milk samples and a maternal serum sample. The mean trough breast-milk/maternal-serum ratio was 0.98 ± 0.20. The abstract reports correlations between maternal treatment amount, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk concentrations, and breast-milk exposure over time.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** Three women reported somnolence in fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that it resolved shortly after partial breastfeeding. All infants gained weight according to age. The authors stated that further studies were needed to assess short- and long-term safety; this is a study interpretation, not proof of causation or a safety conclusion.

A related paper was also retrieved:

### *Levetiracetam concentrations in serum and in breast milk at birth and during lactation* (PMID 15857447) [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15857447/)

- **Study type:** Observational pharmacokinetic study.
- **Source population:** Pregnancy, lactation, and neonates; eight breastfeeding women with epilepsy and their infants.
- **Measured drug exposure:** Levetiracetam was measured in maternal serum, umbilical-cord serum, breast milk, and infant serum. Breastfed infants had very low serum levetiracetam concentrations during continued breastfeeding.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** The abstract reports no detected malformations and no signs of adverse effects in the infants studied.

These summaries are limited to the retrieved PubMed abstracts; full texts were not retrieved. The search itself returned 95 records, so these two papers should not be treated as an exhaustive review.
