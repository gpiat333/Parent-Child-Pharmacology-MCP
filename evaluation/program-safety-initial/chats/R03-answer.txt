## Comparison

### DailyMed label: adult taking metformin

The selected label is **METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]**, one product label rather than all metformin formulations.

- The label’s warning describes **lactic acidosis** as a rare, serious complication associated with metformin accumulation and identifies higher-risk clinical contexts such as impaired kidney function, reduced tissue oxygenation or perfusion, heart failure with hypoxemia or hypoperfusion, liver disease, dehydration, sepsis, older age, and excessive alcohol intake.
- In the label’s adult type 2 diabetes trial, reported adverse reactions included gastrointestinal effects such as diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, indigestion, and abdominal discomfort, as well as asthenia and headache. Other reported reactions included hypoglycemia, myalgia, lightheadedness, dyspnea, rash, and palpitations.
- These findings concern the **medicine-taker label population**. They are not findings in breastfed infants.

### LactMed: breastfed-infant exposure and outcomes

LactMed is a **lactation-only** source.

- **Measured exposure:** Metformin levels in milk were generally low. Low infant-serum concentrations were sometimes detected, while other measured infant samples had undetectable levels.
- **Observed outcomes, observational reports:** Seven infants aged **5–25 months** were described as healthy, with expected growth and development; two had normal developmental screening. Three infants aged **2, 5, and 14 months** had no detected adverse effects attributed to metformin.
- **Observed outcomes, report involving newborns:** Three breastfed infants aged **10–11 days postpartum** had no low-blood-glucose findings, and their mothers reported no infant adverse reactions.
- **Prospective, nonrandomized study:** The returned LactMed narrative describes **111 infants**—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—followed for 6 months. It reports no observed differences in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates between the groups. This mixed population should not be generalized to all breastfed infants.

The LactMed extract does not establish a study count; its reference count is not a study count. Several infant ages, extent of nursing, and exposure details were missing in the returned narrative. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### What can and cannot be compared

- The DailyMed warning concerns risks to the **adult medicine taker**; LactMed reports measured infant exposure and observed infant outcomes.
- The adult label’s gastrointestinal adverse reactions and lactic-acidosis warning should not be relabeled as infant outcomes.
- LactMed’s reported infant findings do not assess the adult’s lactic-acidosis risk.
- LactMed also includes a source recommendation to use caution for newborn or premature infants and infants with renal impairment. That is a recommendation in the source summary, not evidence that those infants experienced an adverse outcome. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### Missing or incomplete sections

- In the adult-label extraction, the **warnings** section was truncated, so the complete warning text was not retrieved.
- That extraction listed **“warnings_and_precautions”** and **“boxed_warning”** as missing. Missing sections do not establish safety or prove that no relevant information exists elsewhere.
- The separate DailyMed pregnancy/lactation extraction found pregnancy, lactation, and pediatric-use sections, but the PLLR-formatted pregnancy subsections—**Risk Summary, Clinical Considerations, Data, and pregnancy-exposure registry**—were missing in the exported data or not parsed. This is a retrieval/format limitation, not evidence of safety. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- The selected label is not confirmed to match a particular patient’s exact formulation. The MCP lookup identifies metformin by ingredient, but the product label is only one matching formulation. [S10](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/6809/properties.json)
