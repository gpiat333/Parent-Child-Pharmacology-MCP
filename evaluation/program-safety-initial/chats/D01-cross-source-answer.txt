## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured exposure

**LactMed** reports low sertraline concentrations in breast milk. Across observational studies and case reports involving lactating mothers and breastfed infants, reported relative infant exposure was generally low, with estimates varying by sampling method and breastfeeding extent. Norsertraline, an active metabolite, was sometimes detectable in infant serum even when sertraline was not. In the returned evidence, study counts were not established; cited references are not equivalent to study counts. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

In one observational study of **30 breastfed infants aged 6–13 weeks**, 22 had undetectable infant-serum sertraline and 8 had detectable levels. In another observational study of **22 breastfed infants aged 4–28 weeks**, sertraline was detectable in 4 infants and norsertraline in 11. These are exposure measurements, not clinical outcomes. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

A case report involved a **33-week preterm breastfed infant** with measured sertraline and norsertraline concentrations and symptoms described as resembling neonatal abstinence. The report also described genetically intermediate metabolism. The returned extract does not establish causation. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- An observational study of 26 breastfed infants reported no detectable acute adverse reactions during the study period. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other small observational groups reported no clinically observed adverse reactions and no abnormal reported effects during follow-up. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One case report described benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation in a breastfed infant; causation was not established. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The LactMed summary states that no adverse developmental effects were found in infants followed up to 5 years, but the returned “Effects in Breastfed Infants” section was truncated. The “Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk” section was omitted by the retrieval limit, so this extract is not complete. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The study populations were mixed—observational groups, case reports, and a meta-analysis—and should not be generalized beyond their reported lactation populations. Details such as study size, infant age, prematurity, and breastfeeding extent were missing in some reports. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Product-label evidence

The selected label was **“SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]”**, DailyMed set ID **687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32**, with an effective date of November 5, 2026. The product match was not confirmed for a specific product in hand. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The retrieved label’s adverse-reactions section concerns the **person taking sertraline**, not a breastfed infant. It lists hypersensitivity reactions, serotonin syndrome, bleeding risk, activation of mania/hypomania, discontinuation syndrome, seizures, angle-closure glaucoma, hyponatremia, and sexual dysfunction. These label findings should not be presented as observed infant outcomes. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The retrieved label extract did not include the expected warnings, boxed-warning, warnings-and-precautions, or drug-interactions sections. Their absence from this extract is a retrieval limitation, not evidence that those topics are absent from the full label. The label also included pregnancy-related context about relapse of major depression after discontinuation; that is not a breastfeeding-infant outcome. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Questions to ask a clinician or pharmacist

- Does the selected label match the exact manufacturer, formulation, and product?
- How closely do the LactMed populations match the infant’s age, gestational age, prematurity status, and extent of breastfeeding?
- Which findings are measured exposure, and which are observed clinical outcomes?
- How should the preterm-infant case be interpreted in the context of the infant’s metabolic status?
- What benefits, uncertainties, and follow-up considerations should be discussed?
- Which infant findings would warrant professional assessment?
- Are other medicines or supplements relevant to interpreting infant exposure or symptoms?

Stopping or changing a medication can also carry risks; discuss any change with the prescribing clinician.
