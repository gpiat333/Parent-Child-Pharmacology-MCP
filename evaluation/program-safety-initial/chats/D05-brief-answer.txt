## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Identity:** Sertraline was matched exactly in the lookup. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Summary

Retrieved evidence describes generally low measured transfer into breast milk and often-undetectable infant serum sertraline, with norsertraline sometimes detectable. Small studies and case reports reported mostly no acute adverse effects, but one preterm-infant case included symptoms and measurable infant concentrations. These findings are not a personalized safety determination and do not establish that outcomes are caused by sertraline. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/) [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Evidence axes

**Effect on the lactating person**

- The retrieved lactation evidence contains limited direct information about effects on the lactating person.
- The DailyMed label states that there are no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production. This is a limitation of the product-label evidence, not proof that no effects exist. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- The returned postpartum-depression review discusses sertraline in a postpartum/pregnancy context; its returned population metadata does not confirm a lactation-specific study population. [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38811474/)

**Measured infant exposure**

- In a study of **12 lactating women**, maternal milk and serum were assessed; infant serum was assessed in **11 mother–infant pairs**. Sertraline was detected in three infants and desmethylsertraline in six. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed reports that infant serum sertraline is often below assay detection, while norsertraline may be detectable. The returned records include mixed populations and contexts, including lactation, neonates, and preterm infants; these populations should not be generalized to one another. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Some LactMed exposure estimates were generated by pharmacokinetic modeling rather than direct measurement; modeled exposure is not measured human exposure. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

**Observed infant outcomes**

- The 1997 study abstract reported no observed adverse effects among the infants studied; this was a small study and does not establish absence of risk in all infants. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed reports one case involving benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. The retrieved extract does not establish causation. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other small reports described no acute adverse reactions or no abnormal effects, but the studies varied in infant age, prematurity, breastfeeding extent, co-medications, and follow-up. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small study of SSRI exposure during pregnancy and/or breastfeeding reported blunted infant pain responses compared with controls. Four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline, and the source noted that maternal depression-related factors could not be ruled out. This finding should not be generalized to lactation-only sertraline exposure. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Important limitations

- The returned LactMed extract is incomplete: the “Effects in Breastfed Infants” section is truncated, and “Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk” was omitted by the output limit. The extract should not be treated as a complete record. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The returned LactMed evidence base does not establish a study count; reference counts are not study counts, and missing counts are unknown rather than zero. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The PubMed search was a bounded relevance search, not a systematic review. It returned 129 records, but only a small sample was returned with abstracts. [S8](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22sertraline%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Milk%2C+Human%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+lactat%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfed%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- Several returned records lacked an abstract or retrieved full text. No free full text was confirmed for the returned articles.
- Study populations were heterogeneous. Some returned metadata list mixed populations such as pregnancy, lactation, neonates, children, or preterm infants; findings should not be generalized beyond the population actually studied. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12452737/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/) [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The DailyMed result is one selected product label, not all sertraline formulations. Its effective date was November 5, 2026, and DailyMed notes that in-use labeling can differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. The label’s lactation warning title/code also had a source title/code mismatch noted by the lookup. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- FAERS results are reporting volume, not incidence, risk, or causation. The query was not limited to lactating people or infants and does not represent a complete exposed population. [S1](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22sertraline%22&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=8) [S10](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)
- The lookup did not perform an interaction assessment or establish safety for combination products. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Questions to ask your clinician or pharmacist

1. Does this evidence apply to my exact sertraline formulation and any other active ingredients?
2. How closely do the study populations match my infant’s age, feeding pattern, health status, and prematurity history?
3. Which findings are measured milk or infant-serum concentrations, and which are modeled estimates?
4. Which reported infant findings were observed outcomes, rather than monitoring recommendations or uncontrolled reports?
5. What evidence is available about effects on milk production?
6. How should the benefits of treating my condition and the risks of untreated illness be considered alongside infant-exposure evidence?
7. Could any other medicines, supplements, or substances affect the infant-exposure assessment?
8. What follow-up information would be useful for assessing the lactating parent and infant?
