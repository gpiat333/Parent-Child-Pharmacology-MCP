## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Bottom line:** Human evidence—mostly involving **lamotrigine monotherapy in pregnancies affected by epilepsy**—has not shown a consistent increase in overall major congenital malformations. However, an isolated oral-cleft signal has been inconsistent, and long-term neurodevelopmental evidence remains limited. These findings are not a safety verdict.

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The selected DailyMed label is **“LAMOTRIGINE KIT … LAMOTRIGINE TABLET”** from OWP Pharmaceuticals, with an effective date of October 12, 2026. It is one product label, not all formulations; DailyMed states that in-use labeling may differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. The label’s pregnancy section was truncated in one returned field, although the structured pregnancy subsections were available. [S12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label separately describes **disease-associated risks of epilepsy**, including preeclampsia, preterm labor, hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. These are risks attributed in the label to epilepsy with or without antiseizure-medication exposure—not observed evidence that lamotrigine caused them. [S12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also reports that pregnancy-related physiological changes can alter lamotrigine concentrations and clinical effect. This is a clinician-discussion consideration, not an individualized dosing recommendation. [S12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Measured exposure

The retrieved pregnancy evidence generally measures or classifies **maternal lamotrigine exposure**, often as first-trimester monotherapy exposure. The studies do not provide one uniform measure of fetal drug concentration. Most of the label’s human pregnancy data come from people with epilepsy, so applicability to bipolar disorder or other indications is not established. [S12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Fetal and infant outcomes

- **DailyMed label:** International pregnancy registries reported major congenital malformations in 2.2% of 1,558, 2.0% of 1,562, and 2.9% of 2,514 first-trimester lamotrigine-monotherapy exposures, respectively. The label describes these frequencies as similar to general-population estimates. [S12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **Oral clefts:** The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 exposures and an estimated threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries; a European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 with a confidence interval including no association. [S12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **Prospective observational cohort, source population: women with epilepsy, including adolescents and adults; outcome: offspring followed to 1 year:** Major congenital malformations occurred in 110 of 3,584 lamotrigine-monotherapy pregnancies, or 3.1% (95% CI 2.5%–3.7%). This study compared several antiseizure medicines, but comparisons remain observational and may reflect differences in treatment populations. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38497990/)
- **Population-based cohort study, source populations: pregnancy-exposed children in UK primary-care data and Swedish registries; children, including infants, preschool children, and adolescents:** The study found little evidence that children exposed to lamotrigine in pregnancy were more likely to receive neurodevelopmental diagnoses. The authors also described long-term evidence for antiseizure medicines other than valproate as limited. [S27](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39548057/)
- **Observational study, source population: 490 lamotrigine-monotherapy pregnancies and 214 unexposed pregnancies in women with epilepsy:** Fetal malformations occurred in 4.49% versus 3.27%; the reported risk ratio was 1.37 (95% CI 0.60–3.16), and the difference was not statistically significant. The authors stated that the findings did not exclude a possible weak teratogenic effect. [S28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39862525/)

The 2024 practice guideline is a **guideline for people with epilepsy of childbearing potential and pregnancy/neonatal outcomes**, not a lamotrigine-only trial. Its recommendations address balancing seizure control and fetal outcomes and should not be converted into an individualized treatment decision. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)

### Limitations

- The evidence is observational rather than randomized.
- Confounding may arise from the underlying condition, seizure severity, co-medications, folate use, adherence, dose, and other maternal factors.
- Most evidence concerns epilepsy and monotherapy; evidence for bipolar disorder, other indications, and polytherapy is less directly established.
- Registry estimates differ in exposure window, comparator group, outcome definition, and follow-up duration.
- A PubMed search returned 690 records, but the retrieved records were a relevance-ranked subset, not a systematic-review search. [S13](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=10&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- **MPRINT-specific coverage:** the Gold MPRINT pregnancy search returned no records for lamotrigine. This means no records were returned by that MPRINT snapshot query—not that MPRINT or the broader literature contains no evidence. The result was from the MPRINT Gold source; no Silver record was substituted. The MPRINT implementation reports that population linkage and interpretation guidance are MCP-generated. [S14](https://github.com/langli-lab/mprint-kp-data)
- In the returned MPRINT metadata, no outcome fields were present because no matching records were returned; therefore, there are no null outcome fields to interpret as zero.

### Questions for the clinician

1. How does the evidence apply to the underlying indication—epilepsy, bipolar disorder, or another condition?
2. Which studies best match the relevant exposure: monotherapy or polytherapy, and which gestational period?
3. How should the inconsistent oral-cleft findings be interpreted for the relevant comparator group?
4. What evidence is available for the specific formulation and indication?
5. How should pregnancy-related changes in measured lamotrigine exposure and clinical response be assessed?
6. Would participation in the North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry be relevant?

The retrieved label notes that stopping or changing medication can also carry risks; any treatment change belongs in a discussion with the prescribing clinician.
