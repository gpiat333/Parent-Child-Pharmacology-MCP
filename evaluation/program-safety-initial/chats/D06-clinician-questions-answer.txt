### Questions to ask your clinician

**Applying the evidence**
- Does the evidence apply to my exact sertraline product and formulation?
- How closely do the studies match my breastfeeding situation and my infant’s age, prematurity, medical conditions, and feeding pattern?
- Which findings are measured drug exposure, and which are observed infant outcomes?
- What important evidence is missing or uncertain?

**Infant exposure**
- What do the reported breastmilk and infant-serum measurements mean for exposure in my infant?
- How should the parent drug and metabolite norsertraline be considered separately?
- Does prematurity or possible reduced infant metabolism change how the evidence applies?
- Would infant testing be clinically useful in my circumstances?

**Observed infant outcomes**
- What outcomes were actually observed in the studies, and which statements are only source recommendations?
- How should isolated reports of agitation, sleep myoclonus, or sedation be interpreted, particularly when other medicines were involved?
- What developmental follow-up evidence is available, and what are its limitations?
- What changes in feeding, growth, alertness, or behavior should I report?

**Lactation and parent health**
- What evidence is available about effects on milk production? The retrieved LactMed extract omitted the “Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk” section, so that part of the record is incomplete. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- How should my clinical need for treatment and the risks of untreated or worsening illness be included in the discussion?
- What follow-up should address both my wellbeing and my infant’s feeding, growth, and development?

### Evidence limitations to discuss

- LactMed is a lactation source; its returned extract was truncated, with the milk-production section omitted and the infant-effects section incomplete. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The PubMed search was bounded and not a systematic review; it returned records with mixed or missing population metadata. For example, the 1997 breastmilk study included pregnancy, lactation, and neonate labels, while the 2024 postpartum review was labeled pregnancy rather than confirmed lactation. [S8](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22sertraline%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Milk%2C+Human%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+lactat%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfed%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json) [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38811474/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- The returned PubMed records included a review, a clinical pharmacokinetics review, and a primary breastmilk/infant-exposure study; the reported study size for that primary study was 12 lactating women, with 11 mother–infant pairs assessed for serum levels. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12452737/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- The DailyMed record was for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, not all sertraline formulations. Its lactation section states that milk-production effects were not available in that label. The retrieved label had effective date November 5, 2026, and its lactation warning code was flagged by the lookup as having a source title/code mismatch requiring verification. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- FAERS figures are reporting volume, not incidence, risk, or proof of causation, and the query was not limited to breastfeeding people or infants.
