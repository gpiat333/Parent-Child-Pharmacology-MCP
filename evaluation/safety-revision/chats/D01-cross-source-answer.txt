## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured milk and infant exposure — LactMed

LactMed reports that sertraline is measured in breast milk, generally at low concentrations. Norsertraline, its metabolite, is also often detectable at low levels in infant serum. Across the summarized studies, estimated relative infant exposure was generally about **0.5%–1% of the maternal weight-adjusted exposure**, although results varied by study, milk-sampling time, and breastfeeding extent. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

In several studies, sertraline was undetectable in infant serum, while norsertraline was sometimes detectable. These are measured exposure findings, not clinical outcomes. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes — LactMed

Reported outcomes included:

- Several small observational groups in which no acute adverse effects were detected.
- One follow-up group reporting normal infant weight gain at 6 months and no abnormal effects reported by mothers.
- One report of benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation.
- One preterm-infant case involving symptoms resembling neonatal withdrawal and measurable infant serum concentrations. The retrieved LactMed text is truncated during this case, so later outcome details are missing in the retrieved record.
- A study of infant pain responses that included four sertraline-exposed infants; the study could not distinguish medication effects from effects related to maternal depression.

These findings are observational and do not establish that sertraline caused any reported event. The studies also do not establish that results apply to every infant, particularly preterm or medically complex infants. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The MCP lookup did not confirm that this is the exact product or formulation being used; that is a product-matching limitation, not a conclusion about other sertraline products. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The retrieved label lists adverse reactions for the person taking sertraline, including hypersensitivity reactions, QTc prolongation and ventricular arrhythmias with pimozide, suicidal thoughts and behaviors, serotonin syndrome, increased bleeding risk, activation of mania or hypomania, discontinuation syndrome, seizures, angle-closure glaucoma, hyponatremia, and sexual dysfunction. These label findings are not automatically observed effects in a breastfed infant. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The retrieved label output did not provide a separate infant-outcome analysis. Therefore, infant exposure and infant outcomes should be taken from LactMed rather than inferred from the label’s adverse-reaction section.

### Questions to ask a clinician

- Does the selected label match the exact product and formulation?
- How closely does the LactMed evidence match the infant’s age, feeding pattern, and prematurity or medical history?
- Which findings represent measured exposure, and which represent observed clinical outcomes?
- What uncertainties remain because the evidence comes mainly from small observational studies?
- What follow-up information should be discussed for the breastfeeding parent and infant?
- Which benefits and risks are relevant to the individual treatment decision?
