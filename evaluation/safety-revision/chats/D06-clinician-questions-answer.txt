## Questions to ask your clinician

### How strong is the evidence?
- How well do the available studies apply to my baby’s age, feeding pattern, gestational age, and prematurity status?
- Which findings measure **sertraline exposure** in breast milk or infant blood, and which measure **observed infant outcomes**?
- How much confidence should we place in the available studies given their sample sizes, follow-up periods, and comparison groups?
- What important uncertainties or missing evidence remain?

### About the infant
- What infant outcomes have actually been observed in the breastfeeding studies?
- Does the evidence differ for premature infants or infants who may metabolize medicines differently?
- What follow-up would be appropriate for my baby based on the evidence and our circumstances?
- How can possible medication-related findings be distinguished from effects of the underlying maternal condition or other medicines?

### About breastfeeding and milk production
- What evidence is available about effects on milk production or breastfeeding success?
- Does the evidence differ between exclusive and partial breastfeeding?
- Are there relevant interactions with my other prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, or supplements?

### About my specific product
- Does the evidence apply to my exact sertraline formulation, manufacturer, and all active ingredients?
- Does the current product label contain breastfeeding-specific information relevant to my situation?
- Are there newer studies or guidelines that were not captured in this brief?

### About the overall decision
- How should the potential benefits of treating my condition be weighed against the available breastfeeding evidence?
- What alternatives have comparable evidence in lactation?
- What changes in my infant or breastfeeding experience should prompt a call to the clinician or pediatrician?
- Which follow-up suggestions are monitoring recommendations, rather than evidence that those outcomes occurred?

## What the retrieved brief supports

- The retrieved LactMed record describes generally low sertraline levels in breast milk and often-undetectable infant serum sertraline, while the metabolite norsertraline may be detectable. It also describes mostly reassuring reported infant findings, but includes a report involving a preterm infant with symptoms and measured infant exposure. The record was **truncated**, so its complete outcome information was not available. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed product is **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**. Its lactation section reports low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk and summarizes a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs in which no adverse reactions were observed. It also states that effects on milk production are unknown. This is one product label, not all formulations. The tool flagged a title/code mismatch for the returned “8.2 Lactation” warning, so the linked label should be verified. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- The PubMed search returned five records, but abstracts were not retrieved. Therefore, this brief does **not** support summarizing the findings of those individual articles. [S8](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28sertraline+evidence%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5Btiab%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&datetype=pdat&reldate=3650&retmode=json) The returned records were:

  - *Antidepressants for preventing postnatal depression* [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29669175/)
  - *Antidepressants in breast milk; comparative analysis of excretion ratios* [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30116895/)

- FAERS results were for reports matching sertraline in an “any” cohort, not a complete breastfeeding or infant-exposure population. The returned counts represent reporting volume, not incidence or causation, and cannot estimate individual risk. [S10](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)
- **NCT03511118** is a recruiting pharmacokinetic and safety study of **lactating women and their breastfed infants**; its intervention list includes sertraline among multiple drugs. The study type is pharmacokinetic/safety research focused on transfer into breast milk and estimated infant exposure, not proof of clinical benefit or safety. The registry includes mixed population labels—`CHILD`, `ADULT`, and `OLDER_ADULT`—while numeric age fields are missing; the MCP therefore marks pediatric age support as unverified. Site recruitment and individual eligibility were not confirmed. [S5](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03511118)
