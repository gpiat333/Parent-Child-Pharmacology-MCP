LactMed’s report on **amoxicillin in breastfed infants**:

**Observed symptoms**
- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, **3 infants had diarrhea**; no rash or candidiasis was reported.
- In a prospective study of 40 infants exposed through breast milk, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**.
- One comparison study reported reactions in **8.3%** of amoxicillin-exposed infants, similar to the macrolide comparison group; reported reactions included **rash and sleepiness**.
- One case report described recurrent **generalized hives** in a 2-month-old after breastfeeding while the mother was taking amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin. The source could not determine which drug caused the reaction; it considered the combination antibiotic the more likely cause.
- One report described **mild diarrhea** in an 11-month-old whose mother was also taking acetaminophen plus codeine.
- In a study of 25 breastfeeding mothers, **no infant adverse effects were reported**.

**Advice to monitor—not observed symptoms**
A controlled study asked mothers to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. This was a **study-monitoring protocol**, not evidence that these symptoms occurred. The study found no statistical differences between infants of mothers taking related antibiotics and control infants.

**Uncertainty**
LactMed states that rash and possible gastrointestinal-flora disruption, including diarrhea or thrush, have been reported but **were not adequately evaluated**. The reports were small, and some involved other medicines or combination products; therefore, the reported timing does not by itself prove that amoxicillin caused each symptom. Published infant blood-level information was not found in the LactMed record. The extract’s study count is not established, and its reference count should not be treated as a study count.
