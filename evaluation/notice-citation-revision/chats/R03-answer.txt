## Metformin: label warnings versus breastfed-infant findings

### What the selected product label says about the adult taking metformin

The retrieved DailyMed label is **“METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]”**. It describes lactic acidosis as a rare, serious complication associated with metformin accumulation and identifies renal dysfunction, hypoperfusion or hypoxemia, dehydration, sepsis, hepatic disease, and some cardiac conditions as relevant risk contexts. These warnings concern the **medicine-taking label population**, not breastfed infants. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The same label reports adult adverse reactions including diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, indigestion, abdominal discomfort, headache, and hypoglycemia. These adult-label findings should not be relabeled as infant outcomes. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### What the label says about lactation

The label states that metformin is excreted into milk in lactating animals and that comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers. It raises a **potential** concern about hypoglycemia in nursing infants. That is a label warning or consideration—not an observed report that hypoglycemia occurred in an infant. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### What LactMed reports in breastfed infants

LactMed separates measured exposure from observed outcomes:

- **Measured exposure:** Metformin levels in milk were generally low; low levels were sometimes detectable in infant serum. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Observed outcomes:** Seven infants aged 5–25 months were described as healthy with expected growth and development; two had normal Denver developmental screening results. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- Three infants aged 2, 5, and 14 months had no detectable adverse effects reported. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- In three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days postpartum, none had low blood glucose, and mothers reported no infant adverse reactions. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- In a nonrandomized prospective study of 111 infants—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—no differences were reported at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates. This was a mixed feeding population, not an exclusively breastfed population. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

These LactMed findings describe the reported study populations only. They do not establish that every breastfed infant will have the same outcome, and the returned LactMed data do not establish a total study count.

### Comparison

- **Adult label warning:** serious lactic acidosis and other adult adverse reactions are described for the person taking metformin. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- **Infant evidence:** LactMed reports low measured exposure and no reported adverse effects or low blood glucose in the specific infant groups described. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

LactMed’s measured milk or infant-serum concentrations are exposure findings, not clinical outcomes.
