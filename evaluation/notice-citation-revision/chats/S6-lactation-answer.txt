## Sertraline during lactation

**Overall evidence picture:** LactMed describes generally low sertraline transfer into milk, with infant serum sertraline often undetectable and low-level norsertraline sometimes detectable. This is measured exposure—not proof of benefit, harm, or safety. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

- In small studies, milk contained measurable sertraline and norsertraline, but estimated infant exposure was generally low. Concentrations varied with sampling time and milk fraction; one study found higher concentrations in hindmilk than foremilk. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Among **30 breastfed infants aged 6–13 weeks**—19 exclusively breastfed and 11 breastfed at least half the time—22 had undetectable infant-serum sertraline; 8 had detectable levels. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In another study of **22 breastfed infants aged 4–28 weeks**, 4 had detectable sertraline and 11 had detectable norsertraline. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** reported infant plasma concentrations averaging 2% of maternal concentrations, with a reported range of 0–15%. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

Milk or infant-serum measurements should not be conflated with observed clinical outcomes.

### Observed infant outcomes

- Several small observational groups reported no acute adverse effects, no reported developmental problems, or normal reported weight gain during follow-up. These findings came from small, non-randomized groups and do not establish absence of risk. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One case report described benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation in an infant. The retrieved source does not establish that sertraline caused these findings. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A retrospective study of **30 nursing mothers** reported adverse effects in 5 infants, including insomnia or restlessness; crying and poor feeding were less common. This was an uncontrolled retrospective study, and the retrieved record does not establish causation. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate report involved sedation in an infant whose mother was also taking other medicines, so the contribution of sertraline alone was unclear. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small study reported blunted pain responses among infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy, with or without breastfeeding exposure. Only 4 infants were exposed to sertraline, and the comparison group differed in maternal depression and treatment status; the source states that maternal factors could not be separated from medication effects. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Rare preterm-infant case

A case report concerned a **33-week preterm infant** exposed during pregnancy and while breastfeeding. The infant had hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying; infant serum contained sertraline and its metabolite, and the infant was later found to have genetically intermediate metabolism of relevant enzymes. The report attributed the reaction probably to sertraline, but it cannot determine how much was attributable specifically to breastmilk exposure rather than prenatal exposure or the infant’s metabolism. The source describes breastfeeding discontinuation after the symptoms had continued and subsequently reports symptom resolution and lower serum concentrations; the exact elapsed time between these events is not fully specified in the retrieved record. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Evidence limitations

- The retrieved LactMed record does not establish a verified study count; its reference count is not a study count. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The evidence includes small pharmacokinetic studies, observational studies, retrospective data, case reports, and a meta-analysis, with inconsistent reporting of breastfeeding extent, infant age, prematurity, co-medications, sampling timing, and maternal illness. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The infant-effects section was truncated in the retrieved record, and the section on effects on lactation and breastmilk was omitted by the retrieval limit. This summary therefore cannot be treated as complete. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- “No adverse effects observed” in a small group is not evidence that the medicine is risk-free or comprehensively studied. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Stopping or changing treatment can also carry risks; treatment changes belong in a discussion with the prescribing clinician. This is a continuity-of-care limitation of the lookup, not a treatment recommendation. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
