## Sertraline during breastfeeding

The medication identity was matched exactly to **sertraline**. The evidence below comes from LactMed and one selected DailyMed product label.

### Measured milk and infant exposure — LactMed

- Sertraline and its metabolite **norsertraline** were measured in breast milk. Reported milk concentrations varied substantially between mothers and sampling times; norsertraline was often measurable at low levels. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In the reported studies, calculated relative infant exposure was generally low, but these estimates came from specific study populations and are not individualized exposure calculations. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Infant serum testing often found sertraline below the assay’s detection limit. Low levels of sertraline, norsertraline, or both were detected in some infants. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One reported preterm infant had measurable sertraline and norsertraline in serum while being breastfed. The record also reported symptoms resembling neonatal abstinence and genetically intermediate metabolism; this is a case report and does not establish that sertraline caused the symptoms. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

Milk or infant-serum concentration is a **measured exposure**, not a clinical outcome.

### Observed infant outcomes — LactMed

- Several reported observational groups found no acute adverse effects during the study follow-up. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Reported infant growth was normal in one follow-up group, and mothers reported no abnormal effects in their infants. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One case report described benign sleep myoclonus and agitation that resolved spontaneously. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The returned LactMed summary states that no developmental adverse effects were found in infants followed up to 5 years, but the study count and full underlying study details were not established in this extract. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- LactMed describes possible accumulation and neonatal-abstinence-like symptoms as a concern particularly for some preterm infants with impaired metabolism; this is source context and not evidence that such symptoms occurred broadly. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The LactMed **Effects in Breastfed Infants** section was truncated, and **Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk** was omitted by the retrieval limit. Therefore, this is not a complete LactMed record. Missing study counts are unknown, not zero, and the retrieved evidence is not a safety verdict.
