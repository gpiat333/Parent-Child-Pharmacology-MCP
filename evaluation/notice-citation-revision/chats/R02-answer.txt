**Measured in breast milk (exposure evidence):**

- Milk samples showed measurable sumatriptan concentrations. In one study, peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L**; in another, peak concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** and average concentration **23.2 mcg/L**. Researchers also calculated estimated infant intake from milk. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
- **Published infant blood-level data were not found** as of the LactMed revision date. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

**Actually observed in infants (clinical-outcome evidence):**

- Manufacturer correspondence reported that among **three women** who used sumatriptan while breastfeeding, none reported adverse effects in their infants. The doses and routes were unspecified. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

These are different evidence types: milk concentrations and calculated intake measure **potential infant exposure**; reports about infants describe **observed clinical outcomes**. The milk measurements do not establish what an infant experienced, while the three reported infant observations do not measure the infants’ drug concentrations or establish that adverse effects cannot occur. The LactMed extract does not establish a study count; that count is unknown, not zero. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
