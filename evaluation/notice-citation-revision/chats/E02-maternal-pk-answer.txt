### Effects on the pregnant person

The retrieved human literature includes mixed populations:

- **Pregnancy; people with epilepsy; review:** Pregnancy management involves balancing seizure control for the pregnant person with fetal outcomes. The review also describes pregnancy-related changes in antiseizure-medication concentrations and seizure risk. This is review context, not a lamotrigine-specific primary outcome estimate. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- **Pregnancy; people with epilepsy; regulatory labeling:** The selected DailyMed label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of lower concentrations during pregnancy and restoration toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is a Clinical Considerations statement, not a complete pharmacokinetic dataset. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **Pregnancy and lactation; naturalistic study of 60 mothers and their children:** The abstract reports measurement of psychotropic-drug concentrations and child development, but the retrieved text does not establish a lamotrigine-specific maternal serum trajectory. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- **Pregnancy and lactation; review:** A review specifically discusses lamotrigine pharmacokinetics, but detailed lamotrigine-specific human PK results were not retrieved in the abstract. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)

### Fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The selected label reports first-trimester **lamotrigine monotherapy** registry estimates:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: 2.2% among 1,558 exposed infants.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: 2.0% among 1,562 exposed infants.
- EURAP: 2.9% among 2,514 exposures.

These source populations are registry cohorts of pregnancy exposures, predominantly in people with epilepsy. The label reports no overall increased frequency compared with general-population estimates, but the cohorts, comparators, and ascertainment methods differ. These figures should not be generalized to every formulation, combination regimen, indication, or exposure pattern. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 exposed infants and described this as higher than in unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 with a confidence interval including no association. The retrieved evidence is therefore inconsistent, not uniform. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A **completed case-control registry study** in the EUROCAT population-based congenital-anomaly-register network reported no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with other defects. The record has no posted results, and its comparison design did not include non-malformed infants. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

#### Other outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while also stating that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This limitation means the findings should not be interpreted as proof of no risk. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The naturalistic study of **60 mothers and their children; mixed pregnancy, lactation, neonatal, and child population** reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or development during the first year between its in-utero-exposed and nonexposed groups. Because the abstract covers multiple psychotropic medicines and does not establish lamotrigine-specific results for each outcome, the finding cannot be generalized as a lamotrigine-specific safety estimate. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Animal evidence

The selected label reports developmental findings in pregnant animals, including reduced fetal body weight, skeletal variations, neurobehavioral findings, offspring mortality, and reduced fetal folate concentrations. These are animal findings and do not provide human risk estimates. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
