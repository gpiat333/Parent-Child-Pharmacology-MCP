**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926; 2022) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

- **Study design:** Women with epilepsy who were receiving levetiracetam completed a questionnaire. Researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples from before dosing through 12 hours after dosing and in one pre-dose maternal serum sample.
- **Population:** 20 breastfeeding women and 21 infants; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed. Infant ages, prematurity, and other detailed demographic characteristics were not provided in the retrieved abstract.

**Measured drug exposure**
- The trough breast-milk/serum levetiracetam ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- The abstract reported estimated infant exposure through breast milk and relative infant exposure of **13.8 ± 3.1%** in fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** in partially breastfed infants.
- Maternal levetiracetam dose, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk exposure over time were substantially correlated.

**Observed infant outcomes**
- Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding; this temporal relationship does not establish causation.
- The abstract states that all infants gained weight according to age.
- The abstract states that further research was needed to assess short- and long-term safety.

The reported exposure quantities are historical study findings, not recommendations. Infant outcomes and exposure findings came from this specific group of breastfeeding women and infants and should not be generalized beyond that population. Evidence retrieved here is not a safety verdict; stopping or changing medication can also carry risks and belongs in a prescribing-clinician conversation.
