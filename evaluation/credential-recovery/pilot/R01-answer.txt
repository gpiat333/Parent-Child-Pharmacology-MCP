**Observed symptoms reported in breastfed infants**

- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, 3 reported infant diarrhea; no rash or candidiasis was reported.
- In a prospective study of 40 infants exposed to amoxicillin through breastmilk, 2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash.
- One comparison study reported reactions in 8.3% of amoxicillin-exposed infants—similar to the comparison antibiotic group—with rash and somnolence among the reported reactions.
- One 11-month-old reportedly had diarrhea while exposed to amoxicillin plus acetaminophen and codeine; the contribution of amoxicillin was therefore uncertain.
- A case report described recurrent hives after breastmilk exposure while the mother was receiving amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin. The specific causative drug could not be determined.
- In another study of 25 mothers and their infants, no infant adverse effects were reported. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

**Monitoring reported in a study**

Mothers in one controlled prospective study were asked to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, changes in stool frequency or consistency, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were items the study asked mothers to watch for—not symptoms shown to have occurred. The study found no statistical differences in those measures between infants of mothers taking related antibiotics and control infants. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

**Uncertainty**

LactMed reports that occasional rash, diarrhea, or thrush have been described, but says these effects have not been adequately evaluated. The evidence comes mainly from small observational studies and case reports, with possible co-exposures and limited ability to establish causation. Infant blood-level information was not found in the LactMed record. Therefore, the reports document observed events but do not establish how often amoxicillin caused them. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
