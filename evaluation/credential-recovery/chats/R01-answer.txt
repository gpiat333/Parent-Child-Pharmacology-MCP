LactMed reports:

**Observed symptoms**
- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, **3 infants had diarrhea**; no rash or candidiasis was reported.
- Among **40 infants exposed through breast milk**, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**.
- In a comparison study, reported reactions among amoxicillin-exposed infants occurred in **8.3%**, similar to the comparison group; reactions included **rash and sleepiness**.
- One case report described **recurrent generalized hives** after breastfeeding. Because the mother had received amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin, the specific causative drug was not established.
- Another report described **mild diarrhea** in an 11-month-old whose mother was also taking acetaminophen plus codeine.
- In a study of 25 mothers with infants older than 4 weeks, **no infant adverse effects were reported**.

**Advice to monitor—not observed outcomes**
A prospective study asked mothers to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulty, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were monitoring items, not evidence that all—or necessarily any—of those symptoms occurred. The study found no statistical differences between infants of mothers taking related antibiotics and control infants.

**Uncertainty**
LactMed states that occasional rash and gastrointestinal effects such as diarrhea or thrush have been reported, but these effects **have not been adequately evaluated**. The evidence consists largely of small observational studies and parental reports. A symptom after exposure therefore does not prove that amoxicillin caused it. LactMed also reports low milk levels, while published infant blood-level information was not found as of the record’s revision date. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
