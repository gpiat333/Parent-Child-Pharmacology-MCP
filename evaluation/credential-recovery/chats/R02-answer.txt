Yes—these are different kinds of evidence:

- **Measured in breastmilk (exposure evidence):** Sumatriptan was detected in milk. In one study of five breastfeeding women, the peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L**, occurring about **2.5 hours** after exposure. In another study of seven women using different formulations, peak milk concentrations averaged **59.3 mcg/L**, generally occurring **2–4 hours** afterward. **Published infant blood-level data were not found** in the LactMed record. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

- **Observed in infants (clinical-outcome evidence):** LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence concerning **three women** who used sumatriptan during breastfeeding; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The dose and route were unspecified, and this was a small, nonsystematic report—not direct measurement of infant drug levels or a controlled infant-outcome study. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Thus, milk measurements show **drug exposure**, whereas the infant report describes **reported clinical observations**. Measurable milk exposure does not by itself establish harm, and no reported adverse effects in three infants cannot establish universal safety. LactMed also describes maternal breast/nipple symptoms and occasional decreased milk production; those are maternal lactation findings, not observed infant effects. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
