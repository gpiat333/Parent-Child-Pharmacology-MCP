R01: What does LactMed report about amoxicillin in breastfed babies? Separate observed symptoms from advice to monitor, and explain uncertainty. Do not give a dose.

With MCP

17.17 seconds; 3 tool calls

LactMed’s amoxicillin record reports:

**Observed symptoms in breastfed infants**
- In one telephone follow-up of 25 mothers, **3 reported infant diarrhea**; no rash or candidiasis was reported.
- In another study of 40 exposed infants, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**.
- A comparative study reported adverse reactions in **8.3%** of amoxicillin-exposed infants, similar to the comparison group; reported reactions included **rash and sleepiness**.
- One infant developed **recurrent generalized hives** shortly after breastfeeding. However, the mother was taking amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin, so the responsible drug could not be determined.
- In a study involving 25 mothers, **no infant adverse effects were reported**.

**Advice to monitor—not evidence that symptoms occurred**
LactMed says that, because occasional reports have involved **rash, diarrhea, or thrush**, clinicians or parents may watch for those effects. That monitoring advice should not be interpreted as evidence that a particular infant experienced them.

**Uncertainty**
LactMed states that amoxicillin produces low milk levels and is not expected to cause adverse effects, but the reported infant effects have **not been adequately evaluated**. The studies were small, some relied on parental reports, and symptoms such as diarrhea or rash can have causes unrelated to amoxicillin. Published infant blood-level data were not available in the record. Thus, the evidence suggests generally low exposure, but it does not prove that adverse effects cannot occur.  
Source: [LactMed—Amoxicillin, NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### 2) Product label: lactation warning
The same label states that animal studies found metformin in milk at levels comparable with plasma, while comparable studies had **not been conducted in nursing mothers**. It says a potential for hypoglycemia in nursing infants may exist and presents breastfeeding-versus-treatment as a decision requiring consideration of the drug’s importance to the mother.

This is a **label warning based partly on animal data and theoretical concern**, not a report that hypoglycemia occurred in breastfed infants.

Source: [DailyMed lactation section](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### 3) LactMed: measured exposure and observed infant findings
The NIH/NLM LactMed record reports:

- **Milk exposure:** Studies found low milk concentrations; LactMed summarizes estimated infant exposure as generally below 0.5% of the mother’s weight-adjusted amount.
- **Infant blood levels:** Metformin was sometimes detectable at low concentrations in infant serum, while it was undetectable in some tested infants.
- **Observed outcomes:** Reported infants were generally described as healthy, with expected growth and development. In a prospective study, no differences were found in several growth, developmental, or illness outcomes between predominantly breastfed and formula-fed groups.
- **Hypoglycemia findings:** In one report involving three newborns, none had low blood glucose; mothers reported no adverse reactions.
- **Limitations:** Some reports did not specify the extent of breastfeeding, infant ages, exposure duration, or other details. LactMed advises caution for newborn or premature infants and infants with renal impairment; that is a source recommendation, not evidence that harm occurred.

Source: [NIH LactMed: Metformin](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### FDA recall lookup

The FDA enforcement search returned naproxen-related records, but **none listed the fictional lot `NOTREAL-77`**. Examples include recalls involving specific naproxen formulations and lots such as `23F02`, `25A37`, `17231956`, and others.  
Source: [FDA openFDA enforcement search](https://api.fda.gov/drug/enforcement.json?search=product_description%3A%22naproxen%22&sort=report_date%3Adesc&limit=20)

### Does the missing lot establish the bottle was unaffected?

**No.** A lot not appearing in this lookup does **not** clear the bottle or prove it was unaffected. FDA records may be incomplete or lag announcements, and some recalls cover ranges, distribution periods, or “all lots.” The search is a recall-record lookup, not bottle-level verification.  
Source: [FDA recall-record limitations and official recall page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/drug-recalls)

### What still needs verification

Verify the bottle against the **specific official recall notice or the manufacturer/pharmacist**, including:

- exact product name and formulation—naproxen versus naproxen sodium, tablet versus suspension;
- strength, package size, NDC, manufacturer, distributor, and repackager;
- lot number exactly as printed, including possible prefixes, suffixes, or formatting differences;
- expiration date and distribution dates;
- whether the notice covers a lot range, all lots, or a broader production/distribution period;
- whether the FDA record is current and whether a newer manufacturer notice exists.

The FDA records themselves advise that a different or missing lot is **not clearance** and that formulation, manufacturer, lot, and expiration must be checked against the official notice. If the product’s identity or recall status remains uncertain, the appropriate verification source is the dispensing pharmacist or manufacturer—not the absence of a database match.
