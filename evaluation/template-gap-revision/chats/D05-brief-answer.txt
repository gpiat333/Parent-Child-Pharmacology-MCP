### Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed reports galactorrhea in nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients; a pharmacovigilance study did not find an increased hyperprolactinemia risk compared with other medicines. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small report described decreased milk supply in six patients after starting sertraline, but this does not establish causation. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small mixed-SSRI study reported delayed lactogenesis; only one participant used sertraline, so the finding is not sertraline-specific. The returned source also reported no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day 4. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- Small studies and cohorts reported no observed acute adverse reactions, no adverse reactions requiring medical attention, or no abnormal effects during the reported follow-up periods. These findings came from limited populations and do not establish safety for every infant. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One report described benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation in a 4-month-old infant. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate case involved a preterm infant born at 33 weeks who developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation problems, and high-pitched crying. The report also described elevated infant serum concentrations and attributed the reaction to sertraline in the context of reduced metabolism. This was a single case report and should not be generalized beyond that preterm-infant context. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In another report, infant sedation occurred with maternal sertraline plus other sedating medicines; attribution to sertraline alone is not possible from the returned passage. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

LactMed’s Drug Levels and Effects in Breastfed Infants sections were truncated in the returned extract, so additional details may be missing. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
