### LactMed findings in breastfed infants

LactMed summarizes human lactation studies involving breastfed infants and their mothers. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

**Measured exposure, not outcomes**

- Reported metformin concentrations in breast milk included averages of **270 mcg/L**, approximately **380 mcg/L** in one sample, **130–270 mcg/L** in another group, and **410 mcg/L** in an early-postpartum group.
- Infant **serum** measurements were available in some studies: two infants had concentrations below **10 mcg/L**, two had reported concentrations of **50 and 80 mcg/L**, and two other infants had concentrations below **5 mcg/L**. Some infant ages and the extent of nursing were not stated.
- These measurements show exposure; they do not by themselves establish safety or harm. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

**Observed infant outcomes**

- In a group of **seven infants aged 5–25 months**, the source reports expected growth and development; two also had normal developmental screening.
- In a group of **three infants aged 2, 5, and 14 months**, no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin were reported.
- In **three early-postpartum infants**, no low-blood-glucose findings were reported, and their mothers reported no infant adverse reactions.
- A prospective, nonrandomized study involving **111 infants**—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—reported no differences at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates between the groups. This mixed feeding population should not be generalized to exclusively breastfed infants. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### What the lactation warning means

The DailyMed label states that metformin enters milk in animal studies and raises a **potential** concern about hypoglycemia in nursing infants. The returned label explicitly distinguishes this warning from observed infant outcomes; it is not evidence that hypoglycemia occurred in the infants described by LactMed. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

LactMed separately reports measured milk and infant-serum concentrations and the infant observations above. Its statement about caution for newborn, premature, or renally impaired infants is a source recommendation, not an observed outcome. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
