### Fetal exposure

- No verified numerical fetal blood, cord-blood, or other fetal lamotrigine concentration was retrieved.
- The label’s pregnancy evidence is based mainly on exposure registries and epidemiologic studies, rather than direct fetal-concentration measurements. Most reported pregnancy exposure data came from people with epilepsy. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** included maternal serum and breast-milk measurements and followed exposed children for 12 months. The abstract reports a high breast-milk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but this is lactation evidence, not fetal exposure evidence; a lamotrigine-specific pregnancy concentration was not reported in the retrieved abstract. The returned population labels were mixed—pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child—and the record is a research article, not a lamotrigine-only study. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- **NCT05450978**, an observational study listed as **RECRUITING**, investigates antiseizure-medication clearance during pregnancy and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. Registration and planned measurements are not posted outcome data. Its returned population is pregnant adults with epilepsy. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### Fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

For **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy exposure**, the selected label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2%** among **1,558 infants**; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: **2.0%** among **1,562 infants**.
- EURAP registry: **2.9%** among **2,514 exposures**; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

These were registry populations, predominantly involving epilepsy; they should not automatically be generalized to other indications, combination regimens, formulations, or individual pregnancies. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Isolated oral clefts

- The NAAED registry reported **3.2 per 1,000** among **2,200 infants** exposed early in pregnancy, compared with unexposed healthy controls; the label describes this as a threefold relative increase.
- The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries.
- A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**; 95% CI 0.8–2.63.

These findings are not fully consistent across the reported sources. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The completed EUROCAT registry record describes a case-control study of first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy exposure and isolated oral clefts. Its returned summary says no statistically significant increased risk was found compared with other defects, but the study record has **no posted results**; the registry summary is not a substitute for the full publication. The record’s population field is missing in the returned metadata, and its study type is a registry-based case-control study. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

#### Other outcomes

The label says meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports that outcome-definition, ascertainment, and comparator differences limit conclusions; underlying study counts and estimates were not returned. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant developmental differences during the first 12 months. Because the retrieved abstract does not establish the lamotrigine-specific sample size or comparator structure, this should not be treated as a lamotrigine-specific conclusion. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
