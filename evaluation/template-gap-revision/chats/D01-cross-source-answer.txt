### Measured milk exposure — LactMed

Reported **breastmilk concentrations** were:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L** average (range **7–207 mcg/L**) in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- In 26 women with 24-hour sampling: sertraline **129 mcg/L** average (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline **258 mcg/L** average (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- At 2 months postpartum, average milk concentrations were sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L**, sampled at random times.
- In a report involving a preterm infant, random-time breastmilk averages were **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- Foremilk and hindmilk collected 13 hours after exposure contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively.
- In first-week postpartum trough samples, sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one woman and **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight others.
- In six women providing random samples, the median sertraline concentration was **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**).
- In another study, the average milk concentration was **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).

### Observed infant outcomes — LactMed

The retrieved LactMed text reported:

- One 4-month-old infant had benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation in a case report.
- In a group of 26 infants, no acute adverse reactions were detected during the study.
- In 14 mother–infant pairs, infant whole-blood serotonin measurements were similar before and after maternal treatment; no infant adverse effects were reported in that study.
- In 25 infants followed through 6 months, reported weight gain was within national growth standards and mothers reported no abnormal effects.
- No clinical adverse reactions were noted in six infants aged 5–34 weeks in one study and in seven 4-week-old infants in another.
- A separate case involving a **preterm infant** reported hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying. The source reported high infant serum concentrations and reduced metabolism in that case; this single case does not establish that the circumstances apply to other infants.
- One co-exposure report described sedation in an infant whose mother was also taking other medicines; it does not isolate sertraline’s effect.
- A small study reported blunted pain responses in infants exposed to an SSRI before birth and/or through breastmilk. Four infants were exposed to sertraline, but the comparison group did not control for maternal depression, so the source could not exclude maternal factors.

These findings come from different populations and study types, including observational studies and case reports.
