### 1. Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?

**Study type:** Observational pharmacokinetic study.  
**Population:** 20 breastfeeding women with epilepsy and 21 infants: 16 fully breastfed and 5 partially breastfed; one woman had twins. Infant age and prematurity details are missing in the returned metadata. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

- **Drug exposure:** Levetiracetam was measured in serial breast-milk samples and in a maternal serum sample. The reported trough breast-milk/serum ratio was approximately 1. The abstract also reports calculated infant exposure estimates and correlations between maternal exposure, maternal serum concentrations, and breast-milk concentrations.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that it resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding. All infants gained weight according to age.
- **Interpretation:** The authors stated that infant exposure was close to the thresholds they used for interpretation and that the somnolence reports could be related to breast-milk exposure. This is an observed temporal association reported in the abstract, not proof of causation. The abstract states that further short- and long-term safety studies are needed.

### 2. Levetiracetam concentrations in serum and in breast milk at birth and during lactation

**Study type:** Observational pharmacokinetic study.  
**Population:** Eight breastfeeding women with epilepsy and their infants; the returned source population includes pregnancy, lactation, and neonates. Detailed infant ages and prematurity information are missing in the returned metadata. [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15857447/)

- **Drug exposure:** The study measured maternal serum, umbilical-cord serum, breast milk, and infant serum levetiracetam concentrations. The abstract reports breast-milk/maternal-serum concentration ratios near 1 and very low infant serum concentrations.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** No malformations were detected, and the abstract reports that none of the infants developed signs of adverse effects.
- **Interpretation:** The authors described transfer into breast milk and reported that low infant serum concentrations suggested rapid elimination.

These small observational studies do not establish long-term infant outcomes or prove that levetiracetam is safe for every breastfeeding situation. Stopping or changing a medication can also carry risks and belongs in a prescribing-clinician conversation.
