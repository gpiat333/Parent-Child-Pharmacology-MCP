## Metformin: selected label versus breastfed-infant findings

### What the DailyMed label says

The retrieved source is one product label, **“METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]”**—not all metformin formulations. Its lactation section reports that animal studies found metformin in milk at levels comparable with plasma, while comparable studies in nursing mothers had not been conducted. It identifies possible hypoglycemia in nursing infants as a potential concern. This is a **label precaution**, not evidence that hypoglycemia occurred in a particular infant. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The label also includes historical “Pregnancy Category B” wording. That category is not a current FDA pregnancy classification; FDA replaced the letter-category system with the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule format. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551) [S3](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/labeling/questions-and-answers-pregnancy-and-lactation-labeling-rule)

### What LactMed reports

The LactMed record’s source population is **lactation**; the returned metadata do not establish a separate age band for the overall record. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

- **Measured exposure:** LactMed reports low metformin levels in milk and occasional low detectable levels in the serum of breastfed infants. This is measured exposure, not an observed clinical harm. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Observed infant outcomes:** In reported groups, infants were described as healthy with expected growth and development; some reports found no detectable adverse effects, and one group of young infants had no low blood-glucose findings. These were observed findings in the cited populations, not proof that adverse effects cannot occur. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Prospective mixed population:** A six-month, nonrandomized prospective study included **111 infants: 61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed**. No differences were reported between groups in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates at the reported follow-up points. Because this was a mixed breastfed/formula-fed population, the finding should not be generalized to exclusively breastfed infants. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- LactMed’s extract mentions case reports, observational studies, and a randomized trial, but the MCP record reports the overall study count as **not established from this extract**; that missing count is not zero. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### Comparison

| Topic | Label | LactMed |
|---|---|---|
| Hypoglycemia | Identifies possible infant hypoglycemia as a potential concern | Reports no low blood-glucose findings in one small group of young infants |
| Human lactation data | Says comparable nursing-mother studies were not conducted for that label | Summarizes human milk, infant-serum, and infant-outcome observations |
| Infant outcomes | Provides a warning/precaution, not an observed infant outcome | Reports no adverse findings in several described groups, with study-specific populations and limitations |
| Exposure versus outcome | Does not provide human infant exposure measurements in the retrieved label section | Separately reports measured milk/serum exposure and observed infant outcomes |

### Missing sections and limitations

- **DailyMed:** Pregnancy, lactation, and pediatric-use sections were retrieved and were not marked truncated. However, structured Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule fields for risk summary, clinical considerations, data, and a pregnancy-exposure registry were missing because the parser reported that the headings were not found. Missing structured fields do not prove that the information is absent elsewhere in the label. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- **LactMed:** The expected lactation sections were returned without truncation or omission by limit. The extract does not establish a definitive study count, and some infant ages or extent of breastfeeding were unspecified. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- The LactMed record is lactation evidence, not pregnancy evidence. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
