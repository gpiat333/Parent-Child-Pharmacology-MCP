### Effects on the pregnant person

- The selected product label’s **Clinical Considerations** discuss pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, but the returned label extract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-effect analysis. The epilepsy-related risks described in the label are disease-associated context, not observed lamotrigine adverse effects. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A review of **pregnancy and epilepsy**—source population: pregnancy; study type: review—describes the need to balance seizure control with fetal outcomes. The returned abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific maternal outcome estimates. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A review of **epilepsy during pregnancy and lactation**—source population: pregnancy and lactation; study type: review—states that pregnancy registries, cohort studies, case-control studies, and prospective cohorts are important because large randomized safety trials are unavailable in this population. Its statement that lamotrigine is “relatively safe” is a review-level summary, not a personalized assessment. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)
- A review of **bipolar disorder in pregnancy and postpartum**—source population: pregnancy; study type: narrative review—includes lamotrigine among perinatal treatments but does not provide lamotrigine-specific maternal outcome estimates in the retrieved abstract. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

**Gap:** Maternal outcomes specific to lamotrigine, including pregnancy complications and adverse effects by indication, were not clearly reported in the retrieved evidence.

### Maternal pharmacokinetics

#### Clinical Considerations

The selected DailyMed label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is source-label information for clinician discussion, not an individualized medication plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Literature

- The pregnancy-and-epilepsy review—source population: pregnancy; study type: review—states that physiological changes during pregnancy affect antiseizure-medication levels and discusses serum-concentration monitoring. The retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific quantitative pharmacokinetic results. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A review specifically covering lamotrigine pharmacokinetics in pregnancy and breastfeeding—source population: pregnancy and lactation; study type: review—describes pharmacokinetics as a topic, but the retrieved abstract does not report quantitative lamotrigine-specific results. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers**—source population: pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child; study type: primary observational study—measured medication concentrations in serum and breastmilk at multiple time points and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. The abstract reports limited knowledge about concentration changes, but it does not establish a lamotrigine-specific maternal serum trajectory in the retrieved text. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

**Interpretive limitation:** The retrieved material supports pregnancy-related concentration changes as a clinical consideration, but it does not establish exact lamotrigine clearance, concentration changes by trimester, or applicability across indications, co-medications, formulations, or individual clinical circumstances.

### Fetal exposure

- The retrieved human evidence documents **in-utero exposure cohorts**, especially first-trimester lamotrigine-monotherapy exposures, but it does not provide direct quantitative fetal lamotrigine concentrations or placental-transfer measurements. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The 60-mother observational study measured maternal serum and breastmilk concentrations; breastmilk findings concern lactation and should not be treated as fetal-exposure measurements. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- The retrieved recruiting observational study, **NCT05450978**, has source population: pregnant adults aged 18–45 with epilepsy; study type: observational; its summary concerns antiseizure-medication clearance, maternal blood concentrations, and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. The returned record lists status **RECRUITING**, but eligibility and current site status were not confirmed by this lookup. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

**Gap:** Direct fetal concentrations, placental transfer, and exposure by gestational stage were not established in the retrieved results.

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The selected label reports the following registry observations, all primarily concerning first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy exposures among pregnancies largely involving epilepsy:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: 2.2% among 1,558 exposed infants.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: 2.0% among 1,562 exposed infants.
- EURAP: 2.9% among 2,514 exposed pregnancies.

The label reports that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These observations should not be generalized to every indication, combination regimen, formulation, or pregnancy context. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Isolated oral clefts

The label reports conflicting findings:

- The NAAED registry observed isolated oral clefts in 3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 early-pregnancy exposures and described this as higher than in unexposed healthy controls.
- The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries.
- A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45, with a confidence interval that included no association.

Therefore, the retrieved evidence does not resolve whether this reported signal represents a causal lamotrigine effect. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT registry study—source population: congenital-anomaly registry records; study type: case-control study—reported no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with its control group. The record was completed, had no posted results, and its registry narrative was truncated; it should not be treated as a complete outcome report. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

#### Other fetal and child outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while noting that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother observational study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between its in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborn groups and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Because the study covered multiple psychotropic medicines and the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific estimates, this should not be treated as a standalone lamotrigine finding. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

#### Animal findings

The label reports developmental findings in mice, rats, and rabbits, including fetal-weight changes, skeletal variations, neurobehavioral findings, offspring mortality, and reduced fetal folate concentrations. These are animal findings and are not observed human fetal outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Questions for a clinician

1. Does this evidence apply to the exact lamotrigine formulation, indication, co-medications, and pregnancy stage?
2. Which findings are measured maternal concentrations, and which are fetal or infant outcomes?
3. How closely do the registry populations match this pregnancy, including gestational age and underlying condition?
4. How should the conflicting oral-cleft findings be interpreted?
5. How do monotherapy findings differ from polytherapy or interacting-medication findings?
6. What maternal, fetal, and postpartum follow-up information is relevant?
7. Which uncertainties in this evidence are most important for the treatment discussion?
