LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin in breastfed infants**:

### Observed symptoms
- **Diarrhea:** reported in 3 of 25 infants in one telephone follow-up study and in 2 of 40 exposed infants in another prospective study.
- **Rash:** reported in 1 of those 40 exposed infants.
- **Rash and somnolence:** reported in a comparison study; reactions occurred in 8.3% of amoxicillin-exposed infants, similar to the macrolide-exposed comparison group.
- **One urticaria case:** a 2-month-old infant developed recurrent hives after breastfeeding while the mother was taking amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin. The responsible drug could not be determined; LactMed judged the combination product the more likely cause.
- In a study of 25 mothers and infants older than 4 weeks, **no infant adverse effects were reported**.

### Advice to monitor—not observed symptoms
One controlled study asked mothers to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were **monitoring items**, not reported symptoms in that study; no statistical differences were found between groups taking related antibiotics and controls.

### Uncertainty
LactMed states that rash and gastrointestinal effects such as diarrhea or thrush have been reported, but they have **not been adequately evaluated**. The reports do not establish that amoxicillin caused every symptom: one involved other medicines, and the comparison studies provide context rather than proof of causation. Published information on infant amoxicillin levels was not found in the record. The evidence base is limited and heterogeneous, and the number of studies is not established from this LactMed extract; a statement that no adverse effects were reported does not prove safety or that the drug is well studied.
