## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved, but it is not a safety verdict. The retrieved human evidence is primarily observational; no large randomized pregnancy-safety trial was identified in the returned records.

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The retrieved DailyMed record is for **LAMOTRIGINE KIT (LAMOTRIGINE) KIT LAMOTRIGINE TABLET**, OWP Pharmaceuticals, Inc.; effective date **October 12, 2026**. It represents one product label, not all lamotrigine formulations. DailyMed states that epilepsy itself, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, has been associated with several adverse pregnancy outcomes, including preeclampsia, preterm labor, bleeding, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. It also states that poorly controlled convulsive seizures may increase maternal or fetal injury risk. These are **disease-associated risks in the label**, not outcomes shown to be caused by lamotrigine. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

For bipolar disorder, the returned review describes pregnancy and postpartum management in people with bipolar disorder and identifies postpartum relapse as an important disease-context concern. It is a narrative review, not a lamotrigine-specific pregnancy-outcome study. The review’s source population was **pregnancy/postpartum, bipolar disorder; study type: narrative review**. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Measured fetal or infant exposure

For pregnancy, the retrieved records describe maternal lamotrigine exposure and pregnancy outcomes but do not provide a generalizable measured fetal-concentration estimate. The label mentions pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations, but this is a clinical pharmacology consideration rather than a quantified fetal-exposure result. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The lactation section is separate from pregnancy: it concerns postnatal milk and infant exposure and should not be used as fetal-exposure evidence. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

MPRINT’s **Gold** pregnancy search returned no matching lamotrigine records in the selected snapshot. This means no records were returned by that query—not that lamotrigine lacks pregnancy evidence, is safe, or is absent from MPRINT or other literature. The snapshot is bounded and not live. Its returned coverage includes Gold study metadata and aggregate pharmacoepidemiology/clinical-trial and pharmacokinetic-summary outcome rows; no Gold outcomes were returned. Any null MPRINT outcome fields would mean **missing in the exported data**, not zero. [S6](https://github.com/langli-lab/mprint-kp-data)

### Observed fetal and child outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

- **International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry — source population: pregnant women, predominantly with epilepsy; study type: pregnancy registry.** The label reports major congenital malformations in 2.2% of 1,558 first-trimester lamotrigine-monotherapy exposures. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **NAAED Pregnancy Registry — source population: pregnant women, predominantly with epilepsy; study type: pregnancy registry.** The label reports 2.0% among 1,562 first-trimester lamotrigine-monotherapy exposures. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **EURAP — source population: pregnant women with epilepsy; study type: international pregnancy registry.** The label reports 2.9% among 2,514 first-trimester exposures. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **JAMA Neurology cohort — source population: women with epilepsy aged 14–55 years and their offspring; study type: prospective observational longitudinal cohort.** Among 3,584 lamotrigine-monotherapy pregnancies, major congenital malformations occurred in 110 pregnancies, or 3.1% (95% CI 2.5%–3.7%); offspring were followed to 1 year after birth. This is an observed prevalence and does not by itself establish causation. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38497990/)

The label states that the registry frequencies were similar to general-population estimates, but comparators and methods differed across sources. The returned label also says that several meta-analyses did not identify an increased overall major-malformation risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while noting limitations from differing outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

- **NAAED Pregnancy Registry — source population: pregnant women with early lamotrigine exposure; study type: pregnancy registry.** The label reports isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 exposures, described there as approximately three times the rate in unexposed healthy controls. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **European case-control study — source population: pregnancies represented in 21 European congenital-anomaly registries; study type: case-control study.** The label reports an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 (95% CI 0.8–2.63). [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that the NAAED finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. Therefore, this is an inconsistent signal rather than a finding replicated across the returned sources. [S4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Neurodevelopment and later child outcomes

- **UK and Swedish registry cohort — source population: children born after pregnancy exposure, including infants, children, and adolescents; study type: population-based cohort study.** Among 3,182,773 children, 17,495 had antiseizure-medication exposure during pregnancy. The abstract reported little evidence that children exposed to lamotrigine were more likely to receive neurodevelopmental diagnoses. The source assessed recorded diagnoses, not a uniform direct developmental assessment of every child. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39548057/)

The returned AAN/AES/SMFM record was a **practice guideline** concerning people with epilepsy of childbearing potential and fetal, neonatal, and child outcomes. Its abstract identifies lamotrigine among antiseizure medications considered in relation to major congenital-malformation risk, but it is a synthesis and guideline record, not a lamotrigine-specific randomized trial. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)

### Limitations

- The PubMed search returned 690 matches, but that count is not a count of distinct relevant studies, verified human outcomes, or evidence quality. The query was a prototype search, not a validated systematic-review strategy. [S5](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=10&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- The evidence is largely from people with epilepsy; applicability to lamotrigine used for bipolar disorder or other indications is less certain.
- The returned study populations were mixed or broad in some records, so findings should not be generalized beyond the stated populations.
- Confounding may involve seizure control, underlying psychiatric or neurologic disease, polytherapy, comorbidities, pregnancy characteristics, and healthcare factors.
- The DailyMed result was marked partial and the pregnancy section was marked truncated in one returned field, although key structured pregnancy subsections were present. It should not be treated as a complete review of all lamotrigine product labels.
- MPRINT coverage is a versioned snapshot. Its zero-result finding is a retrieval limitation, not an evidence-gap finding.

### Questions for the clinician or pharmacist

1. Which underlying condition and disease-related pregnancy risks are relevant in this case?
2. Are the available studies applicable to the indication, formulation, monotherapy/polytherapy status, seizure history, or psychiatric history?
3. How should pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations and clinical response be interpreted?
4. Which comparator is most informative: healthy controls, untreated people with the same condition, or another antiseizure medication?
5. How should the inconsistent oral-cleft signal and the newer child-neurodevelopment findings be weighed?
6. Would pregnancy-registry participation or consultation with a maternal-fetal-medicine, neurology, psychiatry, or clinical-pharmacy specialist add useful context?

Stopping or changing medication can also carry risks and belongs in a prescribing-clinician conversation.
