## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Scope and label context:** Lamotrigine was matched as the single ingredient. The retrieved label is **“LAMOTRIGINE KIT (LAMOTRIGINE) KIT LAMOTRIGINE TABLET [OWP PHARMACEUTICALS, INC.]”**, effective **October 12, 2026**. It is one product label, not all lamotrigine formulations; its pregnancy section was truncated in the retrieved summary. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The label states that most lamotrigine pregnancy data come from women with epilepsy. It also describes epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, as being associated with preeclampsia, preterm labor, antepartum and postpartum hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. The label states that the risk of maternal or fetal injury may be greatest with untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures. These are label statements about the underlying disease and pregnancy context, not findings that lamotrigine caused each outcome. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label reports that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, including reported decreases during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The retrieved passage does not provide a complete quantitative pharmacokinetic dataset or an individualized clinical interpretation. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Fetal or infant exposure

No numeric human fetal lamotrigine concentrations were retrieved in this pregnancy brief.

A separate study of **60 mothers** included serum and breastmilk sampling during pregnancy and lactation and followed exposed children during the first 12 months. Its abstract reported a high concentration-by-dose ratio in breastmilk for lamotrigine, but the retrieved passage did not provide a numeric lamotrigine concentration, sampling value, or fetal concentration. This is lactation-related exposure evidence and should not be treated as a measured fetal concentration. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

The label reports animal findings, including decreased fetal folate concentrations in pregnant rats and developmental-toxicity findings in animal studies. These are animal data, not measured human fetal exposure. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal or infant outcomes

The label reports the following registry findings for **lamotrigine monotherapy**:

- **International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry:** major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558** infants exposed during the first trimester; reported 95% CI, **1.6%–3.1%**.
- **NAAED Pregnancy Registry:** major congenital malformations among **2.0%** of **1,562** infants exposed during the first trimester.
- **NAAED Pregnancy Registry:** isolated oral clefts reported at **3.2 per 1,000** among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy; the label describes this as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls.

The same label section states that prospective registries and epidemiological studies had not detected an increased frequency of major congenital malformations or a consistent pattern of malformations compared with the general population. It also states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions. These statements are from the selected label and should not be treated as a definitive individual risk estimate. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The retrieved material states that analyses also evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay, but the brief did not provide complete estimates for those outcomes. A review of epilepsy in pregnancy discusses fetal outcomes in the context of both antiseizure-medication exposure and seizure control; it is a review rather than a primary outcome study. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35133310/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)
