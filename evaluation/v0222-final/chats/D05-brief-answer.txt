## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Evidence status:** Sertraline identity was matched exactly. The retrieved material is partial and is not a safety verdict. The lactation evidence should not be generalized to pregnancy or to every sertraline formulation. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json) [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Effect on the lactating person

- LactMed reports that a midwife observed **six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline**; administered amounts were not reported. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a separate case, one mother reported increased milk supply during a temporary interruption and decreased supply after restarting. The retrieved passage does not establish causation or provide the exact elapsed time between these events. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- LactMed also discusses galactorrhea and hyperprolactinemia reports, including reports in nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients. Their applicability to lactation is not established by this extract. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure in milk

The retrieved LactMed record reported the following **milk concentrations**:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L**, range **7–207 mcg/L**, in a pooled analysis involving 15 mothers. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a 24-hour milk-sampling study of 26 breastfeeding women: sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**); norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In four mothers sampled at two months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline; sampling times were random. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a case involving a preterm infant, milk levels measured on three days at random times averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One additional milk concentration was reported as **29 mcg/L**; the two sampling times were not stated. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The returned extract did **not** include an infant-level concentration excerpt. Infant-level specimen information and measurements are therefore missing from this extract; this does not establish that no infant-level measurements exist. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports one four-month-old infant with benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and agitation that resolved spontaneously. The retrieved passage reports the maternal sertraline exposure but does not establish causation. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a group of 26 breastfed infants, the source reported no detectable acute adverse reactions attributed to sertraline in breast milk. The source population and study context should be reviewed in the complete record. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of 14 mothers and their breastfed infants, whole-blood serotonin was measured after 6–16 weeks of maternal therapy. Infant serotonin averaged **227 mcg/L before** and **224 mcg/L after** maternal therapy. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These are separate study or case findings. The retrieved extract does not establish that the reported infant events were caused by sertraline, and it does not provide a complete study count. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Product-label context

The selected label was **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, DailyMed effective date **November 5, 2026**. It is one selected product label, not every sertraline formulation. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The label states that published data demonstrate low levels of sertraline and its metabolites in human milk. It reports a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs in which exclusively human-milk-fed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to 2% of maternal serum levels, with a reported range of 0%–15%; the label states that no adverse reactions were observed in those infants. The label also states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The returned label included an adverse-reactions section for the person taking the medicine. Expected sections for warnings, warnings and precautions, boxed warning, and drug interactions were missing from the returned extract; missing sections are retrieval limitations, not evidence that those sections contain no information.
