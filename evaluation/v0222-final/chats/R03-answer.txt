## Sources compared

### Adult taking metformin: DailyMed label

The retrieved label is **“METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]”**—one specific product label, not every metformin formulation. Its adult **Warnings** section states that lactic acidosis is a rare but serious complication associated with metformin accumulation and identifies renal dysfunction, older age, hypoxemia, dehydration, sepsis, hepatic disease, and certain heart-failure circumstances among associated risk factors. The same label’s adverse-reaction section reports gastrointestinal reactions, including diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, indigestion, and abdominal discomfort. ### Breastfed infants: LactMed

LactMed is a lactation-specific source. It reports measured metformin exposure and observed infant findings separately. [S15](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

#### Measured exposure

Reported **milk** concentrations included:

- **270 mcg/L** averaged over 24 hours in six women.
- About **380 mcg/L** at approximately 4–5 hours after administration in one woman.
- **130–270 mcg/L** in three mothers.
- An average of **410 mcg/L** in samples collected 4–17 days postpartum.

The study contexts and sampling times are as reported by LactMed; administered regimens are omitted here. [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436333/) [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12545145/) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15384618/) [S6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15932841/)

Infant-level information was retrieved using the source’s stated specimen terminology: **serum** levels were measured in four breastfed infants. Two were below **10 mcg/L**; the other two had levels of **50 and 80 mcg/L**. In another report, metformin was below **5 mcg/L** in the serum of two infants. Some infant ages and the extent of nursing were not stated. [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436333/) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15384618/)

#### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports that:

- Seven infants aged 5–25 months were judged healthy, with growth and development progressing as expected; two also had normal Denver Developmental Screening tests.
- Three infants had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin.
- In three breastfed infants 10–11 days postpartum, no low blood-glucose levels were reported, and mothers reported no adverse reactions.
- In a study of 111 infants—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—no differences were found at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or rates of illness between the groups.

These are the outcomes reported in the cited LactMed passages. They do not establish that the adult label warnings occurred in infants, and the absence of a reported event is not proof that it did not occur. [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436333/) [S6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15932841/) [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737874/)
