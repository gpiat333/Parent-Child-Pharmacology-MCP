LactMed reports the following about **amoxicillin exposure through breast milk**:

### Measured exposure
- In a study of 6 women, average amoxicillin concentrations in milk were **0.69 mg/L** (range **0.46–0.88 mg/L**) at 4 hours and **0.81 mg/L** (range **0.39–1.3 mg/L**) at 5 hours after administration. These are milk measurements, not infant measurements. [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7246123/)
- **Infant levels:** Relevant published information was **not found in the retrieved LactMed record** as of its revision date. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Observed infant symptoms
- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, **3 reported diarrhea** in their infants. No rash or candidiasis was reported in that study. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8498418/)
- In a prospective study of 40 infants exposed to amoxicillin in breast milk, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16044108/)
- In a study comparing amoxicillin-exposed infants with infants exposed to a macrolide antibiotic, reported reactions occurred in **8.3%** of the amoxicillin-exposed infants and included **rash and somnolence**. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19366316/)
- In one case, an infant developed generalized urticaria after breastfeeding while the mother was receiving **amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin**. The source considered the antibiotics in milk a probable cause but could not determine which drug was responsible; it considered amoxicillin/clavulanate the more likely cause. [S2](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1472-8206.2009.00689.x)
- In another report, an 11-month-old experienced **mild diarrhea** while the mother was taking amoxicillin along with acetaminophen and codeine; breastfeeding extent was unspecified, and the reaction was rated possible or probable. [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40183105/)
- In a study of 25 mothers whose infants were older than 4 weeks, **no infant adverse effects were reported**. [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41582095/)

### Advice to monitor
The retrieved LactMed text does **not** provide a separate explicit recommendation to caregivers to monitor for symptoms. One study asked mothers to record possible effects—including feeding difficulty, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change and jaundice—but those were study observations, not necessarily LactMed advice. No statistical differences were found for those recorded parameters compared with infants of mothers taking related antibiotics. [S1](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.1991.tb05523.x)
