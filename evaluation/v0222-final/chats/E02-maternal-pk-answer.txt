### Observed fetal outcomes

The selected label reports the following registry findings for **infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy in the first trimester or early pregnancy**:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in 2.2% of 1,558 infants; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations among 2.0% of 1,562 infants.
- The same registry reported isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions that can be drawn. Most lamotrigine pregnancy data were from women with epilepsy. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The retrieved extract states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small for gestational age, and neurodevelopmental delay, but it does not provide the corresponding lamotrigine-specific results. Those results therefore remain unestablished by this extract. [S9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The PubMed abstract describing 60 mothers also states that development of exposed children was observed during the first 12 months, but the retrieved passage does not provide lamotrigine-specific developmental results. The source population was mixed: pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
