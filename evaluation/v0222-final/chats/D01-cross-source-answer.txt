## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured exposure

LactMed reports these **breast-milk concentrations**:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers, sertraline averaged **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**). [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/)
- In a 24-hour sampling study of 26 women, sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). [S6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In four mothers at two months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline; samples were collected at random times. [S48](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In one case involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline across three randomly timed sampling days. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- Foremilk and hindmilk samples collected 13 hours after administration contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively. [S48](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In one study, sertraline was undetectable in milk at **<5 mcg/L** in one participant; concentrations in eight other participants ranged from **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**. [S48](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In six mothers, random milk samples had a median sertraline concentration of **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**); LactMed notes that sampling timing might affect these values. [S48](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In 15 mothers, the average milk concentration was **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**). [S48](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

**Infant-level measurements:** LactMed reports infant **serum** measurements. In one study of 30 breastfed infants aged 6–13 weeks, 22 had sertraline serum levels below **1 mcg/L**; eight had detectable levels averaging **7.9 mcg/L**. In a separate report of three infants, sertraline and norsertraline were either undetectable or present below the reported assay thresholds. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11483479/)[S44](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9108810/)

These are measured research exposures, not dosing instructions or evidence that an individual infant will have the same exposure.

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports:

- No detectable acute adverse reactions in a study of 26 infants. [S6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- No clinically observed adverse reactions in a study of six infants and no adverse effects in a report of seven infants. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367050/)[S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367055/)
- Normal reported six-month weight gain and no abnormal effects reported by mothers in one group of 25 infants. [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- One report of benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation in one infant. [S44](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9108810/)
- A retrospective study of 30 nursing mothers reported adverse effects in five infants; reported events included insomnia, restlessness, constant crying, and poor feeding. The returned LactMed extract does not establish causation. [S48](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A case report involving a preterm infant described hyperthermia, altered muscle tone, and high-pitched crying, with measurable infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite. LactMed reports that the authors attributed the presentation to serotonergic overstimulation in that case. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- A study of infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy and through breastfeeding reported blunted pain responses; four of the 30 infants were exposed to sertraline. The source reported that maternal depression could not be ruled out as an explanation. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)

Some reports combine prenatal and breastfeeding exposure, so those findings do not isolate breastfeeding exposure alone.

### Product-label evidence

The selected label is **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective November 5, 2026. Its lactation section states that published literature shows low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk. It cites a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs in which exclusively human-milk-fed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to **2%** of maternal serum levels, with a range of **0%–15%**; no adverse reactions were observed in those infants. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The label also states that there are **no data on effects on milk production**. Its adult adverse-reaction section lists, among other warnings, serotonin syndrome, increased bleeding, mania or hypomania, discontinuation syndrome, seizures, hyponatremia, sexual dysfunction, and suicidal thoughts or behaviors. The detailed boxed-warning, warnings-and-precautions, and interaction sections were missing from the retrieved extract, so this is not a complete adult-label review. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Evidence limitations

The LactMed record is lactation-specific and does not substitute for pregnancy evidence. [S48](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Questions to ask a clinician or pharmacist

- Which findings apply to breastfeeding exposure alone, and which also involve prenatal exposure?
- What infant feeding, growth, or clinical changes should be discussed with the infant’s clinician?
- Does the retrieved label apply to the exact manufacturer and formulation being used?
- How should maternal treatment needs and breastfeeding goals be considered together?
