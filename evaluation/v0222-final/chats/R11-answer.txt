**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926, 2022) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design
The abstract does not state a formal design label. It reports that breastfeeding women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam completed a questionnaire. Researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples collected from before administration through 12 hours afterward and in one pre-administration maternal serum sample. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Population
The study included 20 breastfeeding women and 21 infants: one woman had twins, 16 infants were fully breastfed, and 5 were partially breastfed. The abstract does not provide infant ages, gestational ages, or prematurity status. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Drug exposure
The abstract reports a trough breast-milk/maternal-serum concentration ratio of **0.98 ± 0.20**. It also reports exposure estimates and relative infant dose values separately for fully and partially breastfed infants, and describes correlations among levetiracetam dose, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk area-under-the-curve values. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Observed infant outcomes
Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that this resolved after switching to partial breastfeeding. It also states that all infants gained weight according to age. The abstract reports the authors’ interpretation that infant exposure was close to specified safety thresholds and that further studies were needed to assess short- and long-term safety. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
