## Sertraline during lactation

Sertraline identity was confirmed as the single ingredient sertraline. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects on the lactating person

LactMed reports:

- A midwife observed **6 patients** who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; administered amounts were not reported.
- In one separately described case, the mother reported increased milk supply during a one-week period when she stopped sertraline, followed by decreased supply after restarting. The retrieved passage does not establish whether other factors contributed or provide a complete chronology beyond those stated relationships.
- Galactorrhea has been reported in nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients. A French pharmacovigilance-center analysis did not find an increased hyperprolactinemia signal compared with other drugs.

These are maternal or lactation observations, not infant outcomes. The selected DailyMed label states that there are no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production. The selected product was **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, with an effective date of November 5, 2026; it is one product label, not all sertraline formulations. Its retrieved warning entry is titled **“8.2 Lactation,” code 42229-5**, with a source title/code mismatch noted in the retrieval. ### Measured exposure in milk

Retrieved LactMed milk measurements included:

- In a pooled analysis of **15 mothers**, mean milk sertraline concentration was **45 mcg/L**, with a range of **7 to 207 mcg/L**.
- In a study of **26 breastfeeding women**, milk sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** with a range of **11 to 938 mcg/L**; norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** with a range of **20 to 1,498 mcg/L**. The women were, on average, 15.8 weeks postpartum, with sampling over 24 hours.
- In **4 mothers** at about 2 months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- In a report involving a mother of a preterm infant, milk levels averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline; sampling times were random.
- One report gave a sertraline milk concentration of **29 mcg/L** at two times that were not stated.

No infant-level concentration excerpt was retrieved, and the source does not establish an infant specimen type in the returned measurement extract. The LactMed Drug Levels section was truncated; these are not a complete set of retrieved measurements. Milk concentrations are exposure measurements, not clinical outcomes and not proof of safety. [S55](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports the following separate observations:

- In one case, a **4-month-old infant** had benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and agitation that spontaneously resolved while the mother was taking sertraline. The report does not establish causation.
- In a separate group of **26 infants**, with mean age **16.6 weeks** and range **4–28 weeks**, no acute adverse reactions attributed to sertraline in breast milk were detected in the reported assessment.
- In a study of **14 mothers and their breastfed infants**, infant whole-blood serotonin averaged **227 mcg/L before** and **224 mcg/L after** maternal therapy. This was a laboratory measurement rather than a reported developmental or clinical outcome.
- The selected DailyMed label reports a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which exclusively human-milk-fed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to **2%** of maternal serum levels, with a range of **0%–15%**; the label reports that no adverse reactions were observed in those infants.

The LactMed infant-outcomes section was truncated. The retrieved extract does not establish a complete study count, and some included reports had missing information about maternal exposure, extent of breastfeeding, or infant age.
