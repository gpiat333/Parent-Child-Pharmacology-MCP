## Pregnancy evidence brief: lamotrigine

**Identity:** Lamotrigine was matched exactly as the ingredient lamotrigine. [S22](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)  
**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved, but it is not a safety verdict; missing evidence is not evidence of safety. The search was bounded rather than systematic. The retrieved label was for one lamotrigine tablet product, not all formulations. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Effects on the pregnant person

- The selected label describes pregnancy-related risks associated with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-drug exposure, including obstetric complications and risks associated with poorly controlled convulsive seizures. These are **disease-associated or broader treatment-context statements**, not evidence that lamotrigine itself caused each outcome. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- Reviews describe balancing maternal seizure control and fetal outcomes. The populations were **pregnant people with epilepsy**, and the records were reviews rather than primary outcome studies. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35133310/) [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)
- For bipolar disorder, a 2025 narrative review addressed **pregnant and postpartum women with bipolar disorder** and discussed lamotrigine among perinatal treatments; the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific outcome estimates. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Maternal pharmacokinetics

#### Clinical Considerations

The label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. These are source-reported concentration patterns, not a prediction for an individual. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Literature

- A review of **pregnancy in people with epilepsy** states that physiological changes can affect antiseizure-medication levels and seizure risk. This is a review-level statement; the retrieved abstract does not provide quantitative lamotrigine pharmacokinetic results. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A 2021 review of **pregnant and breastfeeding people exposed to phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, levetiracetam, lamotrigine, or valproate** covered pharmacokinetics, but its retrieved abstract reports no quantitative lamotrigine pharmacokinetic results. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers receiving psychopharmacological medicines during pregnancy and lactation** measured serum and breastmilk concentrations and followed children for 12 months. Its abstract reports a lamotrigine breastmilk concentration finding, but does not provide a quantitative maternal pregnancy pharmacokinetic profile for lamotrigine. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

**Gap:** The retrieved evidence supports a pregnancy-related change in lamotrigine concentrations in the label, but does not establish a complete quantitative profile by trimester, indication, formulation, or co-medication.

### Fetal exposure

- No quantitative human fetal lamotrigine concentration or fetal-to-maternal concentration ratio was reported in the retrieved label passages or abstracts.
- The retrieved 60-mother study concerns maternal serum and breastmilk measurements; its lamotrigine exposure finding is primarily relevant to lactation, not a measured fetal exposure result. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- The label reports reduced fetal folate concentrations in pregnant rats. This is **animal evidence**, not a measured human fetal lamotrigine concentration or demonstrated human fetal outcome. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A recruiting observational study, **NCT05450978**, describes pregnant adults aged 18–45 with epilepsy receiving lamotrigine, levetiracetam, or oxcarbazepine and studying medication clearance and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. It is a **human observational study**, and the returned record provides a study description, not results. The study’s returned location is the University of Pittsburgh, listed as recruiting; individual eligibility was not confirmed. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978) [S3](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.term=lamotrigine+AND+%28pregnancy%29&filter.overallStatus=RECRUITING&pageSize=20&format=json)

### Fetal outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The label summarizes registry findings for **infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy in the first trimester**, primarily among pregnancies in people with epilepsy:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: 1,558 exposed infants; major congenital malformations were reported in 2.2%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: 1,562 exposed infants; major congenital malformations were reported in 2.0%.
- EURAP: 2,514 first-trimester exposures; major birth defects were reported in 2.9%.

The label states that overall frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These are registry observations in the stated populations and exposure groups, not individual risk predictions. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

- The NAAED registry, involving 2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy, reported isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 and described this as higher than in unexposed healthy controls.
- The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries.
- The cited European case-control analysis also did not establish a statistically clear association because its confidence interval included no association. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The completed EUROCAT study was a **case-control study of congenital-anomaly registry data**, comparing first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy exposure with other congenital-anomaly groups; its returned summary states that it found no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts. The study did not use nonmalformed infants as its primary comparison group. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

Thus, the oral-cleft signal is inconsistent across the returned sources.

#### Other fetal and child outcomes

The label reports that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It states that available data did not suggest increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while also noting limitations from differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers receiving various psychopharmacological medicines**, with exposed and nonexposed newborn comparisons and child follow-up through 12 months, reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or early development. Because the study included multiple medicines and the retrieved abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-only analysis, this result should not be generalized as a lamotrigine-specific estimate. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Gaps and limitations

- Most registry evidence in the selected label comes from people with epilepsy; evidence for lamotrigine used for bipolar disorder is less directly characterized. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4) [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)
- Human fetal lamotrigine concentrations were not reported in the retrieved material.
- The retrieved pharmacokinetic literature does not provide a complete quantitative maternal profile across pregnancy.
- Oral-cleft findings differ between registries and study designs.
- Long-term neurodevelopmental evidence is limited in the retrieved material, and the label notes differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- FAERS results are reporting counts, not incidence or risk; they lack exposure denominators and do not establish causation. The returned query concerned pregnancy reports and was not a complete exposed population. [S2](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22lamotrigine%22+AND+patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt%3A%22foetal+exposure+during+pregnancy%22&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=8) [S13](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)
- The MCP did not identify a curated federal pregnancy counterpart to LactMed in this source review. That is a limitation of this lookup, not proof that no other pregnancy resource exists. LactMed is lactation-focused. [S25](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/labeling/questions-and-answers-pregnancy-and-lactation-labeling-rule) [S26](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/)

### Questions for a clinician or pharmacist

1. Which evidence best matches the indication—epilepsy, bipolar disorder, or another condition?
2. Does the evidence apply to the exact formulation and all other medicines being taken?
3. What maternal concentration or clinical-response information, if any, should be followed during pregnancy and after delivery?
4. How should the inconsistent oral-cleft findings be interpreted for the relevant exposure period and indication?
5. Which fetal assessments are appropriate for the pregnancy context?
6. How should maternal disease risks and fetal-outcome evidence be weighed together?
7. Would participation in the North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry be appropriate? The FDA directory lists it as ongoing, but enrollment and eligibility should be verified directly with the registry. [S23](https://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org/join-our-study/) [S24](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/pregnancy-exposure-registries/list-pregnancy-exposure-registries)
