## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Identity:** Sertraline was matched exactly as the single ingredient; combination-product safety and interactions were not assessed. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effect on the lactating person

- No direct maternal clinical-outcome data were identified in the retrieved lactation evidence.
- The selected DailyMed product label—**SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**—states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. This is one product label, not all sertraline formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- The retrieved human studies mainly measured drug concentrations in milk and infant serum; those measurements should not be interpreted as maternal clinical outcomes. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)[S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured infant exposure

- LactMed reports that sertraline and its metabolite norsertraline can be measured in breast milk, while infant serum sertraline is often undetectable and norsertraline may be detectable at low levels. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A human study of 12 women measured sertraline and norsertraline in milk; concentrations varied according to the milk sample, collection timing, and maternal exposure. Detectable concentrations were found in some nursing infants. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- The DailyMed label cites a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs in which exclusively human-milk-fed infants had an average of 2% of maternal sertraline serum concentrations, with a reported range of 0%–15%. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- Modeled exposure estimates in LactMed are predictions, not measured exposure in those simulated infants. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- Several small observational reports described no observed acute adverse reactions among their sampled breastfed infants. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)[S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed reports one infant with benign sleep myoclonus and self-resolving agitation. The retrieved record does not establish that sertraline caused these events. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small follow-up report described normal six-month weight gain and no abnormal effects as reported by mothers. This was a limited observational population, not evidence applicable to every breastfed infant. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study of infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy and/or through breast milk reported blunted pain responses; only four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline, and the comparison group differed in maternal depression status. The reported association therefore cannot be attributed specifically to sertraline exposure through milk. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled survey of mothers taking antidepressants reported infant discontinuation symptoms, but the result was not specific to sertraline and cannot establish incidence or causation for sertraline. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One sedation report involved sertraline together with other medicines, including sedating medicines; attribution to sertraline alone is not established. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Important evidence limitations

- LactMed was retrieved, but its exported text was **truncated** during a preterm-infant case; outcome information after the truncation is missing in the retrieved record. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The evidence includes small observational studies, pooled cases, case reports, and modeled exposure estimates rather than randomized lactation-safety trials. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)[S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Study populations varied in infant age, prematurity, extent of breastfeeding, maternal condition, co-medications, and whether exposure also occurred during pregnancy. Findings should not be generalized beyond the reported populations.
- Some source records did not report infant age, extent of breastfeeding, sampling times, or comparator details. Missing details do not establish safety or absence of evidence.
- The PubMed retrieval was a bounded relevance search displaying five abstracts from a larger result count; it was not a systematic review. [S8](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22sertraline%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Milk%2C+Human%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+lactat%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfed%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- FAERS results were not specific to lactation or infants. They represent reporting volume, not incidence, risk, or causation, and have no exposure denominator. [S1](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22sertraline%22&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=8)[S10](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)
- The MCP-generated pregnancy coverage notice states that its prototype review did not identify a curated federal pregnancy counterpart to LactMed. Pregnancy information should not be substituted for lactation evidence. [S18](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/labeling/questions-and-answers-pregnancy-and-lactation-labeling-rule)[S20](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/)

### Questions to ask your clinician or pharmacist

1. Does the evidence apply to my exact sertraline formulation and all active ingredients?
2. How closely do these study populations match my infant’s age, feeding pattern, gestational age, and prematurity status?
3. Which findings are measured milk or infant-serum concentrations, and which are modeled estimates?
4. Could any reported infant observation be related to pregnancy exposure, prematurity, illness, or other medicines?
5. What follow-up information about feeding, growth, and development is relevant in my situation?
6. How should concerns about milk production be evaluated given the label’s statement that direct data are unavailable?
7. Which uncertainties in this evidence are most important for my circumstances?

The registry describes pharmacokinetic/exposure research—transfer into breast milk and estimated infant exposure—not treatment benefit. Recruitment and eligibility were not confirmed by this lookup.
