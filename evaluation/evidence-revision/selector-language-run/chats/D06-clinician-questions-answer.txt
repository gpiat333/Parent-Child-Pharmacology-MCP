## Questions to ask your clinician about sertraline during breastfeeding

### Applying the evidence
- Does the evidence apply to my exact sertraline product, formulation, and other active ingredients?
- How closely do the studies match my baby’s age, feeding pattern, gestational age, prematurity, and health history?
- Which studies involved breastfeeding exposure alone, and which also involved exposure during pregnancy?
- What important limitations or gaps should affect how we interpret this evidence?

### Infant exposure
- What do the breastmilk and infant-serum measurements show about exposure?
- How should I interpret findings that sertraline was often undetectable in infant serum while its metabolite, norsertraline, was sometimes detectable?
- Are infant blood tests or drug-level monitoring indicated in my circumstances?
- How do milk-sampling timing and sampling method affect interpretation of the reported exposure measurements?

### Observed infant outcomes
- Which findings are actual observed infant outcomes, rather than recommendations to monitor?
- How should the reported isolated cases of agitation or sleep myoclonus be interpreted?
- What is known about longer-term growth and development?
- How should evidence from studies involving other SSRIs, prenatal exposure, or multiple medicines be distinguished from sertraline exposure through breastmilk alone?
- If my baby was premature or has a medical condition, does that change how the evidence applies?

### Breastfeeding and maternal considerations
- What evidence is available about sertraline’s effects on milk production? The retrieved product label states that published data show low levels in human milk but that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- How should my underlying condition, other medicines, or other factors be considered alongside infant exposure?
- Would a pharmacist or lactation specialist add useful evidence review?

### Follow-up
- What information about feeding, growth, sleep, or behavior should I track for discussion?
- What follow-up would allow us to consider both my health and my baby’s development?
- Which clinician should coordinate questions among my prescriber, my baby’s clinician, and a lactation professional?

## Evidence points to discuss

- LactMed summarizes studies measuring sertraline and norsertraline in breastmilk and infant serum; results varied with infant age, breastfeeding extent, sampling timing, and maternal treatment context. LactMed is a lactation-only resource. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a small human study of 12 breastfeeding women and 11 mother–infant pairs, sertraline and its metabolite were detected in milk; some infant serum samples contained detectable drug or metabolite, and the abstract reported no observed adverse effects in those infants. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- Reported infant outcomes include several small groups with no observed acute adverse reactions, alongside isolated reports of agitation or sleep myoclonus. These observations do not establish that sertraline caused the events. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The retrieved LactMed text was truncated during the lookup, so some later outcome details are missing in the exported result. This evidence brief is a bounded search and not a systematic review. [S8](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22sertraline%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Milk%2C+Human%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+lactat%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfed%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)[S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
