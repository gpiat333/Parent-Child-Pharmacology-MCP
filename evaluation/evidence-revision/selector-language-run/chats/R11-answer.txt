**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926) [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

- **Study design:** Observational pharmacokinetic study. Researchers collected serial breastmilk samples over a 12-hour period, a single pre-dose maternal serum sample, and questionnaire data.
- **Population:** 20 breastfeeding women with epilepsy and 21 infants; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five partially breastfed. Infant age, gestational age, and prematurity were missing from the retrieved abstract.
- **Measured drug exposure:** The abstract reports levetiracetam concentrations in breastmilk and maternal serum, a trough breastmilk/serum ratio of 0.98 ± 0.20, calculated infant exposure estimates, and relative infant dose estimates for fully versus partially breastfed infants. It also reports correlations among maternal treatment exposure, maternal serum concentrations, breastmilk concentrations, and breastmilk area-under-the-curve values.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding. All infants gained weight according to age.
- **Causal interpretation:** The authors stated that the somnolence reports could be related to levetiracetam exposure through breastmilk. This observational study does not establish causation or long-term safety.
