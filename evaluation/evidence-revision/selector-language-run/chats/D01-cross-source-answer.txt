## Sertraline during breastfeeding

**Evidence status:** LactMed evidence and one DailyMed product label were retrieved. The DailyMed entry is for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, but the lookup did not confirm that it matches the user’s exact product or formulation. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured milk and infant exposure — LactMed

LactMed reports measurable sertraline and its metabolite **norsertraline** in breast milk in several studies. Estimated infant exposure varied across studies because of differences in milk-sampling methods, postpartum timing, breastfeeding extent, and study populations. The retrieved evidence generally reported low estimated relative infant exposure, but these measurements are not clinical outcomes. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

In infant-serum studies, sertraline was often below the assay’s detection limit; norsertraline was sometimes detectable at low levels. One reported case involved a **33-week preterm infant** with measurable sertraline and norsertraline in serum and symptoms described as similar to neonatal abstinence. The retrieved LactMed text does not provide the complete case report, so this finding should not be generalized beyond that reported preterm-infant case. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The LactMed record contains historical maternal treatment amounts in its study summaries. Those are study observations, not dosing instructions.

### Observed infant outcomes — LactMed

Among the returned LactMed summaries:

- Several small groups of breastfed infants had no observed acute adverse effects during the study period. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One report described benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation in an infant. The retrieved text does not establish that sertraline caused these findings. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One follow-up group had reported normal infant weight gain and no abnormal effects; the available summary does not establish that this applies to all breastfed infants. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study of infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy, with or without breastfeeding exposure, reported blunted pain responses. Because the comparison group did not include depressed, untreated mothers, the study could not isolate the effects of medication exposure from effects related to maternal depression. Four of the exposed infants received sertraline. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- LactMed states that no adverse developmental effects were found in infants followed up to 5 years in the cited evidence. The retrieved record does not provide enough detail to assess every study’s design or population. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The returned LactMed text is **truncated** near the end of the infant-outcomes section, so additional details may be missing. Missing text is not evidence of safety or harm.

### Product-label evidence

The selected DailyMed label lists adverse reactions and warnings for the **person taking sertraline**, including hypersensitivity reactions, QTc prolongation and ventricular arrhythmias in the specified interaction context, suicidal thoughts and behaviors, serotonin syndrome, increased bleeding risk, activation of mania or hypomania, discontinuation syndrome, seizures, angle-closure glaucoma, hyponatremia, and sexual dysfunction. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

These label findings concern the medicine-taker population. They are not automatically observed effects in a breastfed infant, and the retrieved label section did not provide a separate infant-outcome finding.

### Questions to ask a clinician

- Does this evidence apply to my exact sertraline product and formulation?
- How closely do these studies match the infant’s age, feeding pattern, and prematurity status, if relevant?
- Which findings are measured drug exposure, and which are observed clinical outcomes?
- Could other medicines, supplements, or medical conditions affect interpretation of the infant’s exposure or symptoms?
- What follow-up information would be relevant for the lactating parent and infant?
- What uncertainties and limitations in the available evidence should be considered in an individualized discussion?
