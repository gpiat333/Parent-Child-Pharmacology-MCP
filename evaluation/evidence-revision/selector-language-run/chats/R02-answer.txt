**Measured in milk (exposure evidence)**

- In one study of five breastfeeding women, the average peak milk concentration was **87.2 mcg/L**, occurring about **2.5 hours** after administration; the mean milk half-life was **2.2 hours**.
- In another study of seven breastfeeding women using different formulations, peak milk concentrations averaged **59.3 mcg/L**, occurring **2–4 hours** after administration. The average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L**, and the milk half-life averaged **4.9 hours**.
- Published **infant blood-level measurements were not found** in the LactMed record.

These are measurements of drug exposure in milk—not evidence of an infant’s clinical response. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

**Actually observed in infants (clinical-outcome evidence)**

- LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence stating that, among **three infants** whose breastfeeding mothers used sumatriptan, **none had reported adverse effects**. The dose and route were unspecified.
- No other infant clinical outcomes were reported in the retrieved LactMed record. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Thus, milk concentrations and estimated infant intake describe **exposure**, whereas reported infant effects describe **clinical outcomes**. The two should not be treated as interchangeable: detecting drug in milk does not establish harm, and no reported adverse effects in three infants is limited evidence—not proof of safety or absence of risk. The retrieved evidence is not a safety verdict.
