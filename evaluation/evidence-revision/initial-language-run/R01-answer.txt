LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin** in breastfed infants:

### Observed symptoms
- **Telephone follow-up:** Among 25 nursing mothers, 3 reported infant diarrhea; no rash or candidiasis was reported. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- **Prospective information-service study:** Among 40 infants exposed through breast milk, 2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- **Comparison study:** Reported adverse reactions occurred in 8.3% of amoxicillin-exposed infants, similar to the macrolide-exposed group; reactions included rash and sleepiness. The returned record does not provide the study population’s age range. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- **Case report:** A 2-month-old developed generalized hives about 20 minutes after breastfeeding began, and the hives recurred after a later breastfeeding episode. The mother was receiving amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin, so the specific causative drug could not be identified; LactMed considered the antibiotics in breast milk the probable cause, with amoxicillin/clavulanate considered most likely. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- **Study of 25 mothers with infants older than 4 weeks:** No infant adverse effects were reported. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Advice to monitor—not observed symptoms
One controlled prospective study asked mothers to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were **monitoring items**, not evidence that those symptoms occurred. The study compared infants of mothers taking related antibiotics—ampicillin or ampicillin/clavulanate—with control infants and found no statistical differences in those measures. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Uncertainty
LactMed states that reports of rash, diarrhea, or thrush have **not been adequately evaluated**. The evidence is based on small observational studies, parent reports, and case reports; some reports involved other medicines or related antibiotics. Infant blood-level information was not found in the record. Thus, these reports show possible associations, not proof that amoxicillin caused each symptom, and they do not establish that every breastfed infant will experience them. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
