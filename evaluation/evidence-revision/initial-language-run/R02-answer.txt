**Milk measurements (exposure evidence):**
- In 5 breastfeeding women, the average peak sumatriptan concentration in milk was **87.2 mcg/L**.
- In another group of 7 women using different formulations, average peak milk concentration was **59.3 mcg/L**; the estimated relative infant dose averaged **0.7%** (range **0.2–1.8%**).
- **Sumatriptan concentrations in infants’ blood were not reported:** LactMed states that relevant published infant-level information was not found. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

**Observed infant outcomes (clinical observation):**
- A manufacturer-correspondence report described **3 women** who used sumatriptan while breastfeeding; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The exposure details were unspecified. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

These are different evidence types: milk concentrations and calculated relative infant doses estimate **potential infant exposure**; they do not measure an infant’s clinical response. The report of no reported adverse effects is an **observational outcome report**, but it involved only 3 infants, lacked specified exposure details, and did not include infant blood-level measurements. It therefore does not establish that adverse effects cannot occur. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
