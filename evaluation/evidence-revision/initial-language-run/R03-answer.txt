## Metformin: label information vs. breastfed-infant findings

### DailyMed label

The retrieved label is **“METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]”**, representing one product label rather than all metformin formulations. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

- For lactation, the label reports animal findings that metformin enters milk and says comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers.
- It identifies a **potential for hypoglycemia in nursing infants**. This is a label warning about potential risk, not an observed infant outcome reported by the label.
- The retrieved lactation section does **not identify a specific adverse effect in the adult taking metformin**.

The label also contains a historical “Pregnancy Category B” statement. FDA’s Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule removed the letter-category system from current labeling; that historical category should not be treated as a current classification. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551) [S3](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/labeling/questions-and-answers-pregnancy-and-lactation-labeling-rule)

### LactMed findings in breastfed infants

LactMed is a lactation-focused source. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

**Measured exposure**
- Human studies generally found low metformin concentrations in milk.
- Metformin was sometimes detectable at low levels in infant serum, while it was undetectable in other tested infants.
- Some infant ages, extent of nursing, and exposure timing were not stated in the underlying reports.

**Observed infant outcomes**
- **Seven infants aged 5–25 months:** reported as healthy, with expected growth and development; two also had normal Denver developmental screening results.
- **Three infants aged 2, 5, and 14 months:** no detectable metformin-related adverse effects were reported.
- **Three infants aged 10–11 days postpartum:** none had low blood glucose, and mothers reported no infant adverse reactions.
- **Prospective, nonrandomized study of 111 infants:** 61 were predominantly breastfed and 50 were formula-fed. At 3 and 6 months, blinded observers found no differences between groups in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates. This was a mixed breastfed/formula-fed population, so the finding should not be generalized to exclusively breastfed infants.

LactMed also includes a caution concerning newborn or premature infants and infants with renal impairment. That is a source recommendation, not evidence that those infants experienced harm. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### What differs between the sources

| Topic | DailyMed label | LactMed |
|---|---|---|
| Milk transfer | Animal lactation studies reported transfer into milk; comparable nursing-mother studies were described as unavailable in this label | Human studies generally found low milk concentrations |
| Infant hypoglycemia | Identifies potential hypoglycemia as a concern | In one small group of newborns, no low blood glucose was observed |
| Observed infant effects | No human infant outcome data were reported in the retrieved lactation section | Several small reports and one 111-infant prospective study reported no detected adverse developmental, growth, illness, or glucose findings as described above |
| Adult effects | No specific lactation-related adverse effect in the adult was identified in the retrieved section | The retrieved LactMed material primarily reports infant exposure and outcomes, not a specific lactation-related adult adverse effect |

### Missing or limited information

- The selected DailyMed label’s extracted Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule fields—risk summary, clinical considerations, data, and pregnancy-exposure-registry information—were **missing in the exported data because the expected headings were not found**. This does not prove that no information exists elsewhere.
- DailyMed’s result covers one product label, not every metformin formulation.
- LactMed reports that some underlying study details were unspecified, including certain infant ages, extent of nursing, postpartum timing, and exposure timing.
- The retrieved evidence does not establish a safety verdict for every breastfed infant or every metformin product.
