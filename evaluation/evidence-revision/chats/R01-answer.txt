LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin**:

**Observed symptoms**
- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, **3 infants had diarrhea**; no rash or candidiasis was reported. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- In a prospective study of 40 infants exposed through breast milk, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- In a study comparing amoxicillin-exposed infants with infants exposed to a macrolide, reported reactions occurred in **8.3%** of the amoxicillin-exposed infants and included **rash and somnolence**. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- One case involved **recurrent generalized urticaria (hives)** after breastfeeding. However, the mother had received amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin, so the specific causative drug could not be determined. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- Another report described **mild diarrhea** in an 11-month-old whose mother was taking amoxicillin and acetaminophen plus codeine; the extent of breastfeeding was unspecified. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
- In a study of 25 mothers whose infants were older than 4 weeks, **no infant adverse effects were reported**. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

**Advice to monitor—not observed symptoms**
LactMed describes a controlled study in which mothers monitored for tongue changes, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were **items monitored for**, not reported symptoms in that study; no statistical differences were found between the study groups. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

**Uncertainty**
LactMed states that reports of rash and gastrointestinal effects such as diarrhea or thrush have **not been adequately evaluated**. The evidence includes small observational studies and case reports, and some reports involved comparator antibiotics or other maternal medicines. Thus, the symptoms were observed after exposure, but the reports do not establish that amoxicillin caused each event. LactMed also states that relevant published information on infant drug levels was not found as of its revision date. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
