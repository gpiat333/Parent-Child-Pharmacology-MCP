## Questions to ask your clinician

The evidence brief identified **sertraline** specifically; it did not establish a personalized safety decision. The LactMed text retrieved for this brief was truncated, so some outcome information is incomplete. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json) [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Applying the evidence
- Does the evidence apply to my exact sertraline product, formulation, and other active ingredients?
- How closely do the studies match my baby’s age, feeding pattern, gestational age, prematurity, and health status?
- Which studies involved breastfeeding exposure alone versus exposure during both pregnancy and breastfeeding?
- What important evidence gaps or uncertainties should we consider?

### Infant exposure
- Which findings are measured sertraline or norsertraline levels in breast milk or infant serum, and which are modeled estimates?
- How should the variability in milk levels be interpreted, given differences in sampling time and foremilk versus hindmilk?
- What does the evidence show about the frequency and extent of detectable infant exposure?
- Would my baby’s prematurity, medical conditions, or other medicines affect interpretation of the exposure evidence?

### Observed infant outcomes
- Which infant findings were actually observed in sertraline-exposed breastfed infants, and which statements are recommendations rather than observed outcomes?
- How should the isolated reports of agitation, sleep myoclonus, or symptoms in a preterm infant be interpreted?
- What evidence is available about growth and development, and how long were infants followed?
- Which findings may reflect prenatal exposure, the underlying maternal condition, or other medicines rather than breastfeeding exposure alone?

### Follow-up and monitoring
- What follow-up would you recommend for my baby based on the evidence and the baby’s circumstances?
- What findings would warrant contacting the pediatrician or pharmacist?
- Is routine infant blood testing used in situations like mine, or is it generally reserved for particular clinical concerns?
- Could my other medicines or supplements alter the interpretation of the evidence?

### Scope limitation
- LactMed is a lactation resource, not a pregnancy evidence substitute. The MCP review did not identify a curated federal pregnancy counterpart to LactMed; pregnancy information in this brief came from product labeling and primary literature, and the coverage was not exhaustive.
