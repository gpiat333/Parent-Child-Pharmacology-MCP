## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured milk and infant exposure

LactMed reports that sertraline and its metabolite norsertraline were measured in breast milk. Across the summarized studies, calculated infant exposure was generally low, although estimates varied between studies. Infant blood testing usually found sertraline undetectable or at low concentrations; norsertraline was detected more often. These are **exposure measurements**, not clinical outcomes. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

A case involving a **preterm infant** reported measurable sertraline and norsertraline in the infant’s serum. The retrieved LactMed narrative is truncated during the subsequent case description, so the full chronology and outcome cannot be summarized reliably. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

In the LactMed populations described:

- Several small groups reported no acute adverse effects during observation. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One follow-up group reported normal infant weight gain and no abnormal effects reported by mothers. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One report described benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study of infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy alone or during pregnancy and breastfeeding reported blunted pain responses; only four infants were exposed to sertraline, and the study lacked a comparison group of depressed, untreated mothers, so the contribution of maternal illness could not be separated from exposure. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One report of sedation involved sertraline together with other medicines, so the finding cannot be attributed to sertraline alone from the retrieved information. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These are reported observations from particular study groups and case reports, not proof that sertraline caused or prevented any outcome. The LactMed export is truncated, limiting review of the remainder of the infant-outcome section. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Product-label evidence

The retrieved label is for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**. It lists adverse reactions and warnings concerning the person taking the medicine, including hypersensitivity reactions, serotonin syndrome, increased bleeding risk, activation of mania or hypomania, seizures, hyponatremia, and sexual dysfunction. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

Those label findings concern the **medicine-taker population** and should not be treated as observed effects in a breastfed infant. The MCP lookup did not confirm that this specific product matches the user’s formulation, so the exact product should be verified.
