**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926) [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

- **Study design:** A study of breastfeeding women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam. Participants completed a questionnaire. Researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples from before dosing through 12 hours afterward and in one pre-dose maternal serum sample. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
- **Population:** 20 women and 21 infants; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed. Infant ages and prematurity status are missing in the returned abstract. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

**Measured drug exposure:**
- The trough breast-milk/serum concentration ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- The reported relative infant dose was **13.8 ± 3.1%** among fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** among partially breastfed infants.
- Levetiracetam dose, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk exposure measurements were substantially correlated. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

**Observed infant outcomes:**
- Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding; the study does not establish that levetiracetam caused it.
- All infants gained weight according to age.
- The authors stated that further studies were needed to assess short- and long-term safety. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
