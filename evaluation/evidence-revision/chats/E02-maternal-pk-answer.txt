## Pregnancy evidence brief: lamotrigine

**Identity:** The queried ingredient was matched exactly as lamotrigine; the returned product label was for **LAMOTRIGINE KIT / lamotrigine tablet** from OWP Pharmaceuticals. This is not evidence for every formulation or combination product. [S22](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json) [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Effects on the pregnant person

- Retrieved pregnancy evidence is concentrated in women with **epilepsy**, and some literature concerns broader antiseizure-medication populations rather than lamotrigine-specific maternal outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35133310/) [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)
- Reviews describe pregnancy management as balancing maternal seizure control with fetal outcomes. These are review-level interpretations, not measured lamotrigine-specific maternal outcome estimates. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35133310/)
- The selected label discusses risks associated with epilepsy during pregnancy, but its retrieved pregnancy section does not provide a lamotrigine-specific estimate of maternal adverse effects. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Gap:** Maternal effects attributable specifically to lamotrigine were not clearly quantified in the retrieved evidence.

### Maternal pharmacokinetics

**Clinical Considerations**

The selected label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. These are source-label statements, not an individualized treatment plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Literature**

- A review of **women with epilepsy during pregnancy** states that physiological changes affect antiseizure-medication levels and discusses serum-concentration monitoring. The retrieved abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific quantitative pharmacokinetic estimate. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers receiving psychopharmacological medication during pregnancy and lactation** assessed serum and breastmilk concentrations and child outcomes. Its abstract reports limited knowledge about pregnancy-related concentration changes but does not provide a clearly separable, lamotrigine-specific pregnancy estimate. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- A review covering **women with epilepsy and pregnancy or breastfeeding**, including lamotrigine, discusses pharmacokinetics but the retrieved abstract does not report a quantitative lamotrigine result. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)

**Interpretation:** The retrieved evidence supports a reported pattern of lower maternal lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy with return toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The magnitude, timing, and clinical significance for an individual were not established by this retrieval. The MCP classified these as candidate pharmacokinetic passages requiring study-design review; human lamotrigine-specific quantitative pharmacokinetic evidence was not confirmed.

### Fetal exposure

- Direct quantitative human fetal lamotrigine concentrations were not retrieved.
- The selected label reports decreased fetal folate concentrations in **pregnant rats**. This is animal evidence, not a measured human fetal-exposure result. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A recruiting observational study in **pregnant adult women with epilepsy** is examining antiseizure-medication clearance and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. Its returned status is **RECRUITING**; it is not a completed outcome study. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978) [S3](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.term=lamotrigine+AND+%28pregnancy%29&filter.overallStatus=RECRUITING&pageSize=20&format=json)

### Fetal outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The selected label reports the following registry populations and study contexts:

- **International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry:** 1,558 infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy in the first trimester; major congenital malformations were reported in 2.2% (95% CI 1.6%–3.1%).
- **NAAED Pregnancy Registry:** 1,562 infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy in the first trimester; major congenital malformations were reported in 2.0%.
- **EURAP registry:** 2,514 first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy exposures; major birth defects were reported in 2.9% (95% CI 2.3%–3.7%).

The label states that these frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These findings primarily concern **monotherapy exposures among women with epilepsy** and should not be generalized automatically to polytherapy, other indications, or an individual pregnancy. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

- The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts in 3.2 per 1,000 infants exposed early in pregnancy, with a reported threefold comparison against unexposed healthy controls.
- The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries.
- A European case-control study covering more than 10 million births reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 (95% CI 0.8–2.63).

These retrieved sources therefore contain a conflicting signal rather than a consistent finding across studies. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Other fetal and child outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while noting that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers receiving various psychotropic medications during pregnancy and lactation** reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or development during the first 12 months between exposed and nonexposed newborns. Because the retrieved abstract does not isolate sufficient lamotrigine-specific results, this should not be treated as definitive lamotrigine evidence. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### FAERS context

The retrieved FAERS query returned reports including congenital anomalies, prematurity, atrial and ventricular septal defects, patent ductus arteriosus, spina bifida, and hypospadias. These are reporting volumes, not incidence or individual risk estimates; they do not establish causation and lack exposure denominators. The returned cohort covers reports matching the query, not all pregnancies exposed to lamotrigine. [S2](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22lamotrigine%22+AND+patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt%3A%22foetal+exposure+during+pregnancy%22&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=8) [S13](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)

### Main evidence gaps

- No direct quantitative human fetal-exposure measurement was retrieved.
- Maternal pharmacokinetic evidence was not confirmed as a lamotrigine-specific quantitative human study.
- Maternal adverse effects attributable specifically to lamotrigine were not clearly quantified.
- Evidence varies by indication, gestational timing, monotherapy versus polytherapy, comparator, and outcome definition.
- The PubMed searches were bounded searches with retrieved abstracts, not a systematic review; no evidence elsewhere can be inferred from records not retrieved. ### Questions for a clinician

1. Which retrieved populations most closely match the indication, gestational timing, and other medicines involved?
2. Does the evidence apply to the exact formulation and active ingredients being used?
3. How should the reported pregnancy-related concentration changes be interpreted in the context of clinical response?
4. What evidence supports monitoring maternal concentrations in this specific situation?
5. How should the conflicting oral-cleft findings across registries be interpreted?
6. What fetal structural, growth, or developmental follow-up is relevant?
7. How do monotherapy findings differ from evidence involving other antiseizure medicines?
8. Would participation in the North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry be relevant? The FDA directory lists it for lamotrigine, but current enrollment and eligibility were not confirmed by this lookup.
