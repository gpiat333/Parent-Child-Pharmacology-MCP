## Metformin: label warnings compared with breastfed-infant findings

### Product label

**Source:** DailyMed, *METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]*—one selected product label, not all metformin formulations. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

- The label states that animal lactation studies found metformin in milk at levels comparable to plasma.
- It states that comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers.
- It identifies potential **hypoglycemia in nursing infants**. This is a label warning, not a reported infant outcome from the label’s own evidence.
- The retrieved label output did not provide a standalone adult adverse-reaction warning list, so a complete comparison of warnings for the adult taking metformin cannot be made from this retrieval.
- The label also contains the historical **“Pregnancy Category B”** wording. This category system is historical rather than a current FDA pregnancy classification. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551) [S3](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/labeling/questions-and-answers-pregnancy-and-lactation-labeling-rule)

### LactMed: measured exposure and observed infant outcomes

**Source:** LactMed, NLM, *Metformin*. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

**Measured exposure**

- LactMed reports generally low metformin levels in milk.
- Metformin was sometimes detectable at low levels in the serum of breastfed infants.

**Observed outcomes**

- In a prospective study of **seven infants aged 5–25 months**, the infants were described as healthy, with expected growth and development; two had normal Denver developmental screening results.
- In a group of **three infants aged 2, 5, and 14 months**, no detectable adverse effects were reported.
- In **three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days postpartum**, none had low blood-glucose levels, and the mothers reported no infant adverse reactions.
- In a prospective, nonrandomized study involving **111 infants**—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—no differences were found at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or rates of illness between the groups.

These are observed findings in the reported study populations. They do not establish that every breastfed infant will have the same outcome.

### Comparison

| Topic | Product label | LactMed |
|---|---|---|
| Hypoglycemia | Lists potential hypoglycemia in nursing infants as a concern | Reports no low blood-glucose levels in the small newborn group described |
| Infant adverse effects | Does not report observed nursing-infant outcomes in the retrieved label text | Reports no detectable adverse effects in several small groups and no adverse effects in one larger prospective study |
| Infant exposure | Provides animal milk-transfer information and says comparable nursing-mother studies were not conducted | Reports human milk measurements and occasional low-level infant serum detection |
| Evidence type | Product-specific warning/precaution | Human exposure measurements and observed outcomes |

### Missing sections and limitations

- The selected DailyMed record represents one product label, not every metformin product or formulation. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- The retrieved label did not return structured Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule subsections for **Risk Summary, Clinical Considerations, Data,** or a **pregnancy exposure registry**; the parser reported that the headings were not found. This is missing or unparsed information in this lookup, not proof that no information exists elsewhere. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- No standalone adult adverse-reaction section was returned in the evidence used here.
- LactMed reports that some infant ages, breastfeeding extent, exposure timing, and treatment duration were not stated. The studies were observational or otherwise limited in size, and absence of reported events is not proof of absence of risk. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- LactMed’s caution regarding newborn, premature, or renally impaired infants is a source recommendation; it is not evidence that those infants experienced an adverse event. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
