**Measured in milk (exposure evidence):**  
- Sumatriptan concentrations were measured in breast milk in two small studies: one involving **5 breastfeeding women** and another involving **7 women**. Peak and average milk concentrations were reported.  
- These studies estimated potential infant intake from milk, but **published infant blood-level data were not found** in the LactMed record. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

**Actually observed in infants (outcome evidence):**  
- LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence involving **3 breastfeeding women** who used sumatriptan; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The dose and route were unspecified. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Thus, milk measurements show **drug exposure**, whereas the three-infant report describes **reported clinical observations**. They are not equivalent: measured drug in milk does not demonstrate an infant effect, and the small, indirectly reported infant observation does not establish that adverse effects cannot occur. The retrieved LactMed record was not truncated.
