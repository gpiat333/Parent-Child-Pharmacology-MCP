## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved for sertraline as a single ingredient, matched exactly in RxNorm. This lookup is not clinical validation or a safety verdict; missing evidence is not evidence of safety. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effect on the lactating person

- The selected DailyMed product is **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**. Its lactation section states that published data show low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk, but that **data on effects on milk production are absent**. This is one product label, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- The selected label contains both a standalone **8.2 Lactation** section and a standalone pediatric-use section. The returned label also flags a title/code mismatch for the lactation warning; the linked product label should be verified. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured infant exposure

- In a human lactation pharmacokinetic study of **12 women and 11 mother–infant pairs**, sertraline and its metabolite desmethylsertraline were detected in milk; detectable concentrations were found in 3 nursing infants for sertraline and 6 for the metabolite. The study reported that infant serum concentrations were below the detection limit of most commercial laboratories. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed summarizes additional small cohorts and case reports in which infant serum sertraline was often undetectable, while norsertraline was sometimes detectable. Study populations varied in infant age, extent of breastfeeding, prematurity, and prenatal exposure; those differences limit direct comparison. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Some LactMed exposure estimates were modeled rather than directly measured in every infant. The MCP implementation labels these as modeled or context-only evidence; they should not be treated as direct infant measurements. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- The 12-woman study reported **no adverse effects of exposure in any infant**, but its sample was small. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed reports several small cohorts with no observed acute adverse effects and one report of benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. These observations do not establish causation. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate study of **30 infants**, only 4 of whom were exposed to sertraline, examined pain responses after prenatal and/or breast-milk SSRI exposure. The study lacked a depressed, untreated comparison group, so the observed difference could not be attributed specifically to sertraline or to breast-milk exposure. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled survey of **930 breastfeeding mothers using antidepressants** reported discontinuation-type symptoms in about 10% of infants; this was not a sertraline-specific estimate and included pregnancy and/or lactation exposure. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One sedation report involved sertraline together with other sedating medicines, so the report does not isolate sertraline’s effect. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Missing-evidence limitations

- LactMed’s retrieved record is **truncated** during a preterm-infant case; additional outcome details are missing from the returned text. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Much of the evidence comes from small observational cohorts, case reports, pooled reports, or uncontrolled surveys rather than randomized studies.
- Some studies do not report infant age, prematurity, breastfeeding extent, sampling time, maternal co-medications, or prenatal exposure consistently. Missing details are not evidence that those factors were absent.
- The PubMed search returned **129 records**, but the tool displayed only five relevance-ranked records and abstracts. The search was bounded and not a systematic review. [S8](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22sertraline%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Milk%2C+Human%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+lactat%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfed%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- The FAERS query was not specific to lactating people or breastfed infants. FAERS reporting counts reflect reporting volume, not incidence, risk, or causation. [S1](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22sertraline%22&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=8) [S10](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)

### Questions to ask your clinician or pharmacist

- Does this evidence apply to my exact sertraline product and formulation?
- How closely do these studies match my infant’s age, feeding pattern, gestational age, and prematurity status?
- Which findings are direct milk or infant-serum measurements, and which are modeled estimates?
- How should the truncated preterm-infant case and the other incomplete study details affect interpretation?
- Are any other medicines, supplements, or sedating substances relevant to the infant-exposure evidence?
- What information about infant growth, feeding, sleep, or development should be discussed during follow-up?
- Given that the label reports no direct data on milk production, what evidence is relevant to that question?
- What benefits, risks, uncertainties, and follow-up considerations should we discuss together?

### Additional lactation research

- **NCT03511118 — Recruiting:** pharmacokinetics and safety of commonly used drugs in lactating women and breastfed infants; sertraline is one of the listed drugs. The returned study population includes lactating women and breastfed infants, with the infant age criterion described as up to 180 days; individual eligibility was not confirmed, and site status may vary. [S5](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03511118)
- **NCT01796132 — Unknown status:** study record concerns women using SSRIs/SNRIs during pregnancy and lactation and includes sertraline; its returned population is adult mothers and their infants, with some sites listed as recruiting despite an overall unknown status. Current availability was not verified. [S3](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01796132)
