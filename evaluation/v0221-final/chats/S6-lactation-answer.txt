## Sertraline during lactation

Sertraline was exactly matched as the ingredient sertraline (RxNorm 36437). [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects reported in lactating people

LactMed reports:

- A midwife observed **six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline**; administered amounts were not reported. This is a report of patient observations, not proof of causation.
- In one explicitly described case, a mother reported increased milk supply during a one-week period when she was not taking sertraline, followed by decreased supply after restarting it. The retrieved passage does not establish that sertraline caused the change.
- LactMed also describes galactorrhea reports in nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients. A French pharmacovigilance-center comparison reported that sertraline was not associated with an increased risk of hyperprolactinemia compared with other medicines. The relevance of those findings to established lactation is not fully established in the returned extract.

These findings come from small reports and pharmacovigilance material. The LactMed extract does not establish the number of unique studies, and the full section was not returned. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure in milk

The retrieved LactMed **Drug Levels** section reported:

- In a pooled analysis involving **15 mothers**, mean breast-milk sertraline concentration was **45 mcg/L**, with a range of **7–207 mcg/L**.
- In a study of **26 women** who were breastfeeding, all milk samples contained detectable sertraline: mean **129 mcg/L**, range **11–938 mcg/L**. Norsertraline was also detected: mean **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1,498 mcg/L**.
- In four mothers at two months postpartum, mean milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L for sertraline** and **29 mcg/L for norsertraline**, with sampling at random times after the preceding administration.
- In a report involving the mother of a preterm infant, milk levels measured on three days at random times averaged **201 mcg/L for sertraline** and **358 mcg/L for norsertraline**.
- One woman had a mean milk sertraline concentration of **29 mcg/L** at two times that were not stated.

These are measured milk concentrations, not treatment instructions and not evidence by themselves of clinical safety or harm. The returned excerpt did not provide infant-level measurements; the availability of infant-level information is therefore **missing in the returned excerpt**. The LactMed Drug Levels section was truncated, so additional measurements may not be represented. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant findings

LactMed reports these findings separately from milk measurements:

- **One 4-month-old infant:** benign neonatal sleep myoclonus occurred, and agitation spontaneously resolved. The mother was taking sertraline. This is a single reported case; the passage does not establish causation. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Twenty-six infants**, with mean age **16.6 weeks** and range **4–28 weeks:** no detectable acute adverse reactions to sertraline in breast milk were reported in that group. This observation came from a small study population and does not establish that unreported outcomes were absent. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Fourteen mothers and their breastfed infants:** whole-blood serotonin was measured after 6–16 weeks of maternal therapy. Infant serotonin averaged **227 mcg/L before** and **224 mcg/L after** maternal therapy. This is a biomarker observation, not a complete assessment of clinical or developmental outcomes. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The LactMed **Effects in Breastfed Infants** section was truncated. Additional reported findings or details may therefore be missing. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
