### Fetal exposure

#### Maternal pharmacokinetics

The label’s Clinical Considerations states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The retrieved passage does not provide the underlying study sizes, sampling times, concentration values, or study designs. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A review states that physiological changes during pregnancy affect antiseizure-medication levels and seizure risk. In the retrieved passage, the population and study design for a lamotrigine-specific finding were not established. [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** measured psychopharmacological medications in serum and breastmilk at different time points and followed exposed children during the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high concentration-by-dose ratio in breastmilk for lamotrigine. This is lactation-related exposure information, not a measured fetal concentration, and the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific concentration values, units, sampling times, or infant specimen results. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

No verified fetal concentration was retrieved in this brief.

### Fetal outcomes

The selected label reports findings from pregnancy registries involving lamotrigine monotherapy exposure during the first trimester:

- The International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry reported major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558 infants**, with a reported 95% confidence interval of **1.6%–3.1%**.
- The NAAED Pregnancy Registry reported major congenital malformations among **2.0%** of **1,562 infants**.
- In a separate NAAED analysis of **2,200 infants** exposed early in pregnancy, isolated oral clefts were reported at **3.2 per 1,000**, with a reported 95% confidence interval of **1.4–6.3** and a reported comparison with unexposed healthy controls.

These are separate registry findings reported in the label. The label states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions that can be drawn. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. Results for those outcomes were not included in the retrieved summary, so they are not established here. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

Animal findings in the label, including fetal folate changes, are animal data and are not human fetal outcomes. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
