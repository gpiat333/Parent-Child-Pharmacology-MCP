## Brief evidence context

### Effect on the lactating person

LactMed describes reports involving galactorrhea and reports from six patients of decreased milk supply after starting sertraline. The retrieved extract does not establish how applicable these reports are to an individual situation. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

Retrieved LactMed milk measurements included:

- **Sertraline:** 45 mcg/L, with a reported range of 7–207 mcg/L, in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- **Sertraline:** average 129 mcg/L, range 11–938 mcg/L, in a study of 26 breastfeeding women.
- **Norsertraline:** average 258 mcg/L, range 20–1,498 mcg/L, in that same study.
- Additional reported milk measurements included sertraline at 26.4 mcg/L and norsertraline at 29 mcg/L in four mothers, and other study-specific measurements with sampling times described as random or not stated.

These are measured research concentrations, not administered amounts or individualized treatment guidance. The retrieved infant-level concentration excerpt was missing; the source does not establish infant specimen details from this extract. The LactMed **Drug Levels** section was truncated, so the extract is incomplete. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

The retrieved LactMed extract reports one 4-month-old infant with benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation in the context of maternal sertraline use. It also reports a group of 26 infants in which no detectable acute adverse reactions to sertraline in breastmilk were reported. These are separate source findings from different reports and populations; they do not determine an individual infant’s outcome. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
