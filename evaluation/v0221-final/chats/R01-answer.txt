## LactMed: amoxicillin in breastfed infants

### Measured exposure
- In a study of 6 women, reported milk concentrations were **0.69 mg/L** (range **0.46–0.88 mg/L**) at 4 hours and **0.81 mg/L** (range **0.39–1.3 mg/L**) at 5 hours after administration. These are milk measurements.  
- **Infant drug levels:** relevant published information was not found in the LactMed record as of its revision date; the specimen type is therefore not available. [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7246123/)

### Observed infant symptoms
LactMed reports the following observations:

- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, 3 reported infant diarrhea; no rash or candidiasis was reported in that study. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8498418/)
- In a study of 40 infants exposed to amoxicillin in breastmilk, 2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16044108/)
- In a study comparing amoxicillin with a macrolide antibiotic, reactions occurred in 8.3% of amoxicillin-exposed infants and included rash and somnolence. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19366316/)
- A separate case involved an infant who developed generalized urticaria after breastfeeding when the mother was receiving amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin. The drug responsible could not be determined; the report considered amoxicillin/clavulanate the more likely cause. The urticaria appeared after each of two breastfeeding episodes, but the exact elapsed timing between the exposure changes and later follow-up is not fully specified. [S2](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1472-8206.2009.00689.x)
- Another report described mild diarrhea in an 11-month-old whose mother was taking amoxicillin along with acetaminophen plus codeine; the report rated the association possible or probable. [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40183105/)
- In a study of 25 breastfeeding mothers and infants older than 4 weeks, no infant adverse effects were reported. [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41582095/)
