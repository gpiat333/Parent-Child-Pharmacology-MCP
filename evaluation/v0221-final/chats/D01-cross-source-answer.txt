## Sertraline during breastfeeding

The medicine identity was confirmed as **sertraline**. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Measured exposure in breast milk

LactMed reported the following milk concentrations:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers, breast-milk sertraline averaged **45 mcg/L**, with a reported range of **7–207 mcg/L**.
- In 26 women studied with extensive milk and serum sampling over 24 hours, milk sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- In four mothers at 2 months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L for sertraline** and **29 mcg/L for norsertraline**, with sampling at random times.
- In a report involving the mother of a preterm infant, milk concentrations at random times averaged **201 mcg/L for sertraline** and **358 mcg/L for norsertraline**.
- One report gave a milk sertraline concentration of **29 mcg/L**; the sampling times were not stated.

These are research measurements, not administered amounts or dosing guidance. The retrieved LactMed extract did **not** contain infant-level concentration data; infant-level information is therefore missing from this extract. The “Drug Levels” section was truncated, so additional source information may not be represented. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reported:

- One 4-month-old infant had **benign neonatal sleep myoclonus** and **agitation that resolved spontaneously** while the mother was taking sertraline. The passage does not establish that sertraline caused these findings.
- In a group of 26 breastfed infants, the source reported **no detectable acute adverse reactions** in the observations described.
- In a study of 14 mothers and their breastfed infants, infant whole-blood serotonin levels averaged **227 mcg/L before** and **224 mcg/L after** maternal therapy. The source reported that the authors concluded the observed infant exposure did not affect platelet serotonin uptake in those infants.
- One retrieved passage stated that a study lacked specific information about maternal sertraline exposure, extent of breastfeeding, or infant age.

The “Effects in Breastfed Infants” section was truncated, so this is not a complete LactMed extract. Milk concentrations and observed infant outcomes are separate kinds of evidence. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Effects reported in the lactating person

LactMed reported that:

- A midwife observed six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; exposure details were not reported.
- One mother reported increased milk supply during a one-week period when she stopped sertraline and decreased supply after restarting it. The source reports this temporal association but does not establish causation.
- LactMed also discusses galactorrhea and hyperprolactinemia reports in other contexts, including nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients.

These are reports concerning lactation or the lactating person, not infant outcomes. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
