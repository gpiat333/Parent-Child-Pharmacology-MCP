## Metformin: adult label warnings compared with breastfed-infant findings

**Source identity:** Metformin was verified as the ingredient reviewed. [S30](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/6809/properties.json)

### Adult taking metformin: what the selected label says

The selected DailyMed product label states that **lactic acidosis** is a rare but serious complication associated with metformin accumulation. Its warning discusses increased risk in the context of renal dysfunction, hypoperfusion or hypoxemia, unstable or acute heart failure, older age, hepatic disease, dehydration, sepsis, excessive alcohol intake, surgery, and intravascular radiocontrast studies. These are warnings about the person taking the medicine; they are not reported infant outcomes. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The label’s adult adverse-reaction section reports gastrointestinal reactions, including diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, indigestion, and abdominal discomfort, as well as other reported reactions. The retrieved label text does not establish that these adult reactions occurred in breastfed infants. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### Breastfed infants: what LactMed reports

LactMed reports the following observations in specific lactation populations:

- Seven infants aged **5–25 months** were described as healthy, with growth and development progressing as expected; two had normal Denver developmental screening tests.
- Three infants aged **2, 5, and 14 months** were reported to have no detectable adverse effects in the cited passage.
- In three infants aged **10–11 days postpartum**, none had low blood glucose levels.
- In a reported comparison involving **61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed infants**, no differences at 3 or 6 months were reported in height, weight, motor-social development, or rates of illness between the groups.

These are observations from the cited LactMed passages in those specific populations. They do not test every adult label warning, and they do not establish outcomes for all breastfed infants. [S32](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### Measured exposure

LactMed reported these **milk concentrations** in different maternal sampling contexts:

- **270 mcg/L**: average milk level in one group, with levels varying little over 24 hours.
- **380 mcg/L**: an approximately 4–5-hour post-dose milk measurement in one woman.
- **130–270 mcg/L**: average milk levels reported in another group.
- **410 mcg/L**: estimated average milk level in a postpartum sampling study.

Infant-level information was also retrieved: LactMed reports that **serum levels** were measured in four breastfed infants; two measurements were undetectable below the reported threshold, and two were **50 and 80 mcg/L**. In another report, metformin was undetectable in the **serum** of two infants. Infant ages, extent of nursing, sampling details, and other context were not uniformly stated. These are measured exposure findings, not administered doses or treatment instructions. [S32](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
