## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

Lamotrigine identity was confirmed as the single ingredient lamotrigine. [S21](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The selected DailyMed label states that epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, has been associated with preeclampsia, preterm labor, antepartum and postpartum hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. It states that maternal or fetal injury may be greatest with untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures. These are label-described risks associated with the underlying disease context; they are not presented as lamotrigine-specific adverse-event estimates. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also states that pregnancy-related physiological changes may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The retrieved extract does not provide numerical concentration changes or enough study detail to characterize the magnitude or consistency of this finding. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

No lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-effect estimate was retrieved in this brief. The selected label’s medicine-taker adverse-reaction and warning sections were missing from extraction; this is an extraction limitation, not evidence that such effects were absent. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Fetal or infant exposure

No verified human fetal concentration or placental-transfer measurement was retrieved.

A study of **60 mothers** measured psychotropic-medication concentrations in serum and breastmilk at different time points and followed exposed children during their first 12 months. Its abstract reported a high breastmilk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine. This is lactation-related exposure evidence, not a fetal concentration measurement; the retrieved abstract did not provide a numeric lamotrigine concentration, units, sampling times, or infant specimen results. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

The label reports animal findings in pregnant rats involving decreased fetal folate concentrations. This is animal evidence and is kept separate from human pregnancy outcomes. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Fetal and infant outcomes

The selected label reports:

- The International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry reported major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558** infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester; 95% CI, 1.6%–3.1%.
- The North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry reported major congenital malformations in **2.0%** of **1,562** infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester.
- In a NAAED analysis of **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy, isolated oral clefts were reported at **3.2 per 1,000**; the label describes this as a threefold increase compared with unexposed healthy controls.

The label states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions that can be drawn. It also states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay, but the retrieved extract does not provide their numerical results. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The PubMed retrieval included reviews and a practice guideline, but the summarized material does not establish additional lamotrigine-specific outcome estimates from those records. The search was bounded rather than systematic. All eight requested PubMed records had abstract text returned; none were reported as missing, unreturned, or failed abstract lookups. [S9](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json) [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35133310/) [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/) [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)

### Key limitations

- The evidence brief used one selected product label, not all lamotrigine formulations. The label was effective October 12, 2026; DailyMed notes that in-use labeling can differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The pregnancy section was truncated, and the selected label’s medicine-taker adverse-reaction and warning sections were missing from extraction. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- Registry findings concern particular exposure definitions and populations; the retrieved material does not establish applicability to every formulation, co-exposure, indication, timing pattern, or underlying disease.
- LactMed was not applicable because the requested population was pregnancy. The MCP’s pregnancy-coverage notice states that its prototype review did not identify a curated federal pregnancy counterpart to LactMed; pregnancy evidence here came from product labeling and literature, and coverage is not exhaustive.
