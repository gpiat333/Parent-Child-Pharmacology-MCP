## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Evidence status:** Sertraline was exactly identified as the single ingredient sertraline. The retrieved material is partial and does not determine an individual treatment plan. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effect on the lactating person

- LactMed reports that a midwife observed **six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline**; administered amounts were not reported in that passage. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In one reported case, a mother reported increased milk supply during a one-week interruption and decreased supply after restarting sertraline. The returned extract does not establish the exact timing of other relevant events or prove causation. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- LactMed also reports discussion of galactorrhea and a pharmacovigilance analysis concerning hyperprolactinemia. The complete supporting passages were not returned. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These are source-reported observations, not proof that sertraline caused the effects.

### Measured exposure in milk

The returned LactMed extract reported these milk concentrations:

- **Sertraline:** mean **45 mcg/L**, range **7–207 mcg/L**, from a pooled analysis involving 15 mothers. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Sertraline:** mean **129 mcg/L**, range **11–938 mcg/L**; **norsertraline:** mean **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1,498 mcg/L**, in a study with extensive milk and maternal-serum sampling over 24 hours. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- At two months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline in four mothers sampled at random times. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a report involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline; the returned passage does not provide a complete infant-exposure description. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

**Infant-level information:** No infant-level concentration excerpt was returned. Infant specimen type and the complete infant-level measurement record are therefore missing from this extract. Milk concentrations are research measurements, not dose instructions.

### Observed infant outcomes

- One report described benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in a four-month-old infant and agitation that reportedly resolved spontaneously. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a separate group of 26 infants, the returned passage reported no detectable acute adverse reactions to sertraline in breast milk. The returned summary does not provide the complete study context. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of 14 mothers and their breastfed infants, whole-blood serotonin measurements were obtained after maternal therapy. The passage reports infant serotonin averages before and after therapy and the authors’ interpretation concerning platelet serotonin uptake. This is an observed laboratory finding from that study, not an individual prediction. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The retrieved extract includes additional infant-outcome material, but the **Effects in Breastfed Infants** section was truncated. The underlying study count and complete study designs were not established from this extract. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
