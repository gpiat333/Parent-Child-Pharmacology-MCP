**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design and population
- The abstract does not state a formal study-design label. It reports that breastfeeding women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam completed a questionnaire and provided serial breast-milk samples from before administration through 12 hours afterward, plus one pre-administration maternal serum sample.
- The study included **20 women and 21 infants**; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed.

### Drug exposure
- Levetiracetam was measured in breast milk and maternal serum.
- The reported trough breast-milk/maternal-serum ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- The abstract reports relative infant exposure estimates of **13.8 ± 3.1%** in fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** in partially breastfed infants.
- Correlations were reported among maternal levetiracetam exposure, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk exposure over time.

### Infant outcomes
- Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding; it does not establish causation.
- The abstract states that all infants gained weight according to age.
- Short- and long-term safety were not established by the abstract; the authors stated that further studies were warranted.
