## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured exposure in breast milk

LactMed reports these measured milk concentrations:

- **Sertraline:** average **45 mcg/L**; range **7–207 mcg/L** in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- **Sertraline:** average **129 mcg/L**; range **11–938 mcg/L** in a study with extensive milk sampling over 24 hours.
- **Norsertraline:** average **258 mcg/L**; range **20–1,498 mcg/L** in that same study.
- In four mothers at 2 months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L for sertraline** and **29 mcg/L for norsertraline**.
- In one report involving a mother of a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L for sertraline** and **358 mcg/L for norsertraline**; sampling was at random times on three days.
- One woman had a reported average milk sertraline concentration of **29 mcg/L**; the sampling times were not stated.

These are measured research concentrations, not administered doses or dosing instructions. The returned LactMed extract did **not** include an infant-level concentration excerpt. The Drug Levels section was truncated, so additional measurements may be missing. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports:

- One 4-month-old infant had benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and agitation that spontaneously resolved. The retrieved passage does not establish that sertraline caused these findings.
- In a group of 26 breastfed infants, the retrieved passage reported no detectable acute adverse reactions to sertraline in breast milk.
- In 14 breastfed infants, whole-blood serotonin levels averaged **227 mcg/L before** and **224 mcg/L after** maternal therapy. This was a biological measurement, not a clinical symptom outcome.
- One cited report lacked specific information about maternal exposure, extent of breastfeeding, and infant age.

The Effects in Breastfed Infants section was truncated; this is therefore not a complete extraction of the LactMed record. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Effects on lactation

LactMed separately reports:

- A midwife observed six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; the retrieved passage does not provide their administered amounts.
- One case report described increased milk supply during a one-week period when sertraline was stopped and decreased supply after it was restarted. The retrieved passage does not establish causation. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
