## Metformin: adult label warnings compared with breastfed-infant findings

**Identity:** RxNorm matched the exact ingredient **metformin** (RxCUI 6809). [S13](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/6809/properties.json)

### Adult taking metformin — DailyMed label

The selected product is **METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]**. The retrieved adult **Warnings** section states that metformin-associated lactic acidosis is rare but serious and describes reported clinical contexts including renal insufficiency, renal hypoperfusion, hypoxemia, dehydration, sepsis, hepatic disease, certain heart-failure states, older age and alcohol exposure. The label also reports gastrointestinal adverse reactions, including diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, asthenia, indigestion, abdominal discomfort and headache. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

These are warnings and reported adverse reactions for the person taking metformin; they are not reported infant outcomes.

### Breastfed infants — LactMed

#### Measured exposure

LactMed reported these **milk concentrations** in separate lactation studies:

- **270 mcg/L** averaged over 24 hours in six women.
- About **380 mcg/L** in one woman at approximately 4–5 hours after administration.
- Milk averages of **130 and 270 mcg/L** in two women in one study.
- Milk levels averaging **130–270 mcg/L** in three women in another study.
- An average milk concentration of **410 mcg/L** in a postpartum study. [S15](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

Infant-level information was also retrieved. The source describes **infant serum** measurements: in four breastfed infants, two levels were undetectable at **<10 mcg/L**, while two were **50 and 80 mcg/L**. In another report, metformin was undetectable at **<5 mcg/L** in the serum of two infants. Some infant ages and the extent of nursing were not stated. [S15](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

These are measured research concentrations, not administered amounts or dosing instructions.

#### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports:

- **Seven infants aged 5–25 months** were judged healthy, with growth and development progressing as expected; two also had normal Denver developmental screening tests. [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436333/)
- **Three infants aged 2, 5 and 14 months** had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12545145/)
- In **three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days postpartum**, no low blood-glucose levels were reported, and mothers reported no infant adverse reactions. [S6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15932841/)
- A prospective, nonrandomized study followed **111 infants**: 61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed. At 3 and 6 months, blinded observers found no differences between groups in height, weight, motor-social development or rates of illness. [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737874/)

These observations do not convert the adult label warnings into infant outcomes. The retrieved LactMed passages did not report lactic acidosis or gastrointestinal adverse effects in the infants.
