## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Identity:** The medicine input was matched exactly to sertraline. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved, but this is a bounded summary rather than a complete literature review. The retrieved material is not a safety verdict, and missing evidence is not evidence of safety. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Effects on the lactating person

The LactMed extract reports:

- A midwife observed six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; the administered amounts were not reported.
- In one case, the mother reported increased milk supply after stopping sertraline for one week and decreased supply after restarting it. The source does not establish causation.
- LactMed also reports galactorrhea cases in nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients and describes a pharmacovigilance comparison concerning hyperprolactinemia. The returned summary does not include complete details for those reports.

These are separate source-reported observations, not individualized predictions. Fourteen of 19 summarized maternal-effect passages were omitted from the summary. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

The returned LactMed extract reported the following **milk concentrations**:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L**, range **7–207 mcg/L**, from a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- In a study of 26 breastfeeding women with extensive milk and serum sampling over 24 hours: sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**); norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- In four mothers sampled at two months postpartum: sertraline averaged **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L**, with sampling times not specified in that passage.
- In a case involving a preterm infant, milk levels averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline; sampling was at random times.

The returned extract did not provide an infant-level concentration excerpt. The source’s specimen terminology for infant levels therefore cannot be verified from this summary. The LactMed Drug Levels section was truncated, and these measurements are research findings—not dosing instructions. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

The returned LactMed extract reports:

- One report of sleep myoclonus in a four-month-old infant and agitation that resolved spontaneously; the passage does not establish that sertraline caused these events.
- In a group of 26 infants, the returned passage reported no detectable acute adverse reactions.
- In a study of 14 mothers and their breastfed infants, infant whole-blood serotonin averages were reported before and after maternal therapy. The source authors concluded that the measured exposure did not affect platelet serotonin uptake.

The infant-effects section was truncated; 19 of 24 summarized outcome passages were omitted. For at least one reported study, maternal amount, extent of breastfeeding, and infant age were not reported in the returned passage. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
