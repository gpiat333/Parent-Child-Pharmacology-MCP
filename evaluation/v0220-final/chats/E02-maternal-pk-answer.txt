### Effects on the pregnant person

The retrieved label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It also reports decreased lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. These are source statements about pharmacokinetic and therapeutic considerations, not an individualized treatment plan. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The retrieved literature includes reviews discussing pregnancy management of epilepsy and maternal seizure control, but the extracted passages did not provide a lamotrigine-specific estimate of maternal adverse clinical outcomes. One review describes adverse pregnancy outcomes associated with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-drug exposure; this finding is not specific to lamotrigine. ### Fetal exposure

No numeric human fetal concentration or pregnancy-specific placental-transfer measurement was retrieved in this extract.

A retrieved study of **60 mothers** measured psychopharmacological medications in maternal serum and breastmilk at different time points during pregnancy and lactation and observed exposed children during the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high breastmilk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine. This is lactation-related exposure information, not a fetal concentration measurement. The abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific pregnancy concentration results in the retrieved passage. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

The selected label reports animal findings in pregnant rats involving decreased fetal folate concentrations. These are animal findings, not human fetal exposure measurements. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal or infant outcomes

The selected label reports registry findings for infants exposed to **lamotrigine monotherapy**:

- The International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry reported major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558** infants exposed during the first trimester; the reported 95% confidence interval was **1.6%–3.1%**.
- The NAAED Pregnancy Registry reported major congenital malformations among **2.0%** of **1,562** infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester.
- In a separate NAAED analysis of **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy, isolated oral clefts were reported at **3.2 per 1,000**, with a 95% confidence interval of **1.4–6.3 per 1,000**; the label describes a comparison with unexposed healthy controls.

The label states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation. The retrieved extract mentions analyses of fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay, but does not provide lamotrigine-specific estimates for those outcomes. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A separate abstract describes maternal serum and breastmilk measurements and follow-up of exposed children during the first 12 months, but the retrieved passage does not provide lamotrigine-specific child outcome estimates. The source population includes pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children; these population labels should not be treated as interchangeable. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
