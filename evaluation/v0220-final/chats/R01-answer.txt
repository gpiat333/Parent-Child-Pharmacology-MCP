### Measured exposure
- In a study of **6 women**, average amoxicillin concentrations in milk were **0.69 mg/L** at 4 hours (range **0.46–0.88 mg/L**) and **0.81 mg/L** at 5 hours (range **0.39–1.3 mg/L**) after administration.  
- **Infant levels:** relevant published information was **not found** as of the LactMed revision date; the source does not provide infant-specimen measurements. [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7246123/)

### Observed infant symptoms
- In a telephone follow-up of **25 nursing mothers**, **3 mothers reported diarrhea** in their infants; no rash or candidiasis was reported in that study. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8498418/)
- In a prospective study of **40 infants exposed to amoxicillin in breastmilk**, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16044108/)
- In a study comparing amoxicillin-exposed infants with infants exposed to a macrolide antibiotic, reported reactions occurred in **8.3%** of amoxicillin-exposed infants and included **rash and somnolence**; the source states this was similar to the comparison group. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19366316/)
- A separate case report described recurrent generalized urticaria in a **2-month-old infant** after breastfeeding. The mother was receiving amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin, so the specific causative drug could not be determined; LactMed judged the antibiotic combination, most likely amoxicillin/clavulanate, as the probable cause. [S2](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1472-8206.2009.00689.x)
- Another report described mild diarrhea in an **11-month-old infant** whose mother was taking amoxicillin plus acetaminophen and codeine; the reaction was rated possible or probable. [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40183105/)
- In a study of **25 breastfeeding mothers** whose infants were older than 4 weeks, no infant adverse effects were reported. [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41582095/)

### Advice to monitor
The retrieved LactMed text does **not state a separate clinical recommendation to monitor** for particular symptoms. One controlled study had mothers assess tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change and jaundice as study measures. Those assessment measures should not be treated as advice from LactMed to monitor them. That study found no statistical differences for those measures between infants of control mothers and infants exposed to the related antibiotics ampicillin or ampicillin-clavulanate. [S1](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.1991.tb05523.x)

### Uncertainty
LactMed states that reported rash and disruption of gastrointestinal flora, including diarrhea or thrush, have **not been adequately evaluated**. The reports involve different study methods, comparison medicines, and—in the urticaria case—co-exposures, so they do not establish that amoxicillin alone caused each symptom. The retrieved extract does not establish a study count; its nine references are not a study count. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
