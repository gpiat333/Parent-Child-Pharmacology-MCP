### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The selected DailyMed label states that **epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, has been associated with** preeclampsia, preterm labor, antepartum and postpartum hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. It also states that maternal or fetal injury may be greatest with untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures. These are label statements about underlying disease and pregnancy context, not findings that lamotrigine caused each outcome. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The retrieved extract does not provide a validated individualized pharmacokinetic plan. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Maternal pharmacokinetics

The retrieved label passages describe changes in lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy, but no numeric maternal concentration data were returned in this summary. A review abstract describes pregnancy-related changes in antiseizure-medication levels, but the retrieved passage does not establish a lamotrigine-specific study population or design. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4) [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)

### Fetal or infant exposure

No verified numeric **fetal** lamotrigine concentration was retrieved.

A mixed pregnancy/lactation study included **60 mothers** and examined medication concentrations in serum and breastmilk at different time points, with child development assessed during the first 12 months. Its abstract reported a high concentration-by-dose ratio in breastmilk for lamotrigine, but the retrieved extract supplied no lamotrigine-specific concentration value, no fetal concentration, and no infant specimen result. The population was mixed—pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children—so it should not be generalized to fetal exposure during pregnancy. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

The label reports animal findings in pregnant rats involving decreased fetal folate concentrations. This is animal evidence, not measured human fetal exposure. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal or infant outcomes

The selected label reports the following registry findings:

- **International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry:** major congenital malformations in **2.2%** of **1,558** infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester; reported 95% CI, **1.6%–3.1%**.
- **NAAED Pregnancy Registry:** major congenital malformations among **2.0%** of **1,562** infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester.
- In another NAAED analysis, isolated oral clefts were reported at **3.2 per 1,000** among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy; the label describes this as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls.

The label states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions. It also states that the available data did not suggest increased risk of the listed outcomes with lamotrigine monotherapy exposure; this is a source statement with the stated limitations, not an overall safety conclusion. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label says that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay, but the retrieved extract does not provide lamotrigine-specific estimates for those outcomes. [S8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Label and evidence limitations

The selected DailyMed record was one lamotrigine product label, not all formulations. Its pregnancy section was marked truncated, and the returned extraction did not include the label’s adult adverse-reaction, warnings, warnings-and-precautions, boxed-warning, or drug-interaction sections. Missing sections are extraction limitations, not evidence that those sections contain no information. DailyMed also states that in-use labeling can differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling.
