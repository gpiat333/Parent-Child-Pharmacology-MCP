**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926, 2022) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design and population
The abstract does not state a formal study-design label. It describes women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam who completed a questionnaire and provided serial breast-milk samples from before dosing through 12 hours afterward, plus one pre-dose maternal serum sample.

The study included **20 breastfeeding women and 21 infants**; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Drug exposure
- Levetiracetam was measured in breast milk and maternal serum.
- The trough breast-milk/serum ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- The abstract reports relative infant dose estimates of **13.8 ± 3.1%** for fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** for partially breastfed infants.
- Correlations were reported among maternal levetiracetam dose, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk AUC values. These are study findings, not dosing advice. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Observed infant outcomes
- Three women reported **somnolence** in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding; it does not establish causation.
- The abstract states that all infants gained weight according to age.
- The authors stated that further studies were warranted to assess short- and long-term safety. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
