## Measured exposure in milk

LactMed reports these **sumatriptan concentrations in breast milk**:

- In five breastfeeding women, peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L** (range **62–113 mcg/L**), measured about **2.5 hours** after administration. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8866921/)
- In a separate report involving seven women, peak milk concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** (range **24.6–112.8 mcg/L**) and occurred **2–4 hours** after administration. The average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L** (range **7.7–50.6 mcg/L**). [S2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33730376/)

**Infant-level measurements:** Relevant published information was **not found** in the LactMed record as of its revision date. The retrieved material does not specify an infant specimen with a measured sumatriptan concentration. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

## What was observed in infants

LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence stating that, among **three women** known to the manufacturer who used sumatriptan during breastfeeding, **none reported adverse effects in their infants**. The exposure dose and route were unspecified. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

## Why these are different kinds of evidence

Milk concentrations are **measured exposure data**: they show how much sumatriptan was detected in milk at specified times. The infant information is a **clinical observation report**: it describes reported effects in infants, without reported infant drug-level measurements. Therefore, the milk measurements and infant observations should not be treated as the same type of evidence. The retrieved record does not establish a complete study count or a safety verdict.
