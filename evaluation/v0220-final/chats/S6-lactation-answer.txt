## Sertraline during lactation

Sertraline was matched exactly as the single ingredient sertraline, RxNorm ID 36437. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects reported in the lactating person

LactMed reports:

- A midwife observed **6 patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline**; administered amounts were not reported.
- In one of those reports, the mother had taken sertraline since the sixth month of pregnancy, reported increased milk supply during a one-week interruption at four months postpartum, and reported decreased supply after restarting. The source does not establish the exact elapsed time between restarting and the reported decrease.
- LactMed also describes galactorrhea reports in nonpregnant, nonbreastfeeding patients and cites a French pharmacovigilance study in which sertraline was not found to have an increased risk of hyperprolactinemia compared with other drugs.

These are source-reported observations from a limited extract; the retrieved material does not establish a definitive study count. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure in milk

The retrieved LactMed record reported the following **milk concentrations**:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers: sertraline averaged **45 mcg/L**, range **7–207 mcg/L**.
- In a study of 26 women with extensive milk and serum sampling over 24 hours: sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L**, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1498 mcg/L**.
- In a report involving 4 mothers at two months postpartum: average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- In a report involving the mother of a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline over three days.
- One woman’s milk sertraline concentration averaged **29 mcg/L** at two unspecified sampling times.

These are research measurements, not administered amounts or treatment instructions. The retrieved LactMed extract did not include an infant-level concentration passage; the specimen type and timing for infant-level measurements are therefore missing in this extract. The Drug Levels section was truncated, so additional measurement context may be missing. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports:

- One **4-month-old infant** had benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and agitation that resolved spontaneously in a case reported in the context of maternal sertraline use.
- In a group of **26 infants**, with average age **16.6 weeks** and range **4–28 weeks**, the retrieved passage reported no detectable acute adverse reactions to sertraline in breastmilk.
- In a study of **14 mothers and their breastfed infants**, whole-blood serotonin levels were measured after 6–16 weeks of maternal therapy. Infant levels averaged **227 mcg/L before** and **224 mcg/L after** maternal therapy. The source reported the authors’ conclusion that these findings indicated no effect on platelet serotonin uptake in the infants; this biological finding is not the same as an assessment of all clinical outcomes.

The Effects in Breastfed Infants section was truncated: 24 passages were identified and 19 were omitted from the summary. Some retrieved material did not specify maternal exposure details, extent of breastfeeding, or infant age. These omissions limit interpretation; they do not establish that an outcome was absent. [S54](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
