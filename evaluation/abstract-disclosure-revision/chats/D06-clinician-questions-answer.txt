## Questions to ask your clinician

- Does the evidence apply to my exact sertraline product, formulation, and any other active ingredients?
- How closely do the study populations match my situation, including my baby’s age, prematurity, health status, and amount of breastfeeding?
- Which findings are **measured exposure**—such as concentrations in milk or infant specimens—and which are **observed clinical outcomes**?
- What do the reported milk concentrations mean in the context of the studies, and do they apply to my circumstances? These are research measurements, not individualized dosing guidance. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- What infant outcomes have actually been observed, and what are the limitations of those studies? For example, one study of 12 women measured sertraline and desmethylsertraline in milk and infant serum; its abstract reported detectable sertraline in 3 infants and desmethylsertraline in 6, with no observed adverse effects in that study. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- How should the single reported case involving a preterm infant with symptoms and elevated infant serum concentrations be interpreted separately from the larger groups in which no acute adverse reactions were observed? [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- What evidence is available specifically for infants who were premature, very young, medically fragile, or exclusively breastfed?
- Are there evidence-based concerns about milk production or lactation itself, separate from measurements of sertraline in milk? [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- How can findings related to breastfeeding outcomes be separated from the effects of depression, postpartum circumstances, or other treatment factors? [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- What uncertainties or missing evidence should we discuss before making a treatment decision?
- What follow-up plan should my prescribing clinician, pharmacist, and my baby’s clinician discuss together?
