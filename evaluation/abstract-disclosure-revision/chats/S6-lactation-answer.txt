## Sertraline during lactation: balanced evidence summary

Sertraline was identity-matched as the ingredient sertraline. [S9](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Measured exposure

These are **milk-concentration measurements**, not infant clinical outcomes or treatment instructions:

- In pooled data from 15 mothers, breast-milk sertraline averaged **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**).
- In a 24-hour sampling study of 26 women, milk sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). Concentrations were higher in hindmilk than foremilk.
- In four mothers sampled at 2 months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- In one report involving a preterm infant, randomly timed milk samples averaged **201 mcg/L** sertraline and **358 mcg/L** norsertraline.
- In one foremilk/hindmilk report, concentrations were **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively.
- In a small first-week-postpartum study, sertraline was below quantification in one participant (**<5 mcg/L**) and ranged from **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight others.

### Observed infant outcomes

**Findings without reported adverse effects**

- In a study of **26 infants**, no acute adverse reactions were detected; the infants’ average age was 16.6 weeks.
- In a study of **14 mother–infant pairs**, no infant adverse effects were reported while infant whole-blood serotonin was measured before and after maternal treatment.
- In a follow-up of **25 infants**, reported six-month weight gain was within national reference standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects.
- Other small groups—six infants and seven infants—had no clinically noted adverse reactions at the time of assessment.
- The selected DailyMed label reports a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which no adverse reactions were observed in exclusively human-milk-fed infants. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

These were mainly small observational or pharmacokinetic studies. The retrieved LactMed metadata does not establish an exact total study count; its listed study-type mentions include observational studies, case reports, and a meta-analysis. Reference counts are not study counts. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

**Reported adverse findings**

- One 4-month-old infant had benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation that resolved spontaneously. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One report described sedation in a breastfed infant whose mother was also taking zopiclone and had taken alprazolam on two occasions. Because of the co-exposures, attribution to sertraline alone is uncertain. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A case report involved a preterm infant born at 33 weeks. The mother had exposure during pregnancy and breastfeeding. The infant developed hyperthermia, altered muscle tone, and high-pitched crying; the report also described measurable infant serum sertraline and metabolite concentrations and genetically intermediate metabolism involving two CYP450 enzymes. The infant’s symptoms and concentrations later decreased after breastfeeding was discontinued. The source attributed the reaction to sertraline, but this remains a single case involving prematurity, pregnancy exposure, and unusual metabolic findings; it should not be generalized to all breastfed infants. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small study of infants of depressed mothers reported blunted pain responses among infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy, with or without breastfeeding exposure. Only four infants were exposed to sertraline, and the comparison group did not consist of depressed mothers who were untreated; an effect related to maternal depression or behavior could therefore not be excluded. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled online survey of 930 breastfeeding mothers using antidepressants reported infant discontinuation-type symptoms in about 10% of infants. The result was not sertraline-specific and included exposure during pregnancy as well as lactation. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The LactMed sections on drug levels and infant effects were truncated in the returned record. Additional details may therefore be missing; the extract should not be treated as a complete record. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Lactation and milk-production findings

These findings concern the lactating person or breastfeeding process, not infant drug exposure:

- A midwife observed six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; treatment details were not reported.
- In a small study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitors, only one participant used sertraline. The study reported delayed onset of lactogenesis compared with controls, but no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day four.
- Other observational studies involved antidepressants or SSRIs as groups rather than sertraline alone, and underlying illness may have contributed to breastfeeding outcomes.
- The selected product label states that published literature shows low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk, but reports no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32) [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Main evidence limitations

- Exact study populations, infant ages, breastfeeding extent, sampling times, prematurity, and co-exposures were not consistent across reports.
- Case reports preserve the reported timing and source attribution but cannot establish a general risk.
- Several findings combine pregnancy and breastfeeding exposure, so they do not isolate lactation exposure.
- A reported absence of adverse effects in small groups is not proof that adverse effects cannot occur.
- The selected DailyMed record is for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective date **November 5, 2026**. The lookup did not confirm that it is the exact product being used. The lactation section was returned, but the adult warnings, boxed warning, drug-interaction, and warnings-and-precautions sections were missing from this extraction; that omission is not evidence that those sections are absent from the full label.
