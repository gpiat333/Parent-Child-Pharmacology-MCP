### DailyMed label: lactation warning

The label says animal lactation studies found metformin in milk at levels comparable to plasma. It also says comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers and raises a **potential for hypoglycemia in nursing infants**. This is a label warning or precaution; the retrieved label section does **not** provide observed breastfed-infant outcomes. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### LactMed: measured exposure

LactMed reports measured metformin concentrations in milk, including:

- **270 mcg/L** averaged over 24 hours in one study, with approximately **380 mcg/L** in one participant at about 4–5 hours after exposure.
- Other reports of milk concentrations averaging **130 and 270 mcg/L**, stable levels of **130–270 mcg/L**, and an average of **410 mcg/L** in samples collected 4–17 days postpartum.
- Infant **serum** measurements: two infants had levels below **10 mcg/L**; two others had levels of **50 and 80 mcg/L**. Another report found infant serum levels below **5 mcg/L** in two infants. Some infant ages and extent of nursing were not stated.

These are measured exposure findings, not dosing instructions. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### LactMed: observed breastfed-infant findings

LactMed reports:

- Seven infants aged 5–25 months were described as healthy, with expected growth and development; two had normal Denver developmental screening results.
- Three infants had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin.
- In three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days postpartum, no low blood-glucose levels were reported, and mothers reported no infant adverse reactions.
- In a prospective study of 111 infants—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—no differences were found at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates.

These are observations from separate lactation studies and do not demonstrate that the label’s potential hypoglycemia warning occurred in these infants. The returned LactMed extract does not establish a definitive study count. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
