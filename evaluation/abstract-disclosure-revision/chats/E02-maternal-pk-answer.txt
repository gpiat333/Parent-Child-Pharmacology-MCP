### Fetal exposure

No direct human fetal-concentration measurement was retrieved.

The label states that pregnancy-related physiological changes may affect maternal lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of lower concentrations during pregnancy and restoration after delivery. This is maternal pharmacokinetic evidence, not a measured fetal concentration. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother naturalistic study measured medication concentrations in maternal serum and breastmilk at different time points and followed exposed children for 12 months. Its abstract reports a high breastmilk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but no numeric lamotrigine concentration was provided in the retrieved text, and this lactation finding does not establish fetal exposure. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

Animal findings concerning fetal folate and developmental effects were also returned in the label. They are animal data, not measured human fetal exposure. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

The DailyMed label summarizes first-trimester **lamotrigine monotherapy** registry data:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 infants**; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates, while noting that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit interpretation. Most pregnancy exposure data were from people with epilepsy. These results should not automatically be generalized to polytherapy, other indications, or an individual pregnancy. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

One NAAED analysis reported isolated oral clefts at **3.2 per 1,000** among 2,200 early-pregnancy exposures, described in the label as higher than in unexposed healthy controls. The label also states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries; a European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45, with a 95% CI of 0.8–2.63. These retrieved sources therefore do not give a fully consistent result for this outcome. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label discusses fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy but explicitly notes limitations from differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups. Study counts were not established in the returned extract. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The 60-mother study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant developmental differences during the first 12 months. However, the retrieved abstract covers multiple psychotropic medicines and does not establish a lamotrigine-specific analysis. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
