## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Identity:** The lookup matched **sertraline** exactly (RxNorm ID 36437). [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed describes six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; one reported increased supply after stopping and decreased supply after restarting. This is a small observational report and does not establish causation. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitors—including one sertraline user—reported delayed lactogenesis, while the reported difference in feeding difficulties after day 4 was not statistically significant. The drug-specific contribution is uncertain. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed product label states that there are **no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production**. The retrieved label was for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective November 5, 2026; it represents one product label, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured exposure

Retrieved LactMed milk concentrations included:

- Sertraline **45 mcg/L**; range **7–207 mcg/L** in a pooled analysis.
- In a 26-woman intensive-sampling study, sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** in milk (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- At two months postpartum, average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- In a case involving a preterm infant, breastmilk sertraline and norsertraline averaged **201 mcg/L** and **358 mcg/L**, respectively.
- Other reported milk values included **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, foremilk **26 mcg/L**, hindmilk **46 mcg/L**, **<5 mcg/L**, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**), and **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).

These are research measurements, not dosing instructions. The returned LactMed “Drug Levels” section was truncated, and its infant-level excerpt was missing in the returned data. A separate PubMed abstract reported detectable sertraline in three nursing infants and desmethylsertraline in six; the abstract described infant serum measurements. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports one 4-month-old infant with benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation in a case summary. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other small groups reported no acute or clinically noted adverse reactions, no adverse reactions requiring medical attention, normal reported six-month weight gain, or no abnormal effects reported by mothers. These were small studies or reports and do not establish absence of risk in every infant. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A study of SSRI exposure during pregnancy alone or during pregnancy plus breastfeeding reported blunted infant pain responses compared with a control group. Four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline; the source noted that maternal depression-related effects could not be ruled out because there was no depressed, unmedicated comparison group. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled survey of mothers taking antidepressants reported infant discontinuation symptoms in about 10% of infants, but the returned passage did not provide a sertraline-specific estimate. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate preterm-infant case described hyperthermia, altered muscle tone, and high-pitched crying, with elevated infant serum concentrations. The source linked the event to breastfeeding exposure in the context of prematurity and reduced metabolism; this is a single case report, not a general estimate. The returned LactMed infant-effects section was truncated, so additional outcome details may be missing. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

This is product-label evidence for the selected formulation, not an individualized treatment recommendation.
