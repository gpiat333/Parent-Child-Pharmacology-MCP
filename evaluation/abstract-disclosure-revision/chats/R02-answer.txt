### Measured exposure in milk

LactMed reports **sumatriptan concentrations in breast milk**:

- In a group of **5 breastfeeding women**, peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L** (range **62–113 mcg/L**) at about **2.5 hours** after administration.
- In a separate group of **7 women**, peak milk concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** (range **24.6–112.8 mcg/L**) at **2–4 hours**; the average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L** (range **7.7–50.6 mcg/L**).
- **Infant drug-level measurements:** relevant published information was not found as of the LactMed revision date.

These are **exposure measurements**—they show how much drug was detected in milk. They are different from observing whether an infant had symptoms or other clinical outcomes. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

### What was observed in infants

LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence concerning **3 women** who used sumatriptan while breastfeeding; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The report did not specify the mothers’ doses or routes, and it was not an infant drug-level study. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Thus, the retrieved record contains measured milk concentrations but no reported infant concentration measurements, plus a small report of no reported infant adverse effects. That evidence does not establish that adverse effects are impossible or provide an individual safety determination. Effects reported in lactating people—such as nipple or breast pain and occasional decreased milk production—are separate maternal lactation observations, not infant outcomes.
