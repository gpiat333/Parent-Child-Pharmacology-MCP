LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin in breastfed infants**:

### Measured exposure
- In milk from 6 women, reported concentrations were **0.69 mg/L** (range **0.46–0.88 mg/L**) at 4 hours and **0.81 mg/L** (range **0.39–1.3 mg/L**) at 5 hours after administration.
- **Infant-level measurements:** relevant published information was not found as of the record’s revision date; no infant blood or serum concentration was reported. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Observed infant symptoms
- In one telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, **3 mothers reported diarrhea** in their infants; no rash or candidiasis was reported.
- In a prospective study of **40 infants exposed to amoxicillin in breastmilk**, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**.
- Another study reported adverse reactions in **8.3%** of amoxicillin-exposed infants, including **rash and somnolence**; this was similar to the rate in infants exposed to a macrolide antibiotic.
- A separate study of 25 mothers reported **no infant adverse effects**.
- A case report involved **amoxicillin/clavulanate plus gentamicin**, not amoxicillin alone: a 2-month-old infant developed recurrent generalized urticaria after breastfeeding. The source could not determine which drug caused it and considered the antibiotic combination, probably amoxicillin/clavulanate, the more likely cause. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Advice to monitor
The record describes a study in which mothers were asked to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, and skin rash, while weight and jaundice were recorded. This was a **study procedure**, not evidence that those findings occurred and not a separately retrieved LactMed recommendation to monitor them. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Uncertainty
LactMed states that rash, diarrhea, or thrush have been reported but that these effects have **not been adequately evaluated**. The evidence includes small observational studies, parent-reported outcomes, incomplete information about other medicines, and a combination-product case. The record’s study count is not established from the extract, and the number of references is not a study count. Reported symptoms do not by themselves prove that amoxicillin caused them, while the absence of symptoms in one study does not establish safety or exclude uncommon effects.
