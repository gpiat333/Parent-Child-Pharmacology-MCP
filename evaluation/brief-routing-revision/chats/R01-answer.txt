LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin** in breastfed infants:

### Observed symptoms
- **Diarrhea:** reported in 3 of 25 infants in one telephone follow-up study. In another study of 40 infants exposed through breast milk, 2 developed diarrhea.
- **Rash:** 1 of those 40 infants developed a rash.
- **Rash and somnolence:** reported among reactions in a study comparing amoxicillin-exposed infants with infants exposed to a macrolide; the overall reaction rate in the amoxicillin group was 8.3%.
- **Urticaria (hives):** one 2-month-old developed recurrent hives after breastfeeding while the mother was receiving **amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin**. LactMed reported that the antibiotics in milk were probably responsible, but the specific causative drug could not be determined.
- **Mild diarrhea:** reported in one 11-month-old whose mother was taking amoxicillin and acetaminophen plus codeine; the relationship was assessed as possible or probable.
- In another study involving 25 mothers and infants, **no infant adverse effects were reported**.

### Advice to monitor—not observed symptoms
A separate study asked mothers to monitor for tongue coating, feeding difficulty, changes in stool frequency or consistency, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. These were **monitoring items**, not reports that all of those symptoms occurred. That study found no statistical differences in the recorded measures between infants whose mothers took related antibiotics—ampicillin or ampicillin-clavulanate—and control infants; it was not a direct amoxicillin-only comparison.

### What remains uncertain
LactMed states that rash, diarrhea, and thrush have been reported but **have not been adequately evaluated**. The evidence includes small observational studies, parental reports, and a case report, so the reports do not establish a definitive frequency or prove that amoxicillin alone caused each event. The LactMed extract does not establish a study count; its “no adverse effects reported” statement is not proof of safety or of extensive study. Published infant blood-level information was not found in the record. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

Stopping or changing a medication can also carry risks; discuss any treatment change with the prescribing clinician.
