### What these reports can tell a researcher

They show the **volume and types of reports submitted** in this queried infant-filtered dataset. Examples included vomiting (117 reports), pyrexia (98), rash (60), irritability (42), seizure (35), and liver injury (28). The query also returned product-use and administration-related terms. These are reported reaction terms, not necessarily confirmed adverse effects. They can therefore help identify reporting patterns and generate questions for further investigation. In this MCP lookup, the source population for each reaction was **not established** beyond the query’s age filter, and applicability to a confirmed age band was not confirmed in the reaction-level metadata. ### What they cannot establish

- **How often side effects occur:** FAERS counts are reporting volume, not incidence or risk. The query has no exposure denominator, so these counts cannot estimate the frequency of side effects or individual risk. [S1](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22acetaminophen%22+AND+patient.patientonsetageunit%3A802+AND+patient.patientonsetage%3A%5B0+TO+12%5D&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=25)
- **Whether acetaminophen caused an event:** spontaneous reports do not establish causation. A reported event may have other explanations, including the underlying illness or other exposures, but this lookup did not establish those factors for the listed reports. [S1](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22acetaminophen%22+AND+patient.patientonsetageunit%3A802+AND+patient.patientonsetage%3A%5B0+TO+12%5D&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=25)
- **Safety from absence of reports:** no report, or a low count, would not prove that an event cannot occur or that the medicine is safe.

The MCP’s interpretation is therefore limited to describing reported-event patterns; it is not clinical validation or a causal or incidence analysis.
