## Pregnancy evidence brief: lamotrigine

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved, but it is not a safety verdict; limited or missing evidence is not evidence of safety. Identity matched **lamotrigine** exactly. Most pregnancy outcome data concern **lamotrigine monotherapy**, and the label states that most pregnancy exposure data come from people with epilepsy. [S26](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json) [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Effects on the pregnant person

- The retrieved sources emphasize balancing maternal disease control with fetal outcomes. In epilepsy, pregnancy-related risks may reflect the underlying condition, including risks associated with untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures; these are not necessarily medication effects. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/) [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35133310/) [S24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)
- The retrieved label does not provide a pregnancy-specific maternal adverse-reaction subsection. Maternal effects attributable specifically to lamotrigine were therefore **not clearly quantified in the retrieved material**.
- A 2025 narrative review concerns pregnant people with bipolar disorder, but its retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific maternal outcome estimates. The source population is pregnancy with bipolar disorder, not epilepsy. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Fetal exposure

#### Maternal pharmacokinetics

- DailyMed Clinical Considerations states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration toward prepregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is labeling context, not an individualized treatment plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A review of epilepsy in pregnancy states that physiological changes can affect antiseizure-medication levels and describes serum-concentration monitoring as a clinician consideration. The retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific concentration values or a study size for that pharmacokinetic claim. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A naturalistic study included **60 mothers** and measured psychotropic-drug concentrations during pregnancy and lactation, with child follow-up during the first 12 months. Its retrieved abstract reports pregnancy serum changes for several other medicines and a lamotrigine-specific finding involving breast milk; it does **not** provide a lamotrigine-specific pregnancy serum estimate in the retrieved text. The returned source population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children; the exact lamotrigine subgroup size is missing in the returned abstract. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- A currently listed observational study, **NCT05450978**, is **RECRUITING** and concerns antiseizure-medication clearance, maternal blood concentrations, and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. Its returned population is pregnant adults with epilepsy; the study is not evidence of benefit or safety, and individual eligibility was not confirmed. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

**Bottom line on pharmacokinetics:** The retrieved evidence supports pregnancy-associated changes in maternal lamotrigine concentrations as a pharmacokinetic concern, but it does not provide a reliable fetal concentration estimate or an individualized adjustment plan.

### Fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The selected DailyMed label reports the following registry findings for **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy**:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: 2.2% among 1,558 exposed infants, with a reported 95% confidence interval of 1.6%–3.1%.
- North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry: 2.0% among 1,562 exposed infants.
- EURAP: 2.9% among 2,514 exposures, with a reported 95% confidence interval of 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These are registry findings summarized in a product label, not an individual prediction. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

- The NAAED registry reported isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy, described in the label as higher than the unexposed healthy-control comparison.
- The label also states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries.
- A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45, with a confidence interval that included no association.

The retrieved evidence therefore contains an inconsistent oral-cleft signal rather than a consistently replicated finding. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Other fetal and child outcomes

- The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy but also states that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This is a source limitation, not proof that risk is absent. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- In the 60-mother naturalistic study, the abstract reports no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Because the returned population included multiple psychotropic medicines and the lamotrigine-specific subgroup size is missing in the returned abstract, this finding cannot be treated as a lamotrigine-specific estimate. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- A completed EUROCAT registry study examined first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy and isolated oral clefts. Its registry summary reports no statistically significant increased risk compared with the study’s control group; the registry record is a completed study record without posted results. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

### Evidence gaps and limitations

- The DailyMed pregnancy section was marked **truncated** in the composite retrieval, although structured pregnancy subsections were returned. The selected product was **LAMOTRIGINE KIT … LAMOTRIGINE TABLET [OWP Pharmaceuticals, Inc.]**, effective date October 12, 2026; this was one selected product label, not all formulations. DailyMed also notes that in-use labeling can differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The PubMed searches were bounded searches, not systematic reviews. The general pregnancy query returned 690 records and the pharmacokinetic query returned 109 records, but only a small relevance set and abstracts were retrieved. [S11](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json) [S12](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29+AND+%28%22Pharmacokinetics%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Drug+Elimination+Routes%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pharmacokinetic%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+clearance%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+%22area+under+the+curve%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+%22dose+adjustment%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=3&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- Several returned PubMed records have a population field that is **missing in the returned metadata** or is broad/mixed. The MCP implementation advises preserving each source population rather than treating these records as interchangeable.
- Animal findings in the label, including developmental toxicity and reduced fetal folate concentrations in rodents, are animal evidence and should not be treated as human fetal outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- FAERS results are reporting volume, not incidence or causation; exposure denominators are unavailable, and the returned cohort is not a complete exposed population. [S2](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22lamotrigine%22+AND+patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt%3A%22foetal+exposure+during+pregnancy%22&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=8) [S13](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)
- The retrieved material did not identify a curated federal pregnancy counterpart to LactMed. Pregnancy findings here come from product labeling and primary literature; coverage is not exhaustive. [S29](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/labeling/questions-and-answers-pregnancy-and-lactation-labeling-rule) [S30](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/)

### Questions for a clinician or pharmacist

- Does this evidence apply to the exact formulation and indication being treated?
- Which findings apply to epilepsy, bipolar disorder, or another underlying condition?
- What are the relevant maternal pharmacokinetic data for the pregnancy stage in question?
- Which findings are measured exposure, and which are observed fetal or child outcomes?
- How should the uncertainty around the oral-cleft findings and other long-term outcomes be discussed?
- What pregnancy and postpartum follow-up plan is appropriate for this individual situation?
- Would participation in the North American Antiepileptic Drug in Pregnancy Registry be relevant? The returned directory lists it as ongoing, but enrollment status and eligibility should be verified directly; the directory listing is not a recommendation or eligibility determination. [S28](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/pregnancy-exposure-registries/list-pregnancy-exposure-registries) [S1](http://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org/) [S27](https://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org/join-our-study/)
