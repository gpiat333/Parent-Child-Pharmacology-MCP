## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved for the exact ingredient sertraline (RxNorm identity match). The retrieved material is not a safety verdict, and missing evidence is not evidence of safety. [S14](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Maternal effects

The retrieved lactation sources primarily report milk and infant concentrations rather than clinical effects in the lactating person. The selected product label states that there are **no data on effects of sertraline on milk production**. This was one selected product label—**SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**—not all sertraline formulations. Its lactation section was not truncated, but the pregnancy section was truncated. [S6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured infant exposure

LactMed reports that sertraline and its metabolite norsertraline are measurable in human milk; infant serum sertraline is often undetectable, while norsertraline may be detectable at low levels. The record includes measured milk and infant-serum findings as well as modeled estimates; modeled exposure is not the same as measured human exposure. [S17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

A study of **12 lactating women and 11 mother–infant pairs** reported sertraline and norsertraline in milk; detectable concentrations were reported in three infants for sertraline and six for norsertraline. The study’s source population was mixed—**pregnancy, lactation, and neonate**—and the returned PubMed metadata did not establish a more specific infant age range. The abstract reported no observed adverse effects in those infants. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports one infant with benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation; this is an observed case report, not proof that sertraline caused the events. [S17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In several small groups, the source reported no observed acute adverse reactions, no abnormal effects, or normal reported weight gain. These findings came from lactation populations with varying infant ages and breastfeeding extent; they do not establish safety for every lactation situation. [S17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One study reported blunted pain responses among infants exposed to an SSRI prenatally alone or prenatally and through breast milk. Only four of the 30 infants were exposed to sertraline, and the study lacked a depressed, nonmedicated comparison group; the source therefore could not exclude effects related to maternal depression or other factors. [S17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate report described infant sedation in a mother taking sertraline together with other sedating medicines. The report does not establish that sertraline alone caused the sedation. [S17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Missing-evidence limitations

- The LactMed extract was **truncated**: the “Effects in Breastfed Infants” section was incomplete, and the “Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk” section was omitted by the output limit. This lookup therefore cannot be treated as a complete LactMed record. [S17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- LactMed’s study count was **missing in the exported data**; the number of reference markers is not a study count. [S17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The PubMed search was a bounded relevance search, not a systematic review. It returned 129 records, but only five records were retrieved for this brief. [S7](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22sertraline%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Milk%2C+Human%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+lactat%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfed%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- The adverse-event lookup was unavailable. That is a retrieval failure, not evidence that no adverse-event reports exist.
- The retrieved studies include small observational studies, case reports, reviews, and modeled estimates. Their populations and evidence types should not be treated as interchangeable.
- The selected DailyMed label is one product label, and the tool did not claim that it represents all formulations. [S6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- The retrieved literature metadata sometimes had a population field that was missing or not confirmed for lactation; missing population fields remain missing in the returned metadata. [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12452737/) [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38133585/) [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38811474/)

### Questions to ask your clinician or pharmacist

1. Does this evidence apply to my exact sertraline product, formulation, and other active ingredients?
2. How closely do these study populations match my infant’s age, feeding pattern, and prematurity status, if relevant?
3. Which findings are measured drug exposure, and which are observed clinical outcomes?
4. How should the incomplete LactMed extract and unavailable adverse-event lookup affect interpretation?
5. Are any other medicines or supplements relevant to the case reports involving sedation?
6. What follow-up questions should be discussed among my prescriber, pharmacist, pediatrician, and lactation professional?
