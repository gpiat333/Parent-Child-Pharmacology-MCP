**Milk exposure:** Sumatriptan was measured in breast milk in small lactation studies. Reported milk concentrations peaked about 2–4 hours after administration; investigators also estimated the amount an exclusively breastfed infant might ingest. **Infant blood/drug levels were not reported in the LactMed record.** [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

**Observed infant outcomes:** LactMed reports manufacturer correspondence involving three breastfeeding women who used sumatriptan; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The dose and route were unspecified. This was a small, non-systematic report—not a direct measurement of infant drug exposure or a controlled infant-outcome study. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

These are different evidence types:

- **Milk concentrations and estimated infant intake** measure *potential exposure*.
- **Reported absence of adverse effects** describes *observed clinical outcomes*.
- Neither type alone establishes outcomes for all breastfed infants. The MCP record does not establish a study count; its evidence base is therefore limited and not proof that the population is well studied. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Reports of painful nipples, breast pain, or reduced milk production concern the lactating person or lactation, not infant outcomes. [S6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)
