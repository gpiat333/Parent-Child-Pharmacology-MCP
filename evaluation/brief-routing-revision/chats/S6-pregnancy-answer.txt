## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Identity:** The lookup matched lamotrigine as a single ingredient (RxNorm 28439). [S26](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

- Most pregnancy evidence in the retrieved label concerns people with **epilepsy**, so findings may not generalize to lamotrigine use for bipolar disorder or other indications. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The label describes pregnancy risks associated with epilepsy itself—including preeclampsia, preterm labor, bleeding, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality—and notes that poorly controlled convulsive seizures may increase maternal or fetal injury risk. These are **underlying-disease risks**, not findings that lamotrigine caused those outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- A separate review concerns **pregnant people with bipolar disorder** and discusses postpartum relapse risk; this is a different source population from the epilepsy registries. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)
- The label reports pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, including decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration after delivery. This is pharmacokinetic label context, not an individualized medication plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Measured fetal or infant exposure

- The retrieved pregnancy evidence primarily documents exposure status rather than consistently measuring fetal concentrations.
- A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** included serum and breast-milk measurements during pregnancy and lactation and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children, and the abstract does not establish a lamotrigine-specific fetal exposure result. It reported a high concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine in breast milk. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
- LactMed was marked not applicable to this pregnancy request because it is a lactation resource; the retrieved pregnancy evidence came from labeling and PubMed literature. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4) [S30](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

For **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy**, the selected DailyMed label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 exposed infants**.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: **2.0% of 1,562 exposed infants**.
- EURAP: **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**.

The label states that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These registry populations were predominantly people with epilepsy and should not automatically be generalized to polytherapy or other indications. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Isolated oral clefts:** The NAAED registry reported **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy**, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label also reports that this signal was not observed in other large international registries; a European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 with a confidence interval spanning no association. This remains an inconsistent finding rather than a settled causal conclusion. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Other outcomes:** The label says meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while noting that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A separate mixed psychotropic-medication study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or first-year development between exposed and nonexposed groups, but the retrieved abstract does not establish a lamotrigine-specific analysis; its mixed population was pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Limitations

- The PubMed search returned 690 records, but the brief used a bounded relevance extract and abstracts rather than a systematic review. [S11](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- The selected DailyMed record is one product label—**LAMOTRIGINE KIT / lamotrigine tablet, OWP Pharmaceuticals, Inc.**—with effective date October 12, 2026; the pregnancy section was flagged as truncated in the returned label extract. DailyMed’s returned limitation states that in-use labeling may differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- Evidence counts and study designs were not fully established for every retrieved passage; missing fields in the returned metadata should be treated as **missing in the returned metadata**, not as zero studies or proof that evidence is absent.
- Spontaneous-reporting data were also retrieved, but FAERS counts are reporting volume, not incidence or causation, and lack exposure denominators. [S2](https://api.fda.gov/drug/event.json?search=patient.drug.openfda.generic_name%3A%22lamotrigine%22+AND+patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt%3A%22foetal+exposure+during+pregnancy%22&count=patient.reaction.reactionmeddrapt.exact&limit=8) [S13](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/event/)

### Questions for the clinician

1. How closely do these populations—mainly epilepsy registries using monotherapy—match the indication, disease-control history, and combination of medicines under discussion?
2. Which findings represent measured exposure, and which represent observed fetal or infant outcomes?
3. How should the inconsistent oral-cleft signal be interpreted alongside the international registry findings?
4. What pregnancy and postpartum pharmacokinetic issues are relevant to clinician-supervised follow-up?
5. What evidence applies specifically to bipolar disorder rather than epilepsy?
6. Would the North American Antiepileptic Drug in Pregnancy Registry be relevant, and what are its current enrollment requirements? The returned directory lists it as ongoing; eligibility and current enrollment were not confirmed by this lookup. [S28](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/pregnancy-exposure-registries/list-pregnancy-exposure-registries) [S27](https://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org/join-our-study/)
