**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (2022; PMID 34131926) [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

- **Study type and population:** Journal article reporting an observational pharmacokinetic study in breastfeeding women with epilepsy and their infants. It included 20 women and 21 infants: 16 fully breastfed and 5 partially breastfed; one woman had twins. Infant age and prematurity details are not provided in the abstract. The returned source population is **lactation and infant**.
- **Measured drug exposure:** Serial breast-milk levetiracetam concentrations and a single pre-dose maternal serum concentration were measured. The abstract reports a mean trough breast-milk/serum ratio of 0.98 ± 0.20 and correlations among maternal treatment, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk concentrations, and breast-milk exposure over time.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that it resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding. All infants gained weight according to age. The abstract does not establish that levetiracetam caused the somnolence.

**Earlier paper:** *Levetiracetam concentrations in serum and in breast milk at birth and during lactation* (2005; PMID 15857447) [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15857447/)

- **Study type and population:** Journal article reporting a pharmacokinetic study of 8 breastfeeding women with epilepsy and their infants. The returned source population includes **pregnancy, lactation, and neonate**; the abstract does not provide further infant age or prematurity details.
- **Measured drug exposure:** Levetiracetam was measured in maternal serum, umbilical-cord serum, breast milk, and infant serum. The abstract reports transfer into breast milk and very low infant serum concentrations during breastfeeding.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** No malformations were detected, and no infant developed signs of adverse effects during the reported observations. Follow-up duration and detailed outcome ascertainment are not provided in the abstract.
