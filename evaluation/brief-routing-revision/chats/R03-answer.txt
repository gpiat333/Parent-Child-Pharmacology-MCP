## Metformin: label information vs. breastfed-infant findings

### DailyMed label

The retrieved label is **“METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]”**, one product label rather than all metformin formulations. Its retrieved sections were pregnancy, lactation, and pediatric use; it did **not** provide a general adult adverse-effect/warnings section in the returned extract. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

In the **lactation** section, the label states that:

- Animal studies found metformin in milk at levels comparable to plasma.
- Comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers.
- Potential hypoglycemia in nursing infants was identified as a concern.

These are label statements about potential infant risk and available evidence; they are not reports that hypoglycemia occurred in the infants described by LactMed. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The label’s pregnancy section includes the historical “Pregnancy Category B” wording. That category is historical and is not a current FDA pregnancy classification. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551) [S3](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/labeling/questions-and-answers-pregnancy-and-lactation-labeling-rule)

### LactMed findings in breastfed infants

LactMed is a lactation-specific source. Its retrieved record reports:

- **Measured exposure:** Metformin levels in milk were generally low, and low metformin levels were sometimes detectable in breastfed-infant serum.
- **Observed outcomes:** Seven infants aged 5–25 months were described as healthy with expected growth and development; two had normal developmental screening results.
- Three infants had no detectable adverse effects reported.
- In three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days postpartum, no low blood-glucose levels were reported and mothers reported no infant adverse reactions.
- A prospective, nonrandomized study followed 111 infants: 61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed. It reported no differences at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates between the groups.

These are observations in the returned LactMed source populations, not proof that adverse effects cannot occur or that metformin caused or prevented any outcome. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### What differs between the sources

- **DailyMed:** identifies potential infant hypoglycemia as a concern and notes limited direct nursing-mother evidence in that label.
- **LactMed:** reports measured milk/infant exposure and observed infant outcomes, including no reported hypoglycemia in one small group and no reported adverse effects in several described groups.
- The label’s potential warning and LactMed’s observations address different evidence types; they should not be treated as contradictory clinical trials.

### Missing or limited sections

- The DailyMed record shows the pregnancy, lactation, and pediatric-use sections were retrieved and not truncated. However, the structured PLLR subsections for pregnancy—**Risk Summary, Clinical Considerations, Data, and pregnancy-exposure registry**—were missing in the exported record because the headings were not found. This is a retrieval/parsing limitation, not proof that no such information exists elsewhere.
- The DailyMed result represents one selected product label, not every metformin formulation. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)
- LactMed returned all four expected sections without truncation or omission, but its study count was **not established in the extract**. Some infant ages, breastfeeding extent, and timing details were missing in the source descriptions. Missing details are not evidence of safety or of absent evidence. [S4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
