## Sertraline during lactation: balanced research summary

**Identity:** The lookup matched the ingredient **sertraline** (RxNorm 36437). [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Overall evidence

The retrieved lactation evidence generally reports measurable but relatively low and variable sertraline and norsertraline concentrations in human milk. In small observational studies and case reports, most reported infants had no acute adverse effects. This is **not a safety verdict**: the evidence includes small samples, uncontrolled designs, variable sampling times, and case reports. The LactMed extract lists case reports, observational studies, and a meta-analysis, but the total study count is **not established from the extract**. Its Drug Levels and Effects in Breastfed Infants sections are truncated. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

These are research measurements, not dosing instructions.

- A pooled analysis of **15 lactating women** reported a mean milk sertraline concentration of **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**).
- In **26 women** studied with extensive 24-hour milk sampling, mean milk concentrations were **129 mcg/L** for sertraline (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and **258 mcg/L** for norsertraline (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). The source population was lactating women approximately 16 weeks postpartum with severe depression.
- Other reported milk measurements included **26.4 mcg/L** sertraline and **29 mcg/L** norsertraline in four women; **26 mcg/L** in foremilk versus **46 mcg/L** in hindmilk in one woman; **<5 mcg/L** in one woman and **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight others; and a median of **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**) in six women. Some sampling times were unspecified or the source cautioned that timing could affect accuracy.
- In a 15-mother sampling study, the mean milk concentration was **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).
- A case involving a **preterm infant** reported average milk concentrations of **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline on randomly timed samples. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The primary pharmacokinetic study included **12 lactating women and 11 mother–infant pairs**—study type: pharmacokinetic observational study; source populations: **pregnancy, lactation, and neonate**. Sertraline was detected in serum from three infants and desmethylsertraline in six; infant concentrations were below the detection limit of most commercial laboratories. No adverse effects were observed in those infants. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

The returned LactMed extract did not provide a complete infant-serum concentration table; this missing detail should not be interpreted as absence of infant exposure.

### Observed infant outcomes

**Reported without observed adverse effects**

- In the 12-woman pharmacokinetic study described above, no infant adverse effects were observed. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed describes no detectable acute adverse reactions in a group of **26 infants**; source population: breastfed infants of lactating women in an observational exposure study.
- In a study of **14 mother–infant pairs**, infant whole-blood serotonin measurements did not materially change after maternal treatment, and no infant adverse effects were reported; source population: breastfed infants of mothers receiving sertraline.
- In a group of **25 infants**, reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards and mothers reported no abnormal effects; source population: infants breastfed by mothers taking sertraline.
- Smaller groups of six and seven infants also had no reported clinical adverse reactions at the time assessed. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These findings are limited by small sample sizes, lack of randomization, limited or variable follow-up, and reliance partly on clinical or parental reporting. “No observed adverse effects” does not establish that adverse effects cannot occur.

**Reported adverse or concerning observations**

- **One 4-month-old infant:** benign sleep myoclonus and agitation that resolved spontaneously. This was a single case observation; the source does not establish frequency or causation. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Small SSRI pain-response study:** blunted pain responses were reported among SSRI-exposed infants; four of 30 exposed infants received sertraline. The comparison group was infants of nondepressed, unexposed mothers, so effects of maternal depression or behavior could not be separated from medication exposure. This was a mixed-SSRI study, not a sertraline-only outcome study. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Uncontrolled survey:** about 10% of infants in a survey of 930 breastfeeding mothers taking antidepressants were reported to have possible discontinuation symptoms. The survey was not sertraline-specific, included pregnancy exposure, and cannot establish a sertraline-specific rate. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Sedation report:** one infant was reported as sedated when the mother was taking sertraline together with other medicines, including sedating medicines. The contribution of sertraline could not be isolated. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- **Preterm-infant case:** a preterm infant born at 33 weeks developed hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying during the first days of life. Infant serum sertraline and metabolite concentrations were reported; the source attributed the reaction probably to sertraline exposure in the context of reduced metabolism. This is a single case involving prematurity and genetically intermediate metabolism, and should not be generalized to all breastfed infants. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Effects on lactation and breastfeeding

These are effects involving the lactating person or breastfeeding process, not infant drug concentrations:

- The selected DailyMed label reports low milk levels and describes a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which no adverse reactions were observed in the infants. It also states that data on effects on milk production were not available in that label. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- LactMed describes six mothers who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline, but this was an uncontrolled observation and the source did not provide administered amounts.
- A small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitors included only one sertraline-treated participant; findings from the mixed group cannot be attributed specifically to sertraline.
- Larger observational breastfeeding studies involved antidepressants or SSRIs more broadly and may be confounded by underlying psychiatric illness, treatment severity, pregnancy exposure, and other factors. They should not be interpreted as sertraline-specific effects without preserving those population differences. [S18](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
