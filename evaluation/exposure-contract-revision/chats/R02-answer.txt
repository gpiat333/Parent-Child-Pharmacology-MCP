Yes—these are different evidence types.

### Measured exposure in milk
LactMed reports **sumatriptan concentrations measured in breast milk** among lactating women:

- **Five women:** peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L** (range **62–113 mcg/L**), measured about **2.5 hours** after exposure.
- **Seven women:** peak milk concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** (range **24.6–112.8 mcg/L**), occurring **2–4 hours** after exposure; average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L** (range **7.7–50.6 mcg/L**).
- **Infant drug levels:** relevant published information was **not found in the retrieved record**.

These are **exposure measurements**—they show that sumatriptan was detected in milk. They do not measure an infant’s clinical response, and milk concentration is not the same as an infant blood concentration. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

### What was observed in infants
The record describes manufacturer correspondence reporting that, among **three women known to have used sumatriptan while breastfeeding**, **none reported adverse effects in their infants**. The exposure details and route were unspecified, and the report was not an infant drug-level study. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

So the distinction is:

- **Measured exposure:** numeric sumatriptan concentrations in milk.
- **Observed infant outcomes:** no adverse effects reported in that small, indirectly sourced group.
- **What remains unknown:** infant blood concentrations and a precise, systematically assessed infant-outcome dataset.

This limited observation does not by itself establish safety or prove that effects cannot occur. The retrieved record is lactation-specific, and its overall study count was not established.
