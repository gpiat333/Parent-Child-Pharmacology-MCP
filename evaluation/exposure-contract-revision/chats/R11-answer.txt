**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (2022), PMID 34131926 [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design
An observational study of breastfeeding women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam. Participants completed a questionnaire. Researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples collected from before administration through 12 hours afterward and in one pre-administration maternal-serum sample. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Population
The study included **20 breastfeeding women and 21 infants**; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed. The returned abstract does not provide infant ages, gestational ages, or prematurity details. Its returned population labels were **lactation** and **infant**; the age band was not confirmed in the returned metadata. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Drug exposure
- The reported trough breast-milk/maternal-serum concentration ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- The abstract reported calculated infant exposure and relative infant exposure separately for fully and partially breastfed infants, with higher values in the fully breastfed group.
- Maternal treatment amount, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk exposure-over-time values were substantially correlated. These are historical study findings, not dosing advice. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Infant outcomes
- Three women reported **somnolence** in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding.
- All infants gained weight according to age.
- The abstract does not establish that levetiracetam caused the somnolence; the authors described the possible relationship as requiring further study. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
