## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured exposure

LactMed reports these **measured breast-milk concentrations**:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers: sertraline averaged **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**). [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/)
- In 26 women sampled extensively over 24 hours: milk sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- At 2 months postpartum, four mothers had average milk concentrations of **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline; samples were collected at random times. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a case involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline across three samples collected at random times. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- At four weeks postpartum, foremilk and hindmilk concentrations were **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively, in samples collected 13 hours after maternal administration. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In first-week postpartum trough samples from nine women, sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one woman and **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight others. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In six women providing random milk samples, the median sertraline concentration was **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**); LactMed notes that sampling-time differences might affect these values. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In 15 mothers whose milk was sampled at predefined times over two days, the average concentration was **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**). [S42](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38841362/)

### Observed infant outcomes

The returned LactMed extract is truncated; its study count is not established from the extract.

- One 4-month-old infant had benign sleep myoclonus and agitation that resolved spontaneously. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of 26 infants, no detectable acute adverse reactions were observed. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In 14 mother–infant pairs, infant whole-blood serotonin levels were measured before and after maternal treatment; the extract reports no infant adverse effects in that study. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11578995/)
- In a study of 25 infants, six-month weight gain was reported as normal according to national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- In six infants aged 5–34 weeks, no clinical adverse reactions were noted at the time of the study. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367050/)
- In seven infants aged four weeks, no adverse effects were reported. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367055/)
- A small study reported blunted pain responses among infants exposed to an SSRI prenatally and/or through breast milk; four of the 30 infants were exposed to sertraline. The comparison group consisted of unexposed infants of nondepressed mothers, so the extract does not establish the contribution of maternal depression or maternal behavior. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)
- **Separate case report:** a preterm infant born at 33 weeks developed hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying. The report also described infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite; the infant’s later genetic metabolic findings and the authors’ attribution are part of that case report, not evidence that the outcome occurs generally. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)

### Product-label evidence

The retrieved label is **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective November 5, 2026. The tool did not confirm that it matches the specific product or formulation being used. DailyMed’s retrieved lactation section states that published data show low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk. In a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs, exclusively human-milk-fed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to **2%** of maternal serum levels, with a range of **0–15%**; no adverse reactions were observed in those infants. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The label states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. This is a label evidence limitation, not an observed infant outcome.
