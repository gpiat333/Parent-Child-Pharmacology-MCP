## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The retrieved DailyMed label reports that most lamotrigine pregnancy data come from women with **epilepsy**. Its clinical-considerations section describes pregnancy-associated risks reported with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-drug exposure, including preeclampsia, preterm labor, hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. These are presented as **underlying-disease risks**, not as lamotrigine-specific observed adverse effects. The label also states that untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures may carry substantial maternal or fetal risk. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The retrieved label extract’s standalone medicine-taker sections for adverse reactions and warnings were **missing in the exported data**. That is an extraction limitation, not evidence that maternal adverse effects were absent. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

For bipolar disorder, a 2025 narrative review discusses lamotrigine among perinatal treatments and describes postpartum relapse risk in women with bipolar disorder; the abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific pregnancy outcome estimates. The source population is **pregnancy and postpartum in women with bipolar disorder**; the design label is **narrative review**. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Fetal or infant exposure

The retrieved evidence does not provide a verified numeric human fetal lamotrigine concentration.

A study of **60 mothers** assessed psychopharmacological medication concentrations in maternal serum and breast milk at different time points and followed exposed children during the first 12 months. Its abstract reports a high breast-milk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but the retrieved passage does not provide a lamotrigine-specific concentration value, specimen concentration, or fetal concentration. The source population includes **pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children**; the design label was not stated in the retrieved abstract metadata. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

The DailyMed label reports reduced fetal folate concentrations in **pregnant rats**. This is animal evidence, not a measured human fetal-exposure finding. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A recruiting study, **NCT05450978**, is labeled **observational** and concerns pregnant adults with epilepsy receiving lamotrigine, levetiracetam, or oxcarbazepine; its summary concerns antiseizure-medication clearance and exposure to unborn children and nursing infants. It is an ongoing investigation, not posted outcome evidence. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### Observed fetal or infant outcomes

The DailyMed label reports the following registry findings for **lamotrigine monotherapy during the first trimester**:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 exposed infants**; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 exposed infants**.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that the frequency of major congenital malformations in these registry summaries was similar to general-population estimates. It also reports a NAAED-registry finding of isolated oral clefts among **2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy**: 3.2 per 1,000; 95% CI 1.4–6.3; described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45; 95% CI 0.8–2.63. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The same label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions that can be drawn. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT registry record describes a **case-control study** of first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy and isolated oral clefts. Its registry summary reports no statistically significant increased risk compared with other defects, but the record has **no posted results**. The study record’s population field includes **children, adults, and older adults**, while its registry-level eligibility concerns population-based congenital-anomaly registers; participant-level population details are not established in the returned record. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

The 60-mother naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Because the abstract covers multiple psychotropic medicines and does not provide a lamotrigine-specific outcome breakdown, this result should not be treated as a lamotrigine-only estimate. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Limitations

- The PubMed query returned 690 records; the evidence brief retrieved abstracts for 8 requested records, with no failed or unreturned abstract lookups. This was a bounded relevance search, not a systematic review. [S11](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- Human registry estimates summarized in the label concern particular populations and exposure contexts, especially first-trimester monotherapy; applicability to polytherapy, later pregnancy, bipolar disorder, or other clinical contexts is not established by these extracts. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The selected DailyMed record is one lamotrigine product label, and its pregnancy section was marked truncated in the general extract; the structured pregnancy data were available. DailyMed states that its in-use labeling can differ from the latest FDA-approved labeling. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- The MCP-generated interpretation guidance states that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit conclusions; this guidance is from the MCP, not a quotation from the cited source. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
