## Sertraline during lactation: research summary

### Measured exposure

The retrieved LactMed record reports these **milk concentrations**:

- Pooled analysis of 15 mothers: mean sertraline **45 mcg/L**; range **7–207 mcg/L**. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/)
- Study of 26 women, about 5–36 weeks postpartum, with 24-hour milk and serum sampling: mean milk sertraline **129 mcg/L**; range **11–938 mcg/L**; mean norsertraline **258 mcg/L**; range **20–1,498 mcg/L**. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- Four mothers at two months postpartum: mean milk sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L**, sampled at random times. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In one report involving a preterm infant, milk levels averaged **201 mcg/L for sertraline** and **358 mcg/L for norsertraline** on three randomly timed days. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- In one mother, foremilk and hindmilk collected 13 hours after exposure contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a first-week-postpartum sampling study, sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one woman; levels in the other eight women ranged from **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**. The record notes that timing may affect those values. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In six women providing random milk samples, the median sertraline concentration was **11.3 mcg/L**, range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**; the record notes that the sampling timing may make these values inaccurate. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In 15 mothers providing samples at prespecified times, mean milk concentration was **87 mcg/L**, range **12–288 mcg/L**. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The retrieved LactMed extract does **not** include the infant-level concentration passage in full. Its summary states that sertraline is usually not detected in infant serum and that norsertraline is often detectable in low levels in infant serum; the exact infant-level data should therefore be checked in the complete record. Milk concentration is an exposure measurement, not an infant clinical outcome and does not establish safety. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

Reported findings were mixed and came from small studies, surveys, and case reports:

- One 4-month-old infant had benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of 26 infants, no acute adverse reactions were detected. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In a study of 14 mother–infant pairs, infant whole-blood serotonin measurements were reported before and after maternal treatment, and no infant adverse effects were reported. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11578995/)
- In a report of 25 infants followed through six months, reported weight gain was within national growth standards and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- No clinical adverse reactions were noted at the time of study in six infants aged 5–34 weeks; another report described no adverse effects in seven 4-week-old infants. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367050/)
- A study of infants exposed to an SSRI before birth and through breastfeeding reported blunted pain responses compared with infants of nondepressed, unexposed mothers. Four of the 30 exposed infants received sertraline. The returned passage states that the study lacked a comparison group of depressed mothers not taking medication, so an effect related to maternal depression or behavior could not be separated from medication exposure. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)
- An uncontrolled online survey of 930 breastfeeding mothers taking antidepressants reported infant discontinuation symptoms in about 10% of infants. The survey included different antidepressants and combined pregnancy and lactation exposure, so the returned record does not provide a sertraline-specific estimate. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20807106/)
- One mother reported sedation in her infant while taking sertraline with other medicines, including a benzodiazepine and a sedative-hypnotic; the co-exposures mean this report does not isolate sertraline. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22504099/)

**Separate preterm-infant case report:** A preterm infant born at 33 weeks developed hyperthermia, altered muscle tone, and high-pitched crying during the reported exposure period. The report states that infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite were measured, and that symptoms later dissipated after breastfeeding was discontinued while serum levels decreased. The report attributed the episode to serotonergic overstimulation in the setting of reduced metabolism, but this is a single case report involving prematurity and cannot establish frequency or applicability to other infants. The returned LactMed section is truncated after the beginning of another case, so later details are missing. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)[S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Lactation and milk-production findings

These findings concern the lactating person or breastfeeding behavior, not measured infant exposure:

- A midwife described six patients who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; this was an uncontrolled observation, and maternal treatment details were not fully reported. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small prospective study of eight mothers taking serotonin reuptake inhibitors—including one taking sertraline—reported delayed onset of milk secretory activation compared with 423 mothers not taking an SRI. Because several medicines were represented and only one participant took sertraline, the result does not establish a sertraline-specific effect. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19965920/)
- Observational studies involving antidepressants or SSRIs reported differences in breastfeeding initiation, continuation, or discharge rates. Some included sertraline users, while others did not identify the specific antidepressant. The returned record states that underlying psychiatric illness and treatment exposure could not always be separated. [S24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22344850/)[S31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28682318/)[S32](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28984528/)[S34](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29522259/)[S41](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35170756/)
- The selected DailyMed label states that published literature shows low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk, reports a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs with no observed adverse reactions, and states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
