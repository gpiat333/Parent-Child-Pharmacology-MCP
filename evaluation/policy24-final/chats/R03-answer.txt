## Metformin: adult label warnings compared with breastfed-infant findings

### Adult medicine-taker label

The retrieved label is for **METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]**. The exact product/formulation was not confirmed. Its **Warnings** section identifies lactic acidosis as a rare, serious complication associated with metformin accumulation and discusses increased risk in renal dysfunction and conditions involving hypoxemia, hypoperfusion, dehydration, sepsis, hepatic disease, or heart failure. The retrieved warning text is truncated. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The label’s adult adverse-reactions section reports gastrointestinal reactions—including diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, indigestion, and abdominal discomfort—as well as other reported reactions, including hypoglycemia. These findings come from adult patients with type 2 diabetes and are not infant outcomes. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The label’s **lactation** section says animal studies found metformin in milk, that comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers, and that potential infant hypoglycemia was a concern. This is a label warning, not an observed infant outcome. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### Breastfed-infant evidence

LactMed reports measured exposure and observed outcomes separately. Its population is lactating women and breastfed infants; some infant ages, extent of nursing, and postpartum timing were unspecified. [S23](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

#### Measured exposure

Reported **milk metformin concentrations** included:

- **270 mcg/L** averaged over 24 hours in six women.
- About **380 mcg/L** in one woman approximately 4–5 hours after administration.
- **130 and 270 mcg/L** in two women measured over 72 hours.
- **130–270 mcg/L** in three women.
- An average of **410 mcg/L** in women studied 4–17 days postpartum.

LactMed also reports **infant serum** measurements: in four infants, two results were below **10 mcg/L**, while two were **50 and 80 mcg/L**; in two other measured infants, levels were below **5 mcg/L**. [S23](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)  
The underlying transfer studies are cited by LactMed at [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436333/), [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12545145/), [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15384618/), and [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15932841/).

#### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports that:

- Seven infants aged 5–25 months were judged healthy, with expected growth and development; two had normal Denver developmental screening results. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436333/)
- Three infants had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15384618/)
- In three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days postpartum, none had low blood glucose, and their mothers reported no adverse reactions. [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15932841/)
- In a prospective study of 111 infants—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed—no differences were found at 3 or 6 months in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates. The reported study population included infants of women treated with metformin for polycystic ovary syndrome; the retrieved LactMed extract does not establish a complete study count. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737874/)

These observations do not establish that every possible label concern was absent, and the label warning should not be converted into an infant outcome.
