### Measured exposure
- In milk samples from **6 women**, average amoxicillin concentrations were **0.69 mg/L** (range **0.46–0.88 mg/L**) at 4 hours and **0.81 mg/L** (range **0.39–1.3 mg/L**) at 5 hours after administration. These are **milk concentrations**, not infant-level measurements. [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7246123/)
- For **infant levels**, LactMed states that relevant published information was **not found as of the revision date**. [S13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Observed infant symptoms
- In a telephone follow-up of 25 nursing mothers, **3 infants had diarrhea**; no rash or candidiasis was reported in that group. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8498418/)
- In a prospective study of **40 infants exposed through breast milk**, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**. [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16044108/)
- In a study comparing amoxicillin-exposed infants with macrolide-exposed infants, reported reactions among the amoxicillin-exposed infants occurred in **8.3%** and included **rash and somnolence**. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19366316/)
- One case involved recurrent **generalized urticaria** after breastfeeding. The mother was receiving **amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin**, so the responsible drug could not be determined; LactMed reported that the combination product was considered the more likely cause. [S2](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1472-8206.2009.00689.x)
- Another report described **mild diarrhea** in an 11-month-old whose mother was taking amoxicillin along with acetaminophen plus codeine; the relationship was rated possible or probable. [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40183105/)
- In a study of 25 mothers and infants older than 4 weeks, **no infant adverse effects were reported**. This does not establish that adverse effects never occur. [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41582095/)
