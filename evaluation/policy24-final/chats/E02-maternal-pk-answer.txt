### Fetal exposure

The label states that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery.[S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The retrieved maternal-pharmacokinetic material did not provide numeric human maternal concentration, clearance, or exposure results specific to lamotrigine. A retrieved PubMed abstract describes concentration measurements in serum and breast milk in a naturalistic sample of 60 mothers and reports a high concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine in breast milk. This is lactation exposure information, not a measured fetal concentration, and the abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific participant count.[S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

A recruiting study, **NCT05450978**, is described as examining antiseizure-medication clearance, physiological factors affecting blood concentrations, and exposure to unborn children in pregnant adults with epilepsy. It has no posted results in the returned record; the study team must confirm eligibility and current site availability.[S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### Fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The selected label reports the following registry findings for **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy**:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2%** among **1,558 infants**, with 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: **2.0%** among **1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: **2.9%** among **2,514 exposures**, with 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that frequencies were similar to general-population estimates, while noting that outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation.[S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

The NAAED Pregnancy Registry reported isolated oral clefts at **3.2 per 1,000** among **2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy**, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**, with 95% CI 0.8–2.63.[S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The completed EUROCAT registry study record states that its case-control study found no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with other defects after in-utero lamotrigine exposure. Its primary comparison group consisted of malformed infants without oral clefts; the record states that the registers did not collect data on non-malformed infants.[S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

#### Other fetal and infant outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports that the extracted evidence did not suggest increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, but also states that differences in outcome definition, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit conclusions.[S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A 2023 naturalistic study of 60 mothers reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. The abstract covers multiple psychotropic medicines and does not establish lamotrigine-specific sample size or lamotrigine-only outcome estimates.[S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
