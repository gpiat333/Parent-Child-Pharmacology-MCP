## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Identity:** The tool matched **sertraline** exactly as a single ingredient. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effect on the lactating person

- LactMed reports a small observation in which six patients reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; the administered amounts were not reported. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small prospective study of eight primiparous women taking different serotonin-reuptake inhibitors, including one taking sertraline, reported delayed lactogenesis compared with 423 mothers not taking an SRI. The extract states that feeding difficulties after postpartum day 4 did not differ statistically. This was a mixed-SRI study, not a sertraline-only study. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed label states that published data demonstrate low levels of sertraline and its metabolites in human milk and that it contains no data on effects on milk production. This is one selected product label, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured exposure

Retrieved LactMed milk measurements included:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L**, range **7–207 mcg/L**, in a pooled analysis. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study with extensive milk sampling: sertraline **129 mcg/L**, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1498 mcg/L**. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- At two months postpartum: sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L** in milk samples collected at random times. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a report involving a preterm infant: milk sertraline and norsertraline concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L** and **358 mcg/L**, respectively, at random sampling times. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Foremilk and hindmilk collected 13 hours after a dose contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other reported milk findings included **<5 mcg/L**, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** with a range of **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**, and **87 mcg/L** with a range of **12–288 mcg/L**. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The returned LactMed extract did not include a complete infant-level concentration section; infant-level information is therefore incomplete in this retrieval. A PubMed abstract reported detectable sertraline in three nursing infants and detectable desmethylsertraline in six; the abstract described the specimen as **infant serum**. [S52](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

These are research measurements, not dosing instructions, and do not establish safety.

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in one four-month-old infant and spontaneously resolving agitation in the same case passage. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a separate group of 26 infants, the extract reports no detectable acute adverse reactions during the study. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Another passage reports normal six-month weight gain in infants in a study of breastfeeding mothers taking antidepressants; the extract does not establish that all findings were sertraline-specific. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small study of infants exposed to an SSRI prenatally alone or prenatally and through breast milk reported blunted pain responses compared with unexposed infants. Four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline; the passage states that the comparison lacked a group of depressed, nonmedicated mothers. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled survey of 930 mothers taking an antidepressant reported infant drug-discontinuation symptoms in about 10% of infants; the result was not specific to sertraline. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate case report describes a preterm infant born at 33 weeks who developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation disorders, and high-pitched crying. The passage reports elevated infant serum concentrations and states that the authors attributed the symptoms to sertraline exposure and reduced metabolism. This is a single case report, not a population estimate. [S30](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)[S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The Effects in Breastfed Infants section was truncated in the returned LactMed extract, so additional outcome details may be missing.
