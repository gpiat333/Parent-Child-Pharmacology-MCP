**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?*  
**PMID:** 34131926 — [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design
The abstract does not state a formal design label. It reports that breastfeeding women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam completed a questionnaire. Researchers measured levetiracetam concentrations in serial breast-milk samples collected from before administration through 12 hours afterward, and in one pre-dose maternal-serum sample. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Population
The study included 20 breastfeeding women and 21 infants; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed. Infant ages, gestational ages, and prematurity status were not provided in the abstract. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Drug exposure
The abstract reports:

- A trough breast-milk/maternal-serum levetiracetam ratio of **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- Relative infant exposure estimates of **13.8 ± 3.1%** among fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** among partially breastfed infants.
- Correlations among maternal levetiracetam exposure, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations, and breast-milk AUC values. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Infant outcomes
- Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants.
- The abstract states that the somnolence resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding.
- The abstract states that all infants gained weight according to age.
- Longer-term infant outcomes were not reported in the abstract. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

The abstract states that further studies are needed to assess short- and long-term safety. This study’s reported exposure measurements and outcomes do not determine an individual medication plan.
