### Measured exposure

LactMed reports sumatriptan concentrations measured in **breastmilk**:

- In five breastfeeding women, peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L** (range **62–113 mcg/L**), measured about **2.5 hours** after administration.
- In a separate group of seven women, peak milk concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** (range **24.6–112.8 mcg/L**), occurring **2–4 hours** after administration. The average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L** (range **7.7–50.6 mcg/L**).

### What was observed in infants

LactMed describes manufacturer correspondence involving **three women** who used sumatriptan during breastfeeding; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The infants’ drug levels were not reported, and the exposure details were unspecified. [S7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

These are different types of evidence: milk concentrations measure **drug exposure in breastmilk**, whereas the three-infant account reports **reported clinical observations**. The infant observation does not establish infant drug concentrations, and the milk measurements do not by themselves establish an infant clinical outcome.
