# Pregnancy evidence, gaps and participation

The evidence brief retains three rows for each ingredient: effects on the pregnant or lactating person; measured fetal or infant exposure; and observed fetal or infant outcomes. Empty rows remain visible. The extractor groups attributed candidate passages heuristically. An empty row can reflect bounded retrieval or extraction limits. FAERS remains a separate spontaneous-report source: empty label/abstract rows must not be interpreted as the absence of all human reports. Human, animal, predicted and source-recommendation context must remain distinct; these groups are not clinician-adjudicated findings.

DailyMed section 8.1 is parsed into Risk Summary, Clinical Considerations and Data, with a separate Pregnancy Exposure Registry subsection when present. Missing subsections remain null. Human Data and Animal Data are kept distinct, including subsequent sentences without a repeated species word. `maternal_dose_adjustment` preserves Clinical Considerations and the explicit dose-adjustment subheading when found. It is source context for clinician discussion, never a personal dose. Clinical Considerations can exist without pharmacokinetic data.

Pregnancy briefs include `maternal_pharmacokinetics`, populated from Clinical Considerations and retrieved literature abstracts. It explicitly reports when no data were found in those retrieved sources. Candidate passages preserve citations and study context; no dose is calculated, and human applicability is not automatically confirmed.

Pregnancy and lactation PubMed searches use versioned MeSH, substance-name and title/abstract strategies. They retain the medicine concept and population, offer maternal, exposure, offspring-outcome and maternal-PK focuses, and impose no arbitrary recent-year cutoff. Query translation, warnings and result counts are retained. New medicines may lack a MeSH descriptor while matching a supplementary concept or title/abstract. This is a curated prototype search, not a validated systematic-review strategy. Titles without abstracts cannot support outcome claims.

## Participation tier

`find_pregnancy_exposure_registries(drug)` returns the FDA directory's listed registry, contacts, links and status, with a non-recommendation statement. The pre-existing `search_pregnancy_registries` remains as a compatible directory tool. Recruiting studies and public-input notices share this participation tier. Displayed lists are alphabetical, not ranked for clinical suitability. Upstream retrieval limits still apply. No enrollment, contact, comment, report, eligibility decision or recommendation is performed.

The companion preflights the actual question and suppresses all research and participation during urgent, crisis, identity and other stop routes. Direct tools also screen their inputs, but short medicine/topic selectors are data rather than natural-language questions. Every client must preflight the actual question; a stateless tool cannot reconstruct omitted conversational context from a drug name alone. Spanish sentences remain blocked. Spanish MedlinePlus drug-code education remains a separate capability.

## Verified source scope and history

- **LactMed is lactation-only:** it concerns drugs in milk, breastfed infants and effects on lactation. It is not a pregnancy database. [NLM LactMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/).
- **PLLR:** section 8.1 separates Risk Summary, Clinical Considerations and Data. Clinical Considerations includes dose-adjustment information when relevant supporting data are available. It is not automatically a PK study. [FDA content-and-format guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/92565/download).
- **TOXNET/DART:** NLM announced retirement of the TOXNET website on December 16, 2019. Its transition page directs developmental/reproductive toxicology literature users to a PubMed DART filter. This does not establish that every former record migrated, that DART is a current connected service, or that a pregnancy counterpart to LactMed exists. [NLM retirement notice](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/so19/so19_toxnet_discontinued.html), [NLM transition page](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/toxnet/index.html), [DART filter strategy](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/bsd/pubmed_subsets/dart_strategy.html).
- **PRGLAC:** its recommendations identify a need for more and better research specific to pregnant and lactating populations. [NICHD recommendations](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/about/advisory/PRGLAC/recommendations).
- **BPCA:** FDA describes an incentive for voluntary pediatric studies requested by FDA. [FDA BPCA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-resources/best-pharmaceuticals-children-act-bpca).
- **PREA:** FDA may require pediatric studies for certain products, with appropriate age-group formulations. These pediatric mechanisms do not substitute for pregnancy or lactation evidence. [FDA PREA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-resources/pediatric-research-equity-act-prea).
- **Registries:** FDA lists but does not run or endorse listed registries; its list is not comprehensive. [FDA directory](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/pregnancy-exposure-registries/list-pregnancy-exposure-registries).

The tool surfaces evidence gaps; it does not close them. The pre-tested gap example uses daridorexant because the retrieved label states a human pregnancy-data limitation, the targeted maternal-PK query returned no records, and the directory returned a registry. Three general PubMed records had no abstracts in this retrieval. Animal label data remain visible as context, not human reassurance. These are retrieval-specific findings, not a systematic claim that no human evidence exists anywhere. Lamotrigine provides a contrasting example with Clinical Considerations and PK literature passages. Neither example recommends taking a medicine or joining a study.
