**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?*  
**PMID:** 34131926 [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design
A human observational study of breastfeeding women with epilepsy. Participants completed a questionnaire; researchers measured levetiracetam in serial breast-milk samples collected from before dosing through 12 hours afterward and in one pre-dose maternal serum sample. The retrieved abstract does not provide a more specific design label.

### Population
- 20 breastfeeding women
- 21 infants, including one set of twins
- 16 fully breastfed and 5 partially breastfed infants
- The retrieved abstract does not report infant ages, gestational ages, or prematurity status.

### Measured drug exposure
The study reported:
- A mean trough breast-milk/serum levetiracetam ratio of **0.98 ± 0.20**
- Relative infant exposure values of **13.8 ± 3.1%** among fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** among partially breastfed infants
- Correlations among maternal treatment exposure, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk concentrations, and breast-milk AUC values

These are study measurements or estimates, not dosing instructions.

### Observed infant outcomes
- Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that this resolved after breastfeeding became partial.
- The abstract states that all infants gained weight according to age.
- The authors described a possible relationship between breast-milk levetiracetam exposure and the reported somnolence; this temporal association does not establish causation.
- No long-term infant outcomes are reported in the retrieved abstract.
