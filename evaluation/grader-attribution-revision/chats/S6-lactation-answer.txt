## Sertraline during lactation: research summary

**Identity:** The retrieved record matches sertraline exactly. [S9](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Measured exposure

These are **milk-concentration measurements**, not infant outcomes or treatment instructions:

- In a pooled analysis of **15 mothers**, mean milk sertraline was **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**).
- In a 24-hour sampling study of **26 women**, mean milk concentrations were **129 mcg/L** for sertraline (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and **258 mcg/L** for norsertraline (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**); concentrations were higher in hindmilk than foremilk.
- In smaller studies, reported sertraline concentrations included **26.4 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**), **26 mcg/L** in foremilk and **46 mcg/L** in hindmilk, and **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).
- In one small series, sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one milk sample and **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight others.
- In a case involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L sertraline** and **358 mcg/L norsertraline** at sampling times described as random.

The returned record says infant serum sertraline is usually undetectable and norsertraline may be detectable at low levels, but a complete infant-blood-measurement excerpt was **not retrieved**. Milk concentration does not establish a clinical effect. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

**No adverse outcome observed in small observational groups**

- No acute adverse reactions were detected in **26 infants** in one observational study.
- No clinical adverse reactions were noted in a group of **6 infants aged 5–34 weeks** and in **7 infants aged 4 weeks**.
- In a follow-up of **25 infants**, reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards and mothers reported no abnormal effects.
- A study of **14 mother–infant pairs** found little change in infant whole-blood serotonin measurements; this was a pharmacodynamic measurement, not a direct developmental outcome.

**Adverse or uncertain findings**

- One **4-month-old infant** had benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. The retrieved record does not establish causation.
- One report described sedation in an infant whose mother was also taking other sedating medicines; the contribution of sertraline cannot be separated from the co-exposures.
- One case involved a **preterm infant born at 33 weeks** exposed during pregnancy and breastfeeding. The infant developed hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying during the first days after birth. Infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite were reported; later, genetically intermediate metabolism was identified. The report attributed the reaction to serotonergic overstimulation associated with persistent exposure and reduced metabolism. Because exposure occurred during pregnancy as well as breastfeeding, this is not evidence about postnatal milk exposure alone. The returned record does not provide a complete chronology for every exposure change and outcome relationship. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small study of infants of depressed mothers reported blunted pain responses among SSRI-exposed infants; only **4 of 30** exposed infants received sertraline. The comparison group did not consist of depressed, non-medicated mothers, so maternal depression or related factors could not be excluded. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- An uncontrolled survey of **930 breastfeeding mothers using antidepressants** reported infant discontinuation-type symptoms in about 10%, but the returned record does not establish a sertraline-specific estimate. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Lactation and breastfeeding context

The DailyMed lactation section reports a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which no adverse reactions were observed in exclusively human-milk-fed infants. It also states that effects on milk production were not available in the cited data. This is label evidence, not proof that all formulations or all lactating populations have been studied. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

LactMed describes:

- A report involving **6 patients** who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; this was uncontrolled.
- A prospective study of **8 primiparous women taking different serotonin-reuptake inhibitors**, including one sertraline user, compared with 423 mothers not taking an SRI. The study reported delayed onset of milk secretory activation, but no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day four.
- Larger observational studies involving antidepressants or SSRIs reported differences in breastfeeding rates, but these findings were not consistently sertraline-specific and may reflect underlying psychiatric illness, illness severity, pregnancy exposure, or treatment patterns.

These lactation findings concern the lactating person or breastfeeding behavior; they are not infant adverse-event measurements. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
