## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### LactMed: measured milk exposure

LactMed reports these research measurements of sertraline or norsertraline in human milk:

- Pooled analysis of 15 mothers: sertraline **45 mcg/L** on average, range **7–207 mcg/L**.
- Twenty-six mothers with 24-hour milk sampling: sertraline **129 mcg/L** on average, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1,498 mcg/L**.
- Four mothers at two months postpartum: sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L** in randomly timed samples.
- A case involving a preterm infant: randomly timed milk samples averaged **201 mcg/L sertraline** and **358 mcg/L norsertraline**.
- Foremilk and hindmilk sampled 13 hours after administration: **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively.
- First-week postpartum trough samples: sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one woman; among eight others, concentrations ranged from **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**.
- Six women providing random samples: median sertraline concentration **11.3 mcg/L**, range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**.
- Fifteen mothers providing serial samples: average **87 mcg/L**, range **12–288 mcg/L**.

### LactMed: observed infant outcomes

The returned LactMed record describes:

- No detectable acute adverse reactions in a group of **26 infants**.
- No clinical adverse reactions in several other small groups, including infants followed for growth in one study.
- One 4-month-old infant with benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation.
- A small study of SSRI-exposed infants reported blunted pain responses; four infants were exposed to sertraline, and the comparison group did not control for maternal depression.
- A case involving a **preterm infant** with hyperthermia, altered muscle tone, and high-pitched crying, alongside elevated infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite. LactMed reports that the authors attributed the episode to sertraline exposure in the context of prematurity and reduced metabolism. This is a case report and does not establish that the same outcome occurs in other infants.

LactMed also reports no adverse developmental effects in infants followed up to five years, but the retrieved record does not establish a complete study count. The Drug Levels and Effects in Breastfed Infants sections are truncated, so this is not a complete LactMed record. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
