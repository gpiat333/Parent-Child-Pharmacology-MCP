## Sertraline during lactation

### Measured exposure

LactMed reports numeric **milk concentrations** from several small studies:

- **45 mcg/L**; range **7–207 mcg/L**, from a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- **Sertraline: 129 mcg/L**; range **11–938 mcg/L**, and **norsertraline: 258 mcg/L**; range **20–1,498 mcg/L**, from extensive 24-hour milk sampling in 26 women.
- **Sertraline: 26.4 mcg/L** and **norsertraline: 29 mcg/L** in a report of four mothers sampled at two months postpartum.
- In a report involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L sertraline** and **358 mcg/L norsertraline** across three randomly timed samples.
- **29 mcg/L** in one participant, with sampling times unspecified.
- **58 mcg/L** in three participants, with sampling times unspecified.
- **26 mcg/L in foremilk** and **46 mcg/L in hindmilk**, sampled 13 hours after a dose.
- **<5 mcg/L** in one first-week postpartum trough sample; among eight other women in that report, milk concentrations ranged from **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**.
- Median concentration **11.3 mcg/L**, range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**, in six women providing random milk samples; the source notes that timing may affect accuracy.
- Average concentration **87 mcg/L**, range **12–288 mcg/L**, in 15 mothers providing samples at prespecified times.

These are measured milk concentrations, not infant outcomes and not treatment instructions. The retrieved LactMed extract does not provide a complete infant-level concentration section; infant-level information is therefore incomplete in this extract. ### Observed infant outcomes

Reported findings include:

- One **4-month-old infant** had benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation in a case report. The retrieved passage does not establish that sertraline caused these findings. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of **26 infants**, no acute adverse reactions were detected during the study period. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In a study of **14 mother–infant pairs**, infant whole-blood serotonin measurements were similar before and after maternal treatment; the report states that none of the infants experienced adverse effects. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11578995/)
- In a follow-up of **25 infants**, reported six-month weight gain was normal according to national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal effects. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- No clinical adverse reactions were noted in a study of **six infants aged 5–34 weeks**. [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367050/)
- No adverse effects were reported in **seven 4-week-old infants** in another report. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367055/)
- A small study compared pain responses among infants of mothers with depression who had prenatal and/or breastfeeding SSRI exposure with infants of nondepressed, unexposed mothers. Four of 30 exposed infants received sertraline, and the exposed group had blunted pain responses. Because the comparison group did not consist of depressed mothers without medication, the retrieved source states that the role of maternal depression-related factors could not be ruled out. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)
- An uncontrolled online survey of 930 breastfeeding mothers using antidepressants reported infant discontinuation-type symptoms in about 10% of infants overall; the result was not sertraline-specific and included pregnancy and lactation exposure groups. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20807106/)
- A case report described a **preterm infant born at 33 weeks** who developed hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying. The report also described infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite and attributed the event to serotonergic overstimulation associated with persistently high infant levels and reduced metabolism. This was a single case report involving prematurity and cannot establish frequency or risk in other infants. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)

The infant-outcome section in the retrieved LactMed record was truncated, so additional reported findings may be missing. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Lactation-related findings in the breastfeeding parent

LactMed describes a report in which six patients reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; the extract does not provide their administered regimens. It also describes a small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitors in which only one participant took sertraline; the study reported delayed onset of milk secretory activation among the inhibitor-exposed group, but no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day four. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

Other cited studies concerned antidepressants or SSRIs as groups rather than sertraline alone. Their breastfeeding outcomes therefore should not be treated as sertraline-specific. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The DailyMed label retrieved was for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective November 5, 2026. Its lactation section states that published literature shows low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk, reports a pooled analysis of 53 mother–infant pairs with no observed adverse reactions, and states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. The exact product match was not confirmed. The retrieved label extract included the adverse-reactions and lactation sections but did not include the expected warnings, warnings-and-precautions, boxed-warning, or drug-interactions sections; this lookup therefore cannot be treated as a complete label extract. ### Evidence limitations and context

- The LactMed evidence base includes small reports and studies described in the record as case reports, observational studies, and a meta-analysis; the total number of studies is not established in the retrieved extract. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The retrieved evidence combines different infant ages, breastfeeding extent, and term or preterm populations. Findings from the preterm case should not be generalized to all breastfed infants. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- A case report can describe a possible adverse event but cannot estimate how often it occurs. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- “No adverse effects observed” in small studies does not establish that uncommon or long-term effects are absent.
- Milk concentration is an exposure measurement, not proof of an infant clinical effect or proof of safety.
- The LactMed record was revised August 15, 2026; its retrieved package was published October 22, 2026, and the record notes that LactMed updates monthly. [S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)[S3](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pub/litarch/file_list.csv)
- The source context notes that maternal illness and treatment changes may also affect breastfeeding outcomes; this is context for interpretation, not a treatment recommendation. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)[S47](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
