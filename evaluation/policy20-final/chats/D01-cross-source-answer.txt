### Measured exposure

LactMed reported the following **milk concentrations**:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers, mean breast-milk sertraline concentration was **45 mcg/L**, with a range of **7–207 mcg/L**. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/)
- In a study of 26 women with extensive 24-hour milk and serum sampling, mean milk sertraline concentration was **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and mean norsertraline concentration was **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- At 2 months postpartum, four mothers had mean milk concentrations of **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline; samples were collected at random times after the previous administration.
- In one case involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline across three randomly timed sampling days. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- One study reported **26 mcg/L** in foremilk and **46 mcg/L** in hindmilk; samples were collected 13 hours after administration.
- In one woman, sertraline was below the breast-milk quantification limit of **<5 mcg/L**. In eight other women, milk concentrations ranged from **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**.
- In six women providing random milk samples, the median sertraline concentration was **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**).
- In 15 mothers providing timed milk samples, the mean concentration was **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).

These are research measurements, not dosing instructions. ### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reported findings from separate study populations:

- **26 breastfed infants:** no detectable acute adverse reactions were observed in the cited study. The study population and exposure findings are reported in the LactMed extract. [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- **14 mother–infant pairs:** infant whole-blood serotonin levels were measured before and after maternal treatment; the reported infant averages were similar, and no adverse effects were reported in those infants. [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11578995/)
- **25 breastfed infants followed through six months:** reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- **One 4-month-old infant:** benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation were reported. The returned extract does not establish that sertraline caused these findings.
- **One preterm infant born at 33 weeks:** hyperthermia, altered muscle tone, and high-pitched crying were reported after exposure through breastfeeding. The case report also reported measurable sertraline and metabolite concentrations in the infant specimen, but the returned LactMed extract does not provide the full infant-level measurement details. [S26](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- **A study of infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy, with or without breastfeeding exposure:** blunted pain responses were reported compared with the study’s control group. Four of the 30 exposed infants were exposed to sertraline; the returned extract states that the control group did not include depressed, unmedicated mothers, so the specific contribution of sertraline was not established. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)

### Product-label evidence

The selected DailyMed label states that published literature shows low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk. In a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs**, exclusively human-milk-fed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to **2%** of the maternal serum levels, with a reported range of **0–15%**; the label states that no adverse reactions were observed in those infants. This is label-reported exposure and outcome information, not an individualized safety determination. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

The label’s returned adult adverse-reaction section lists hypersensitivity reactions, serotonin syndrome, increased bleeding risk, activation of mania or hypomania, discontinuation syndrome, seizures, angle-closure glaucoma, hyponatremia, and sexual dysfunction, among other listed reactions. These are findings and warnings for the medicine-taker label population, not automatically observed effects in a breastfed infant. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
