### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The label distinguishes **disease-associated maternal/embryofetal risk** from medication exposure. It states that epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, has been associated with preeclampsia, preterm labor, antepartum and postpartum hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. It also states that maternal or fetal injury may be greatest with untreated or poorly controlled convulsive seizures. These are label-described risks associated with epilepsy, not lamotrigine-specific adverse outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The retrieved label extract did not contain the selected product’s standalone adult adverse-reaction, warnings, or warnings-and-precautions sections; those sections are **missing in the exported data**. Therefore, maternal adverse effects cannot be comprehensively summarized from this label extract.

A pregnancy review describes the evidence base as relying substantially on pregnancy registries, cohort studies, case-control studies, and prospective cohorts because no large randomized safety trials were identified for this subgroup. Its population was people with epilepsy during pregnancy and lactation; the design label in the returned record was **review**, not a primary outcome study. [S24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37684052/)

### Maternal pharmacokinetic context

The label reports that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is label context for clinician discussion, not an individualized dosing recommendation. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

For **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy** exposures, the label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 infants**; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that the frequency of major congenital malformations was similar to estimates from the general population. These source populations were pregnancy-registry populations, predominantly involving women with epilepsy, and the reported exposures were lamotrigine monotherapy. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

For isolated oral clefts, the NAAED registry reported **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants** exposed early in pregnancy, described in the label as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls; 95% CI 1.4–6.3. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control study based on 21 congenital-anomaly registries reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45; 95% CI 0.8–2.63. These are separate source findings with different comparators and populations. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small for gestational age, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk of these outcomes with lamotrigine monotherapy, while stating that differences in outcome definition, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This is a label summary; the returned extract did not provide the meta-analyses’ study counts or full study-population details. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A separate study of psychotropic medications included pregnancy, lactation, neonate, and child populations; its abstract reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant developmental differences during the first 12 months. The returned abstract did not establish a lamotrigine-specific sample size or lamotrigine-specific outcome estimate, so those findings should not be generalized as lamotrigine-only results. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

Animal findings in the label included developmental toxicity, neurobehavioral abnormalities, offspring mortality, and decreased fetal folate concentrations in rats and other animals. These are animal findings and are not observed human fetal or infant outcomes. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
