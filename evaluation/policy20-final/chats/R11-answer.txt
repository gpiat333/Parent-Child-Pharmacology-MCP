**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design and population
- The abstract does not state a formal study-design label. It reports that breastfeeding women with epilepsy treated with levetiracetam completed a questionnaire and provided serial breast-milk samples from before dosing through 12 hours afterward, plus one pre-dose maternal serum sample.
- The study included **20 women and 21 infants**; one woman had twins. Sixteen infants were fully breastfed and five were partially breastfed. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Drug exposure
- Levetiracetam concentrations were measured in breast milk and maternal serum.
- The reported trough breast-milk/serum ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- The abstract reports infant exposure estimates and relative infant-dose values separately for fully and partially breastfed infants; these are study findings, not dosing instructions.
- Correlations were reported among levetiracetam dose, maternal serum concentrations, breast-milk trough concentrations and breast-milk AUC values. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Infant outcomes
- Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants; the abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding.
- The abstract states that all infants gained weight according to age.
- The abstract does not establish that levetiracetam caused the reported somnolence; it reports the authors’ statement that the reports could be related to breast-milk exposure. [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)
