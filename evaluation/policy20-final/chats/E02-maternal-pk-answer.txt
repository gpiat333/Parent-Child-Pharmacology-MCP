### Fetal exposure

No quantitative maternal-to-fetal lamotrigine concentration measurements were retrieved.

The label’s **Clinical Considerations** state that physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reported decreases during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. The returned passage does not provide concentration values, gestational-stage measurements, clearance estimates, or a complete human pharmacokinetic dataset. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** measured psychotropic-medication concentrations in serum and breastmilk and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its abstract reported a high breastmilk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine, but this is lactation exposure evidence—not a measurement of fetal exposure—and the retrieved abstract does not provide a numeric lamotrigine concentration. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Observed fetal and infant outcomes

The selected DailyMed label reports the following registry findings for **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy**:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 infants**; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label separately reports that the NAAED Pregnancy Registry observed isolated oral clefts among **2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy** at **3.2 per 1,000**; it describes this as a threefold increase versus unexposed healthy controls. The label also states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control analysis reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**; 95% CI 0.8–2.63. These are separate source findings and should not be combined into one estimate. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It states that differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit the conclusions. The retrieved extract does not provide the underlying meta-analysis study counts or detailed results; those fields are missing in the returned material. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT registry record states in its summary that the study found no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with other defects after in-utero lamotrigine exposure. The record has no posted results, so the registry summary is not a substitute for a full results report. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

The naturalistic study of 60 mothers reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. The retrieved abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific analysis for those outcomes. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Maternal pharmacokinetic evidence

- **Clinical Considerations:** The label reports possible pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, decreased concentrations during pregnancy, and restoration toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. These are source-label statements, not individualized treatment instructions. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **Literature:** A pregnancy review states that physiological changes affect antiseizure-medication levels and discusses serum-concentration monitoring, but the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific numerical PK results. [S20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)
- A review specifically covering lamotrigine pharmacokinetics in pregnancy was retrieved, but its abstract describes the review scope and does not report lamotrigine-specific numerical PK findings. [S21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573557/)
- The 60-mother naturalistic study measured serum and breastmilk concentrations, but the retrieved abstract does not provide numeric lamotrigine concentration values or fetal concentrations. [S23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

**Interpretation of the retrieved PK evidence:** The returned material supports a reported change in maternal lamotrigine concentrations across pregnancy and postpartum, but does not supply a complete, lamotrigine-specific human PK dataset or an individualized concentration target.
