LactMed’s amoxicillin entry reports these findings in breastfed infants:

### Observed infant findings
- A telephone follow-up of **25 nursing mothers** recorded diarrhea in **3 infants**; the report recorded no rash or candidiasis in that group.[4] [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8498418/)
- In a prospective study of **40 infants exposed to amoxicillin in breastmilk**, **2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash**.[6] [S4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16044108/)
- In a study comparing infants exposed to amoxicillin with infants exposed to a macrolide antibiotic, reported reactions occurred in **8.3%** of the amoxicillin-exposed infants and included **rash and somnolence**.[7] [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19366316/)
- A case report described recurrent generalized urticaria in a **2-month-old** after breastfeeding while the mother was receiving **amoxicillin/clavulanic acid and gentamicin**. LactMed states that the causative drug could not be determined and that the combination product was considered the most likely cause.[8] [S2](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1472-8206.2009.00689.x)
- A report described **mild diarrhea** in an **11-month-old** whose mother was taking amoxicillin together with acetaminophen plus codeine; the relationship was rated possible or probable.[9] [S7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40183105/)
- In a study of 25 breastfeeding mothers whose infants were older than 4 weeks, **no infant adverse effects were reported**.[3] [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41582095/)

### Symptoms monitored in a study
One controlled prospective study asked mothers to record tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool-frequency or stool-consistency changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. The study found no statistical differences in these parameters between infants of control mothers and infants of mothers taking the related antibiotics ampicillin or ampicillin-clavulanate.[5] [S1](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2125.1991.tb05523.x)

This was a study measurement approach, not a separate LactMed recommendation to monitor those symptoms.

### Measured exposure
- Reported **amoxicillin concentrations in milk** averaged **0.69 mg/L** at 4 hours, with a range of **0.46–0.88 mg/L**, and **0.81 mg/L** at 5 hours, with a range of **0.39–1.3 mg/L**, in a study of 6 women. [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7246123/)
- Relevant published information on **infant levels** was not found as of the LactMed revision date. [S13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Uncertainty
LactMed states that rash and disruption of the infant’s gastrointestinal flora—resulting in diarrhea or thrush—have been reported, but that these effects have **not been adequately evaluated**. The reports involve different study populations, comparators, and, in the urticaria case, more than one maternal medicine; the LactMed extract does not establish that amoxicillin caused every reported symptom. The record’s study count is not established in the exported data, and the extract does not provide a complete evidence inventory. [S13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
