## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Identity:** The medication-evidence lookup matched **sertraline** exactly as a single ingredient. [S53](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed reports observations of decreased milk supply after starting sertraline in six patients; maternal doses were not reported. One patient reported increased supply after stopping and decreased supply after restarting, but this is a single reported observation and does not establish causation. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor exposure—including one participant taking sertraline—reported delayed lactogenesis II compared with controls, while the extract states that feeding difficulties after postpartum day 4 did not differ statistically. This was not a sertraline-only study. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed label states that published data show low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk and that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. The selected label was **“SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]”**, effective November 5, 2026; this was one selected product label, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured exposure

Retrieved LactMed milk-concentration findings included:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L**; range **7–207 mcg/L** in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a study of 26 breastfeeding women, milk sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L**; range **11–938 mcg/L**. Norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L**; range **20–1,498 mcg/L**. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- At two months postpartum, average milk concentrations in four mothers were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a case involving a preterm infant, breastmilk sertraline and norsertraline averaged **201 mcg/L** and **358 mcg/L**, respectively, at random sampling times. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One report found **26 mcg/L** in foremilk and **46 mcg/L** in hindmilk, sampled 13 hours after exposure. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other reported milk measurements included **<5 mcg/L** in one woman, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight women, a median of **11.3 mcg/L** in six women, and an average of **87 mcg/L** with a range of **12–288 mcg/L** in another study. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These are research concentration measurements, not personal dosing guidance. The returned LactMed extract did **not** include an infant-level concentration excerpt; infant-level information was therefore incomplete in that extract. A separately retrieved PubMed abstract reported detectable concentrations of sertraline in three nursing infants and desmethylsertraline in six, using **infant serum** as the specimen, with detected infant serum concentrations below the detection limit of most commercial laboratories. [S52](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in one 4-month-old infant and spontaneously resolving agitation in that case passage. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The extract reports several small groups in which no acute adverse reactions or other adverse effects were observed during the reported study periods. These observations do not establish that unaddressed outcomes were absent. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In one case involving a preterm infant born at 33 weeks, the reported outcomes included hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation disorders, and high-pitched crying. The case also reported infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite and later reported dissipation of symptoms and decreased serum levels after breastfeeding was discontinued. The source’s attribution was a case-report interpretation; this single case should not be generalized beyond its reported population. [S30](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- Another passage describes sedation in an infant whose mother was also taking zopiclone and occasional alprazolam; the co-exposures are part of that source record. [S29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22504099/)
- A pain-response study included four sertraline-exposed infants among 30 infants exposed to an SSRI. The source stated that the comparison group did not include depressed, nonmedicated mothers, so the role of maternal depression-related factors was not resolved. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15687451/)

The returned **Effects in Breastfed Infants** section was truncated, including an incomplete passage from a case involving sertraline with methylphenidate. The missing text cannot be reconstructed from this lookup. [S56](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
