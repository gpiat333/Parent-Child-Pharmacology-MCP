## LactMed: breastfed-infant evidence

LactMed reports measured exposure and observed infant findings separately. [S23](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### Measured exposure

Reported **milk metformin concentrations** included:

- **270 mcg/L** as an average over 24 hours in one group.
- Approximately **380 mcg/L** in one sample collected about 4–5 hours after administration.
- **130 and 270 mcg/L** as average milk levels in two women over 72 hours.
- **130–270 mcg/L** as average levels in another group.
- **410 mcg/L** as an average milk level in a postpartum study.

Infant-level information was also retrieved: LactMed reports **infant serum** measurements, including undetectable results below the assay thresholds and measured concentrations of **50 and 80 mcg/L** in two infants. In another group, metformin was undetectable in infant serum at **<5 mcg/L**. Some infant ages and extent of nursing were not stated. [S23](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed reports that:

- Seven infants aged 5–25 months were judged healthy, with growth and development progressing as expected; two had normal Denver Developmental Screening tests.
- Three infants had no detectable adverse effects attributed to metformin.
- In three young breastfed infants, no low blood glucose was reported, and their mothers reported no adverse reactions.
- In a study of infants who were predominantly breastfed or formula-fed, no differences were found at the reported follow-up points in height, weight, motor-social development, or rates of illness.

These are findings from separate cited reports and study populations, not a single combined study result. LactMed’s returned extract does not establish a study count; its ten reference entries are not a study count. Relevant citations listed by LactMed include [S11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12436333/), [S12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12545145/), [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15384618/), [S14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15932841/), and [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737874/). The individual article abstracts were not separately retrieved here, so these findings are reported from LactMed’s summaries rather than from independent abstract review.
