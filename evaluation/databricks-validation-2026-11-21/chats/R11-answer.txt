**Paper:** *Levetiracetam in lactation: How much is excreted into human breast milk?* (PMID 34131926) — **Journal Article; source population: lactation and infant**. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Study design
The study assessed levetiracetam excretion into human breast milk and its relationship to maternal dose and serum concentrations. Researchers collected serial breastmilk samples from before dosing through 12 hours afterward, plus one pre-dose maternal serum sample; women also completed a questionnaire. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Population
- 20 breastfeeding women with epilepsy
- 21 infants, including one set of twins
- 16 fully breastfed infants and 5 partially breastfed infants
- Infant ages, gestational ages, and prematurity status were not provided in the returned abstract. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Measured drug exposure
- The trough breastmilk-to-maternal-serum levetiracetam ratio was **0.98 ± 0.20**.
- The abstract reported infant levetiracetam exposure estimates and relative infant doses of **13.8 ± 3.1%** for fully breastfed infants and **6.9 ± 1.6%** for partially breastfed infants.
- Maternal levetiracetam dose, maternal serum concentrations, breastmilk trough concentrations, and breastmilk exposure over time were substantially correlated. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

### Observed infant outcomes
- Three women reported somnolence in their fully breastfed infants. The abstract states that this resolved shortly after switching to partial breastfeeding; the temporal relationship does not establish causation.
- All infants gained weight according to age.
- No other specific infant clinical outcomes were reported in the retrieved abstract. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34131926/)

The abstract states that further studies are needed to evaluate short- and long-term outcomes. This small study’s findings should not be generalized beyond its reported participants and outcomes.
