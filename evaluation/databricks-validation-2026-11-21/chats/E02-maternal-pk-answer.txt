## 1. Effects on the pregnant person

- Retrieved reviews describe pregnancy management as balancing maternal seizure control with fetal outcomes. They also describe seizure-related and disease-related risks as relevant context. These are review-level statements, not individualized findings about a particular person. - No specific maternal adverse-outcome estimate attributable to lamotrigine was established in the retrieved extract.
- The selected label states that pregnancy-related physiological changes may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect. It reports decreased concentrations during pregnancy and return toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is source pharmacokinetic context, not an individualized medication plan. ## 3. Observed fetal and infant outcomes

### Major congenital malformations

The selected label reports registry observations among **first-trimester lamotrigine-monotherapy exposures**:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: 2.2% of 1,558 exposed infants.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: 2.0% of 1,562 exposed infants.
- EURAP: 2.9% of 2,514 exposures.

The label states that these frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. These are observations from different registry populations and should not be treated as a personalized risk estimate. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Isolated oral clefts

- The NAAED registry reported 3.2 oral-cleft cases per 1,000 among 2,200 early-pregnancy exposures and described an increased risk versus unexposed healthy controls.
- The label states that this signal was not observed in other large international registries.
- A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45, with a confidence interval including no association.

Thus, the retrieved evidence is inconsistent regarding this specific outcome; causation was not established by the lookup. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Other fetal and infant outcomes

The label states that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, while noting that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit conclusions. This qualified statement is not proof that no risk exists. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** measured psychotropic medications during pregnancy and lactation and followed exposed children for the first 12 months. Its abstract reports no significant differences in birth outcomes or first-year development between exposed and nonexposed newborns, but the returned population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children, and the abstract did not provide enough lamotrigine-specific detail to attribute every result solely to lamotrigine. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
