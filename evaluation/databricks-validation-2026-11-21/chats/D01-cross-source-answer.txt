## Sertraline evidence

### Measured milk and infant exposure — LactMed

LactMed reports that sertraline is measurable in breast milk, while estimated relative infant exposure in the cited studies was generally low—commonly about **0.2%–2.4% of the maternal weight-adjusted amount**, with several estimates near 1%. Norsertraline, the active metabolite, was also detected in milk and was more often detectable in infant serum than sertraline. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

Infant serum measurements were often undetectable, although some infants had detectable sertraline or norsertraline. One reported preterm infant had measurable concentrations of both compounds in the context of symptoms resembling neonatal abstinence; prematurity and genetically intermediate metabolism were reported as relevant context. This case does not establish that sertraline caused the symptoms. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These are **measured or estimated exposure findings**, not clinical outcomes or treatment instructions.

### Observed infant outcomes — LactMed

Reported observations included:

- No acute adverse reactions in one observational group of 26 breastfed infants.
- No adverse effects reported in several other small groups.
- Normal reported weight gain and no abnormal effects in one follow-up group.
- One report of benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation.
- A separate report of sedation involved exposure to sertraline together with other medicines, so the contribution of sertraline alone was not established. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
