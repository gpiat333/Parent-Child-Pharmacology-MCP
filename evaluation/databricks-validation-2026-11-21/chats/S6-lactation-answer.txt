## Sertraline during lactation

**Overall evidence:** LactMed reports generally low sertraline transfer into milk and frequent absence of measurable sertraline in infant serum, although norsertraline may be detectable. This is exposure evidence, not proof of clinical safety. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

- In small milk-sampling studies, sertraline and norsertraline were measurable in milk, with concentrations varying by study, sampling time, and foremilk versus hindmilk.
- Several studies estimated relatively low infant exposure, but estimates were based on small samples, variable sampling methods, or pharmacokinetic modeling. One modeling study used data from 38 mothers and simulated levels in 2,000 patients; the simulated population is not the same as a directly observed infant cohort. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Infant serum concentrations were often undetectable. In one study of **30 breastfed infants**—19 exclusively breastfed and 11 breastfed at least half the time—22 had undetectable sertraline; 8 had detectable levels. In another study of **22 breastfed infants**, 4 had detectable sertraline and 11 had detectable norsertraline. These populations were small and had differing maternal treatment and breastfeeding characteristics. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Milk or infant-serum detection indicates exposure; it is not itself an observed adverse outcome.

### Observed infant outcomes

- In a study of **26 breastfed infants**, no acute adverse reactions were detected during the study. The source population consisted of infants breastfeeding for at least 3 weeks whose mothers were being treated for severe depression. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a small follow-up of **25 infants**, reported weight gain through 6 months was within national growth standards and mothers reported no abnormal effects. This was a small observational report relying partly on parental reporting. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A case report described benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation in one 4-month-old infant. The report does not establish that sertraline caused these events. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate report described sedation in an infant whose mother was also taking other medicines, including sedating medicines; the contribution of sertraline could not be isolated. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One small study reported blunted pain responses among infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy alone or during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Only 4 of the 30 infants were exposed to sertraline. The comparison group consisted of infants of nondepressed, unexposed mothers, so the study could not separate medication effects from effects associated with maternal depression or other group differences. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- LactMed describes one **33-week preterm infant** with symptoms resembling neonatal abstinence and measurable sertraline and norsertraline. The source also reported genetically intermediate metabolism. This was a single preterm case and does not establish the same risk for other infants. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The LactMed summary states that no developmental adverse effects were found in infants followed up to 5 years, but the retrieved extract does not establish the total study count, and its infant-effects section is truncated. This should not be interpreted as proof that uncommon or long-term effects have been fully excluded. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Source context and limitations

- The evidence base described in the retrieved LactMed extract includes case reports, observational studies, pooled analyses, and meta-analysis material; the exact study count is **not established from this extract**. A reference count is not a study count. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The studies differ in infant age, extent of breastfeeding, maternal illness, sampling time, and whether exposure occurred during pregnancy as well as lactation. Findings from preterm infants should not be generalized to term infants, and findings from pregnancy-plus-lactation exposure should not be attributed solely to breast milk. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The LactMed section on **Effects in Breastfed Infants** was truncated, and the section on **Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk** was omitted by the retrieval limit. Therefore, this is not a complete record. [S10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The retrieved DailyMed record is for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective November 5, 2026. Its adverse-reaction section concerns the medicine-taking label population, not automatically breastfed infants. The exact product match was not confirmed, and several expected label sections were not returned. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)
- The label context included a study of 201 pregnant women with a history of major depression in which discontinuation of antidepressants was associated with more depressive relapse than continuation.
