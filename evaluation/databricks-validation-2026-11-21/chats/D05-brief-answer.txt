## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Identity:** Sertraline was matched exactly in RxNorm. The lookup concerns sertraline as a single ingredient, not combination-product safety. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Lactating person

The selected DailyMed record is **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, SPL version 13, effective November 5, 2026. Its lactation section reports low levels of sertraline and metabolites in human milk and states that data on effects on milk production are unavailable. This is one product label, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Measured infant exposure

- LactMed summarizes studies measuring sertraline and norsertraline in breast milk and infant serum. Reported infant exposure varied with sampling time, milk fraction and maternal exposure; norsertraline was often detectable at low levels in infant serum. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A 1997 journal study of **12 lactating women and 11 mother–infant pairs** measured milk and infant serum concentrations. The returned metadata labels its source population as mixed **pregnancy, lactation and neonate**; it should not be generalized beyond that population. Detectable sertraline was reported in three nursing infants and desmethylsertraline in six. The abstract reported no observed adverse effects in those infants. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
- LactMed also describes a preterm-infant case with measured infant serum exposure and symptoms temporally associated with exposure. The returned extract is incomplete, so the exact subsequent serum values are missing; the report does not establish causation or a population risk. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed’s lactation-only evidence includes small observational groups and case reports. It reports one infant with benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation, while other small groups reported no acute adverse reactions. These findings are observations, not proof of cause or proof of safety. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A small pain-response study included infants exposed to SSRIs prenatally alone or prenatally and through breast milk; four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline. The returned LactMed text states that lack of a depressed, untreated comparison group prevented separation of medication effects from effects related to maternal depression. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The returned LactMed extract mentions an uncontrolled survey of 930 breastfeeding mothers taking antidepressants, in which discontinuation-type symptoms were reported in about 10% of infants. The extract does not establish a sertraline-specific rate or causation. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- One sedation report involved sertraline together with other sedating medicines, so the observed sedation cannot be attributed to sertraline alone. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Evidence limitations

- The LactMed extract is truncated: **“Effects on Lactation and Breastmilk”** was omitted by the retrieval limit, and **“Effects in Breastfed Infants”** was also truncated. The tool therefore did not return a complete LactMed record. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The tool did not establish a study count; reference counts are not study counts. A no-effects statement is not evidence that the population is well studied or that safety is established. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The PubMed search was a bounded relevance search, not a systematic review; its result count does not establish completeness or absence of other evidence. [S8](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22sertraline%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22sertraline%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Lactation%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Breast+Feeding%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22Milk%2C+Human%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+lactat%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfeed%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+breastfed%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- Returned literature metadata often had source population **“not established”** or missing age/prematurity information. Where a source population was supplied, mixed labels such as pregnancy, lactation and neonate were retained rather than generalized.
- The selected DailyMed record is formulation-specific, its pregnancy section was truncated, and its lactation warning code was flagged by the tool as a source title/code mismatch requiring verification.
