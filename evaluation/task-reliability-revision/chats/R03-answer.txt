## Metformin: adult label warnings compared with breastfed-infant findings

### Adult medicine-taker label

The selected DailyMed product is **METFORMIN HCL TABLET [QPHARMA, INC.]**. Its adult **Warnings** section describes lactic acidosis as a rare but serious complication and associates reported cases particularly with renal dysfunction, renal hypoperfusion, hypoxemia, dehydration, sepsis, hepatic disease, older age, and certain cardiac conditions. The same selected label reports adult gastrointestinal adverse reactions including diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, indigestion, and abdominal discomfort. These findings concern the person taking metformin, not breastfed infants. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

The label’s **Lactation** section says animal studies found metformin in milk and that comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers. It raises potential infant hypoglycemia as a concern; this is a label warning, not an observed infant outcome reported in that section. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### LactMed: breastfed-infant findings

LactMed reports findings from several different populations and study types:

- **Seven infants aged 5–25 months** were judged healthy, with expected growth and development; two had normal Denver developmental screening results. The study type is not specified in the returned metadata; the source population is breastfed infants, with breastfeeding extent and exposure timing incompletely specified. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Three infants aged 2, 5, and 14 months** had no detectable adverse effects reported. The study type is not specified in the returned metadata. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- **Three breastfed infants aged 10–11 days postpartum** had no reported low blood glucose, and their mothers reported no infant adverse reactions. The study type is not specified in the returned metadata; the extent of breastfeeding was not stated. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
- A **6-month, nonrandomized prospective study** followed **111 infants**: 61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed. The source reported no differences in height, weight, motor-social development, or illness rates at 3 and 6 months between those groups. This was a mixed population, not an exclusively breastfed-infant study. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

These are reported observations in the specific study populations above; they do not convert the adult label warnings into infant outcomes or establish an individual treatment decision.

### Measured exposure, separately from outcomes

LactMed reported metformin concentrations in milk of:

- **270 mcg/L** averaged over 24 hours in six women;
- approximately **380 mcg/L** in one woman at about 4–5 hours after administration;
- **130–270 mcg/L** in other lactating-woman studies; and
- **410 mcg/L** in a postpartum study.

Infant **serum** measurements were also reported: some were below assay detection limits, while other samples were **50 and 80 mcg/L**; another set was below **5 mcg/L**. Infant ages and extent of nursing were not stated for some measurements. These are research exposure measurements, not administered doses, recommendations, or clinical outcomes. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
