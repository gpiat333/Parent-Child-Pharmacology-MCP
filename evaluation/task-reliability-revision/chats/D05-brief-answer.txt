## Evidence brief: sertraline during lactation

**Evidence status:** Sertraline identity was matched exactly in RxNorm. This is a partial, bounded evidence retrieval—not a safety verdict or individualized treatment decision. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed includes reports of decreased milk supply after starting sertraline, including one report in which supply reportedly increased after stopping and decreased after restarting. These are limited observations and do not establish causation.
- A small study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitors, including one sertraline-exposed participant, reported delayed onset of milk secretory activation by an average of 16.7 hours; the retrieved passage states that feeding difficulties after day 4 did not differ statistically between groups.
- Broader antidepressant/SSRI studies in LactMed were not always sertraline-specific and should not be attributed solely to sertraline.
- The selected DailyMed product label states that there are no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production. It includes an adult adverse-reaction section, but the full sections for warnings, warnings and precautions, boxed warning, and drug interactions were missing from this retrieval. The label was **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective date November 5, 2026; it represents one selected product label, not all formulations. ### Measured exposure in milk and infant specimens

Retrieved LactMed milk measurements included:

- Sertraline: **45 mcg/L** average, range **7–207 mcg/L**, in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- In a 26-woman intensive-sampling study: sertraline **129 mcg/L** average, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline **258 mcg/L** average, range **20–1498 mcg/L**.
- Other reported milk concentrations included sertraline **26.4 mcg/L**, **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, foremilk **26 mcg/L**, hindmilk **46 mcg/L**, **<5 mcg/L**, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** with range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**, and **87 mcg/L** with range **12–288 mcg/L**.
- The returned LactMed concentration extract was incomplete and did not include a full infant-level concentration section. A PubMed abstract for one study reported detectable sertraline in three nursing infants and detectable desmethylsertraline in six; the reported infant serum concentrations were below the detection limit of most commercial laboratories.

These are research measurements, not administered doses or dosing advice. Milk concentrations do not establish safety. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- One case report described benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in a 4-month-old infant and agitation that resolved spontaneously.
- Several small cohorts reported no acute clinical adverse reactions, no abnormal effects reported by mothers, or normal six-month weight gain in the studied infants.
- A small study of infants exposed to an SSRI prenatally and/or through breast milk reported blunted pain responses; only four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline, and maternal depression-related factors could not be ruled out.
- An uncontrolled survey of 930 breastfeeding mothers taking antidepressants reported infant drug-discontinuation symptoms in about 10% of infants; this result was not sertraline-specific.
- A separate case report involved a preterm infant born at 33 weeks whose symptoms and high infant serum concentrations were reported in the context of maternal sertraline exposure, reduced infant metabolism, and breastfeeding. The source attributed the reaction to sertraline, but this is a single case report and does not establish frequency or individual risk.
- The abstract for PMID 9286185 states that no adverse effects of exposure were observed in the infants studied.

The LactMed infant-effects section was truncated, so some later outcome details are missing. These reports should not be generalized beyond their stated populations. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)
