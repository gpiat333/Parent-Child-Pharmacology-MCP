## Sertraline during lactation: balanced evidence summary

Sertraline identity was verified as the ingredient sertraline (RxNorm RxCUI 36437). [S9](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Measured exposure

LactMed reports small, generally variable pharmacokinetic studies measuring sertraline and norsertraline in human milk:

- In pooled data from **15 mothers**, mean milk sertraline was **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**).
- In a 24-hour sampling study of **26 women**, mean milk sertraline was **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and mean norsertraline was **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- Other small studies reported sertraline milk concentrations of **26.4 mcg/L**, **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**), and **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**).
- In one first-week postpartum study, sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one participant and **11.1–24.5 mcg/L** in eight others; the source noted that collection timing could affect the estimates.
- Foremilk and hindmilk samples in one report contained **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively.

These are **milk-concentration measurements**, not clinical outcomes. The returned extract did not provide a complete infant-level concentration table; it states that infant sertraline is usually not detected in serum, while norsertraline may be detectable at low levels. The LactMed **Drug Levels** section was truncated, so additional measurements may be missing. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

The retrieved LactMed record describes several small studies and reports:

- **26 infants:** no detectable acute adverse reactions were observed in the study.
- **14 mother–infant pairs:** infant whole-blood serotonin measurements were similar before and after maternal therapy; no adverse effects were reported in those infants.
- **25 infants:** reported six-month weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal effects.
- **Six infants aged 5–34 weeks:** no clinical adverse reactions were noted at the time of the study.
- **One 4-month-old infant:** benign sleep myoclonus and transient agitation were reported. The source does not establish that sertraline caused either finding.
- **One infant in a mixed-medication exposure report:** sedation was reported when the mother was also taking other medicines, including zopiclone and occasional alprazolam. This does not isolate a sertraline effect.
- **One preterm-infant case:** the mother used sertraline during gestation, at delivery, and postpartum while exclusively breastfeeding. Her infant, born at 33 weeks, developed hyperthermia, abnormal muscle-tone regulation, and high-pitched crying during the first 24 hours; symptoms worsened by day 4 while breastfeeding continued. On day 5, infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite were reported. Breastfeeding was discontinued on day 9; the source reports that symptoms then dissipated and serum levels decreased. The infant was later found to have genetically intermediate metabolism of two relevant CYP450 enzymes. The report attributed the reaction to serotonergic overstimulation, but this is a single case involving prematurity, pregnancy exposure, postpartum exposure, and unusual metabolism; it does not establish risk for other infants.
- A small study of infants of depressed mothers reported blunted pain responses among infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy and/or breastfeeding. Only four infants received sertraline, and the comparison group consisted of infants of nondepressed mothers; the source said an effect related to maternal depression could not be excluded.
- An uncontrolled survey of **930 mothers** reported discontinuation-type symptoms in about 10% of infants exposed to antidepressants collectively, not sertraline specifically.

The Effects in Breastfed Infants section was also truncated. These findings therefore should not be treated as a complete list of reported outcomes. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Lactation and milk-production findings

These should be kept separate from drug concentrations in milk:

- LactMed reports observations from six patients who perceived decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; important details, including administered regimens, were not reported.
- A small prospective study included eight women taking an SRI, only one of whom took sertraline, and compared them with 423 women not taking an SRI. Lactogenesis was delayed on average in the SRI group, but the study did not find a statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day four.
- Larger observational studies concerned antidepressants or SSRIs collectively rather than sertraline alone. Their results may also reflect underlying illness and treatment history, so they do not establish that sertraline caused the breastfeeding outcomes.
- The selected DailyMed label states that data on sertraline’s effects on milk production are unavailable. The selected product was **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**; exact product matching was not confirmed. Its effective date was **November 5, 2026**. ### Evidence limitations and source context

The returned LactMed evidence includes small pharmacokinetic studies, observational studies, surveys, and case reports; it does not establish a study count. Sample timing varied, some studies lacked controls, and rare or long-term outcomes may be missed. “No adverse effects observed” in a small cohort is not proof that adverse effects never occur.

The DailyMed label reports a pooled analysis of **53 mother–infant pairs** in which exclusively human-milk-fed infants had average sertraline serum levels equal to 2% of maternal levels and no observed adverse reactions. This is label evidence for the selected product, not proof of safety for every formulation or infant. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

Adult label adverse reactions are findings concerning the medicine-taking population and should not be presented as infant outcomes.
