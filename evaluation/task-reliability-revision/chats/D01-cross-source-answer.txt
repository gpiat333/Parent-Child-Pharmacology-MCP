## Sertraline during breastfeeding

### Measured milk exposure

LactMed reports the following breast-milk concentrations:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers: sertraline **45 mcg/L** on average, range **7–207 mcg/L**.
- In 26 women studied with extensive 24-hour sampling: sertraline **129 mcg/L** on average, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline **258 mcg/L**, range **20–1,498 mcg/L**. Concentrations were higher in hindmilk than foremilk.
- In four mothers at 2 months postpartum: sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L** at random sampling times.
- In a case involving a preterm infant: breast-milk sertraline and norsertraline averaged **201 mcg/L** and **358 mcg/L**, respectively, at random times.
- In one study, milk sertraline was **<5 mcg/L** in one participant; among eight others, levels ranged from **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**.
- In six mothers’ random milk samples: median sertraline **11.3 mcg/L**, range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**.
- In 15 mothers with repeated sampling: average milk sertraline **87 mcg/L**, range **12–288 mcg/L**.
- One paired sampling study reported **26 mcg/L** in foremilk and **46 mcg/L** in hindmilk.

These are measured milk concentrations, not administered doses or dosing instructions. ### Observed infant outcomes

LactMed’s returned extract describes:

- One 4-month-old infant with benign sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation.
- No detectable acute adverse reactions in a study of 26 infants.
- No reported adverse effects in a study measuring whole-blood serotonin in 14 mother–infant pairs.
- Reported normal 6-month weight gain and no abnormal effects in a follow-up group of 25 infants.
- No clinically noted adverse reactions in six infants aged 5–34 weeks and no adverse effects reported in seven 4-week-old infants.
- A small study reported altered pain responses among infants exposed to an SSRI during pregnancy and/or breastfeeding; four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline, and the comparison did not control for maternal depression.
- One preterm infant developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation problems, and high-pitched crying. The source also reported infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite in the adult therapeutic range and described the reaction as probably caused by sertraline. This was a single case involving prematurity and genetically intermediate metabolism.
- One report described sedation in an infant whose exposure included sertraline and other medicines; the contribution of sertraline alone was not established.
