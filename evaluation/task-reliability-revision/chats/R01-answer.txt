LactMed’s amoxicillin entry reports:

### Measured exposure
- In **6 lactating women**, average amoxicillin concentrations in milk were **0.69 mg/L** (range **0.46–0.88 mg/L**) at 4 hours and **0.81 mg/L** (range **0.39–1.3 mg/L**) at 5 hours after administration.
- **Infant-level measurements were not found in the published information as of LactMed’s revision date.** Milk concentrations are exposure measurements, not evidence of an infant symptom or proof of safety. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Observed infant symptoms
- **Telephone follow-up; 25 nursing mothers:** 3 mothers reported diarrhea in their infants; no rash or candidiasis was reported in that group.
- **Prospective controlled study; 40 breastfed infants exposed through milk:** 2 developed diarrhea and 1 developed a rash.
- **Comparison study; breastfed infants of mothers taking amoxicillin:** adverse reactions were reported in 8.3%; reported reactions included rash and somnolence.
- **Case report; 2-month-old breastfed infant:** after breastfeeding began, generalized urticaria appeared about 20 minutes later and disappeared after 30 minutes. When the infant breastfed again several hours later, urticaria reappeared after 15 minutes and then disappeared after an hour. The mother had received amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin, so the specific causative drug could not be determined.
- **Report involving an 11-month-old breastfed infant:** mild diarrhea was reported while the mother was taking amoxicillin plus acetaminophen/codeine; the relationship was rated possible or probable.
- **Study of 25 mothers with infants older than 4 weeks:** no infant adverse effects were reported. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Advice to monitor
The retrieved LactMed text describes a study in which mothers recorded tongue coating, feeding difficulty, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice. That was a **study procedure**, not evidence that those symptoms occurred and not an explicit LactMed recommendation to monitor them. No separate monitoring recommendation was retrieved. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Uncertainty
LactMed states that reports of rash and gastrointestinal effects such as diarrhea or thrush have **not been adequately evaluated**. The evidence includes small observational studies and a case report; the returned record does not establish a study count. Some reports involve co-exposures or incomplete information, so reported symptoms do not prove that amoxicillin caused them. Conversely, the absence of reported symptoms in one study does not establish that such effects cannot occur. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
