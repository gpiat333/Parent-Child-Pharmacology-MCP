### Maternal effects and underlying disease

The retrieved label is **LAMOTRIGINE KIT (LAMOTRIGINE) KIT LAMOTRIGINE TABLET**, OWP Pharmaceuticals, Inc.; SPL version 15, effective October 12, 2026. It is one selected product label, not all formulations. The label’s pregnancy section was truncated in the general extract, although the structured pregnancy subsections were available. Its adult medicine-taker sections—adverse reactions, warnings, warnings and precautions, boxed warning, and drug interactions—were missing in the returned extract; this is a retrieval limitation, not evidence that those sections contain no information. [S2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label describes epilepsy-associated pregnancy risks, including preeclampsia, preterm labor, hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality. These are presented as risks associated with epilepsy, with or without antiseizure-medication exposure, and should not be treated as lamotrigine-attributable outcomes. The label also identifies poorly controlled convulsive seizures as relevant context. The AAN/AES/SMFM publication is a **practice guideline** concerning people with epilepsy of childbearing potential and pregnancy/neonatal outcomes; its abstract discusses seizure control and fetal outcomes but does not provide a lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-effect estimate. [S2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4) [S6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11175651/) [S16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)

The label reports pregnancy-related changes in lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, including decreased concentrations during pregnancy and restoration toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is source-label information, not an individualized dosing recommendation. [S2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

### Measured fetal exposure

No measured fetal lamotrigine concentration was reported in the retrieved label or the abstracts reviewed. The available human studies report maternal exposure categories—such as monotherapy exposure—not fetal concentration measurements. Therefore, **measured fetal exposure is not established by this retrieved evidence**.

### Observed fetal and child outcomes

- **Selected product-label registry summaries — source type: regulatory label; source population: pregnant women, with most exposure data from women with epilepsy.** The label reports major congenital malformations after first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy exposure of 2.2% among 1,558 infants in the International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry, 2.0% among 1,562 infants in the NAAED Pregnancy Registry, and 2.9% among 2,514 EURAP exposures. The label states that overall frequencies were similar to general-population estimates. [S2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- **Oral-cleft finding — source type: regulatory label summarizing registry and case-control evidence; source populations: NAAED-exposed infants, other international pregnancy registries, and European congenital-anomaly registries.** The label reports that NAAED observed isolated oral clefts at 3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 infants exposed early in pregnancy, compared with unexposed healthy controls. It states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45 (95% CI 0.8–2.63). These findings are not interchangeable across their different populations and comparators. [S2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- **Major congenital malformations — source type: prospective observational longitudinal cohort; source population: women with epilepsy, ages 14–55, exposed to antiseizure-medication monotherapy during pregnancy, with offspring followed to one year.** Among 3,584 lamotrigine-exposed pregnancies, 110 had major congenital malformations: 3.1% (95% CI 2.5%–3.7%). This is an observed prevalence in that cohort, not proof of causation. [S5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10949148/) [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38497990/)

- **Fetal malformations — source type: comparative observational study; source population: 490 lamotrigine-monotherapy pregnancies and 214 pregnancies in women with epilepsy unexposed to antiseizure medications during at least the first half of pregnancy.** Fetal malformation occurrence was 4.49% versus 3.27%; the reported risk ratio was 1.37 (95% CI 0.60–3.16). The abstract states that the result was not statistically significant and that a small effect was not excluded. [S18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39862525/)

- **Neurodevelopmental outcomes — source type: population-based cohort using UK primary-care and Swedish registry data; source population: children exposed to antiseizure medications during pregnancy, including infants, children, and adolescents.** The abstract reports little evidence that children exposed to lamotrigine were more likely to receive neurodevelopmental diagnoses. The abstract does not provide a lamotrigine-specific effect estimate in the retrieved text, and the study’s mixed population should not be generalized beyond its recorded groups. [S7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11568279/) [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39548057/)

- **Additional outcomes — source type: regulatory-label summary of meta-analyses; source population: lamotrigine-monotherapy pregnancy exposures.** The label says that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay, but notes that differing outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups limit interpretation. This does not establish absence of risk. [S2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- **Animal findings — source type: animal developmental studies; source population: pregnant mice, rats, and rabbits.** The label reports developmental findings including reduced fetal body weight, skeletal variations, offspring neurobehavioral abnormalities, and increased offspring mortality in particular studies. These findings are animal observations and are not human pregnancy outcome estimates. [S2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

- The reviewed abstracts were retrieved successfully, but full texts were not retrieved for those papers.
- Most human pregnancy evidence concerns epilepsy, so underlying disease, seizure control, co-medications, and treatment selection may affect comparisons.
- Registry, healthy-control, disease-matched, and untreated-epilepsy comparators answer different questions.
- MPRINT Gold search: **no lamotrigine records were returned for the pregnancy filter.** This means no records were returned by that bounded query—not that MPRINT contains no relevant record, that the drug lacks pregnancy evidence, or that lamotrigine is safe. The MPRINT snapshot is versioned and not a complete live portal. The MCP implementation attributes the linkage and interpretation caveats to its own search and mapping process. [S4](https://github.com/langli-lab/mprint-kp-data)
