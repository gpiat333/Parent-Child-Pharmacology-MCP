The warning discusses increased risk in contexts including renal impairment, hypoperfusion or hypoxemia, heart failure, older age, hepatic disease, dehydration, sepsis, and alcohol exposure. ### Label’s lactation statement

The label says animal studies found metformin in milk and states that comparable studies had not been conducted in nursing mothers. It raises a **potential** concern about hypoglycemia in nursing infants. This is a label precaution, not an observed infant outcome. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=06ade2e1-5981-4e3d-b7e9-7ff083eb4551)

### LactMed: measured exposure

LactMed reports measured metformin concentrations in breast milk, including:

- **270 mcg/L** averaged over 24 hours in six women;
- approximately **380 mcg/L** at about 4–5 hours after administration in one woman;
- average milk concentrations of **130 and 270 mcg/L** in two women sampled over 72 hours;
- average milk concentrations of **130–270 mcg/L** in three women; and
- an estimated average of **410 mcg/L** in five women sampled 4–17 days postpartum.

Infant **serum** measurements were also reported: in one group of four infants, two measurements were below **10 mcg/L**, while two were **50 and 80 mcg/L**. In another report, metformin was below **5 mcg/L** in the serum of two infants. Infant ages and extent of nursing were not always stated. These are exposure measurements, not clinical outcomes. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)

### LactMed: observed infant outcomes

The returned LactMed record describes separate populations:

- **Seven infants aged 5–25 months**: judged healthy, with growth and development progressing as expected; two also had normal Denver developmental screening results.
- **Three infants aged 2, 5, and 14 months**: no detectable adverse effects were reported.
- **Three infants aged 10–11 days postpartum**: none had low blood-glucose levels, and their mothers reported no adverse reactions.
- **A mixed feeding population from a six-month, nonrandomized prospective study:** 111 infants were included—61 predominantly breastfed and 50 formula-fed. The report found no differences between groups in height, weight, motor-social development, or rates of illness.

These findings come from different study populations and designs and should not be treated as one uniform result. The returned LactMed metadata does not establish a total study count; reference markers are not a study count. [S12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501020/)
