### Measured exposure in milk
LactMed reports **sumatriptan concentrations in breast milk**:

- In **5 breastfeeding women**, peak milk concentration averaged **87.2 mcg/L** (range **62–113 mcg/L**), measured about **2.5 hours** after administration.
- In **7 women at least 1 month postpartum**, peak milk concentration averaged **59.3 mcg/L** (range **24.6–112.8 mcg/L**), occurring **2–4 hours** after administration. The average milk concentration was **23.2 mcg/L** (range **7.7–50.6 mcg/L**).
- **Infant drug levels:** relevant published information was not found in the retrieved LactMed record. The record does not specify an infant specimen type. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

These are **drug-exposure measurements in milk**, not measurements of drug in infants.

### What was observed in infants
The record describes a report involving **3 breastfeeding women known to the manufacturer**; none reported adverse effects in their infants. The infants’ drug levels were not measured, and the exposure details were unspecified. This is a limited clinical-observation report, not a direct infant-concentration study. [S2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501255/)

Thus, the milk measurements show that sumatriptan was present in milk in those source populations, while the infant evidence consists of reported clinical observations in a separate, small group. These findings do not establish infant drug levels or provide a complete safety assessment.
