LactMed reports the following for **amoxicillin in breastfed infants**:

### Measured exposure
- In milk samples from 6 women, concentrations averaged **0.69 mg/L** (range **0.46–0.88 mg/L**) at 4 hours and **0.81 mg/L** (range **0.39–1.3 mg/L**) at 5 hours after administration.
- Relevant published **infant-level** measurements were not found as of the record’s revision date. These were milk measurements, not infant blood or serum measurements. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Observed infant symptoms
- In one telephone follow-up of 25 mothers, **3 infants had diarrhea**; no rash or candidiasis was reported.
- In a prospective study of 40 exposed infants, **2 had diarrhea and 1 had a rash**.
- Another study reported reactions in **8.3%** of amoxicillin-exposed infants, including **rash and somnolence**; this was similar to the rate in the macrolide comparison group.
- One infant developed **generalized urticaria** after breastfeeding following maternal treatment with **amoxicillin/clavulanate and gentamicin**. The responsible drug could not be determined; LactMed considered the combination, particularly amoxicillin/clavulanate, the more likely cause. This is not evidence specific to amoxicillin alone.
- A separate study of infants older than 4 weeks reported **no adverse effects**.

### Advice to monitor
The retrieved LactMed record does **not** provide a separate general recommendation to monitor particular symptoms. It reports that one study asked mothers to record tongue coating, feeding difficulties, stool changes, diaper rash, skin rash, weight change, and jaundice; this was a **study protocol**, not an observed-outcome list or a general monitoring recommendation. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)

### Uncertainty
The reports come from small observational studies, parental reports, a comparison involving related antibiotics, and a multi-drug case report. The exact study count is not established in the retrieved record, and infant drug-level data were unavailable. LactMed states that reports of rash, diarrhea, or thrush have not been adequately evaluated. These findings therefore describe reported observations and possible associations, not proof that amoxicillin caused each symptom or a quantified risk for an individual infant. [S3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500887/)
