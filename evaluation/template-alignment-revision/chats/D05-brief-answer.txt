## Sertraline during lactation: evidence brief

**Identity:** Sertraline was matched exactly to the generic ingredient. [S16](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/36437/properties.json)

### Effects on the lactating person

- LactMed reports a small observational account in which six patients reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; doses were not reported. A separate small study involved several serotonin-reuptake inhibitors, including one sertraline user, and reported delayed lactogenesis II in the inhibitor group, but no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day 4. These findings are not specific enough to establish a sertraline effect. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- The selected DailyMed label states that there are no data on sertraline’s effects on milk production. The retrieved adult label extract includes adverse-reaction information, but warnings, warnings-and-precautions, boxed-warning, and drug-interaction sections were missing from the returned extract. The label was for one product, **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, not all formulations. [S7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

**No maternal-effects conclusion:** The retrieved evidence does not establish an individualized effect on milk production or other maternal outcomes.

### Measured exposure: milk and infant specimens

Reported breastmilk concentrations included:

- Sertraline **45 mcg/L** average, range **7–207 mcg/L** in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers.
- In a 26-woman 24-hour sampling study: sertraline **129 mcg/L** average, range **11–938 mcg/L**; norsertraline **258 mcg/L** average, range **20–1498 mcg/L**.
- Other reported milk values included sertraline **26.4 mcg/L**, **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, foremilk **26 mcg/L**, hindmilk **46 mcg/L**, **<5 mcg/L**, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, median **11.3 mcg/L** with range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**, and average **87 mcg/L** with range **12–288 mcg/L**. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

These are research measurements, not dosing instructions. The returned LactMed extract did **not** include a complete infant-level concentration section. A PubMed abstract for a 12-woman study reports that sertraline was detected in three nursing infants and desmethylsertraline in six; the abstract identifies infant serum, but the full article was not retrieved. [S15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9286185/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- LactMed reports benign neonatal sleep myoclonus in one 4-month-old infant and spontaneously resolving agitation in the same case passage. It also reports no detectable acute adverse reactions in a separate group of 26 infants. These are separate source observations, not a combined study result. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- Other small groups described no observed clinical adverse reactions and reported normal six-month weight gain; the source populations were small and study details were incomplete in the returned extract. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A mixed-SSRI study reported blunted pain responses in exposed infants; four of 30 infants were exposed to sertraline. The source passage states that maternal depression-related behavior could not be ruled out because there was no control group of depressed, nonmedicated mothers. This does not establish causation by sertraline. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A separate case involved a preterm infant born at 33 weeks who developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation disorders, and high-pitched crying. The returned chronology states that symptoms worsened by day 4 while breastfeeding continued; on day 5, infant serum concentrations were in the reported adult therapeutic range; breastfeeding was discontinued on day 9, after which symptoms and serum concentrations decreased. The source attributed the reaction to sertraline in the context of prematurity and reduced metabolism. This is a single case report, not an incidence estimate. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The LactMed **Drug Levels** and **Effects in Breastfed Infants** sections were truncated, so additional outcome details are missing from the exported extract. [S19](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
