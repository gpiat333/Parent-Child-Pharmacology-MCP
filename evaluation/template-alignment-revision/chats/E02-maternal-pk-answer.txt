### 2. Maternal pharmacokinetics

**Clinical Considerations:** The label states that physiological pregnancy changes may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect; it reports decreased lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is a label-level statement, not a quantitative pharmacokinetic dataset. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A pregnancy/epilepsy review states that physiological changes can affect antiseizure-medication levels and discusses serum-concentration monitoring, but the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific numeric concentration, clearance, or area-under-the-curve results. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768086/)

A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** measured psychotropic medications in serum and breastmilk at different time points and followed exposed children for 12 months. Its abstract reports a high breastmilk concentration-to-administered-amount ratio for lamotrigine, but the returned text does not provide lamotrigine-specific numeric maternal serum values or clearance estimates. The returned population was mixed: pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children; a lamotrigine-specific pregnancy subgroup was not established in the returned metadata. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

A recruiting observational study is investigating antiseizure-medication clearance, pregnancy-related physiological factors, and fetal/infant exposure in pregnant adults with epilepsy. Recruitment and registration are not posted outcome data. [S9](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05450978)

### 4. Observed fetal and infant outcomes

#### Major congenital malformations

The selected label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: **2.2%** among **1,558** infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy in the first trimester; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: **2.0%** among **1,562** infants exposed to lamotrigine monotherapy in the first trimester.
- EURAP: **2.9%** among **2,514** first-trimester exposures; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that overall malformation frequency was similar to general-population estimates, while noting that outcome definitions, ascertainment, and comparator groups limit interpretation. These were registry observations, not an individualized risk estimate. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

#### Oral clefts

The NAAED Pregnancy Registry reported **3.2 oral-cleft cases per 1,000** among **2,200** infants exposed early in pregnancy, with a reported 95% CI of 1.4–6.3 and a threefold comparison versus unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international pregnancy registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of **1.45**, 95% CI 0.8–2.63. These retrieved sources therefore reported discordant findings. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT study record states that its case-control study found no statistically significant increased risk of oral clefts compared with other defects, but the record has no posted results; registration alone is not outcome evidence. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

#### Other fetal or child outcomes

The label says that meta-analyses evaluated fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It reports no data suggesting increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, but explicitly limits interpretation because of differences in outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The mixed naturalistic study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes between in-utero-exposed and nonexposed newborns and no significant differences in development during the first 12 months. Because the returned population included multiple medicines and pregnancy/lactation/infant groups, this is not a lamotrigine-specific risk estimate. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)
