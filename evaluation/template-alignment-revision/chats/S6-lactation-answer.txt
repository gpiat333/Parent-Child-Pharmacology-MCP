## Sertraline during lactation: balanced research summary

**Evidence status:** Evidence was retrieved, but it is not a safety verdict. The LactMed extract mentions case reports, observational studies, and a meta-analysis, but the study count is **not established from this extract**. The “Drug Levels” and “Effects in Breastfed Infants” sections are truncated, so additional findings may be missing. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

Reported **breast-milk** concentrations included:

- In a pooled analysis of 15 mothers: sertraline averaged **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**).
- In a study of 26 women with 24-hour milk sampling: sertraline averaged **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline averaged **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**).
- In four mothers sampled at two months postpartum: average milk concentrations were **26.4 mcg/L** for sertraline and **29 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- In a case involving a preterm infant, milk concentrations measured at random times over three days averaged **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline.
- Other small studies reported sertraline concentrations of **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, **26 mcg/L in foremilk and 46 mcg/L in hindmilk**, **less than 5 mcg/L** in one sample, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**, and **12–288 mcg/L**.

These are exposure measurements, not clinical outcomes. The returned extract did not include a complete infant-level concentration section; its narrative says infant serum sertraline is usually not detected and that norsertraline may be detectable at low levels, but infant-level measurement details were not retrieved in full. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Observed infant outcomes

The returned LactMed extract describes:

- **One 4-month-old infant:** benign sleep myoclonus and agitation that resolved spontaneously.
- **Twenty-six infants:** no detectable acute adverse reactions in one study.
- **Fourteen mother–infant pairs:** infant whole-blood serotonin measurements did not materially change in the reported study, and no adverse effects were observed in that group.
- **Twenty-five infants followed through six months:** reported weight gain was within national growth standards, and mothers reported no abnormal infant effects.
- **Six infants aged 5–34 weeks and seven 4-week-old infants:** no clinical adverse reactions were noted in the respective small studies.
- An uncontrolled online survey of 930 breastfeeding mothers using antidepressants reported infant “drug discontinuation” symptoms in about 10% of infants. This was not sertraline-specific and had no control group.
- A small study of infants of mothers with depression reported blunted pain responses among SSRI-exposed infants; only four of the 30 exposed infants received sertraline. The comparison group consisted of infants of nondepressed mothers, so the source could not exclude effects related to maternal depression or behavior.
- In one report, a **preterm infant born at 33 weeks** developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone abnormalities, and high-pitched crying. The report also described infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite, genetically intermediate metabolism, and an attribution by the report authors to serotonergic overstimulation associated with breastfeeding exposure and reduced metabolism. This is a single case involving a preterm infant and should not be generalized to all breastfed infants. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

The retrieved case narrative supports that symptoms began during the first day after birth, worsened on the fourth day, and that breastfeeding was stopped on the ninth postpartum day; the extract does not provide a complete chronology for every exposure change or outcome. [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Effects on lactation and milk production

These findings concern the lactating person and should be kept separate from drug concentrations in milk:

- A midwife reported decreased milk supply in six patients after sertraline initiation; the observation was uncontrolled and administered amounts were not reported.
- A small prospective study of serotonin-reuptake inhibitors, including one sertraline user, reported delayed onset of milk secretory activation compared with controls. Because the study combined several medicines and included only eight treated mothers, it does not isolate a sertraline-specific effect.
- Other observational studies concerned antidepressants or SSRIs generally and included pregnancy and postpartum treatment. Their breastfeeding findings may reflect the underlying psychiatric illness, treatment indication, or both; they do not establish a sertraline-specific effect.

The selected product label states that data on sertraline’s effects on milk production are unavailable. [S1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=687ebdc4-6946-454e-8d46-3207488ccf32)

### Source and design limitations

- Several reports involved very small groups, uncontrolled designs, maternal self-report, mixed antidepressants, or combined prenatal and breastfeeding exposure.
- Some infant findings may reflect prenatal exposure, maternal depression, co-exposures, or other factors rather than breastfeeding exposure alone.
- The retrieved LactMed extract does not establish a study count; reference markers are not study counts.
- The LactMed record is incomplete because the drug-level and infant-outcome sections were truncated. Missing information is not evidence of safety.
- The selected DailyMed record is for **SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE TABLET [REMEDYREPACK INC.]**, effective November 5, 2026. The exact product match was not confirmed.
