## Lamotrigine pregnancy evidence

**Evidence status:** Lamotrigine identity was matched exactly in RxNorm. This is a bounded evidence summary, not a systematic review or clinical validation. [S26](https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui/28439/properties.json)

### Maternal effects and underlying disease

No lamotrigine-specific maternal adverse-outcome estimate was retrieved from the selected label. The pregnancy evidence is predominantly from people with **epilepsy**. The label describes epilepsy-related pregnancy risks—including hypertensive complications, preterm labor, bleeding, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, prematurity, fetal death, and maternal mortality—and identifies poorly controlled convulsive seizures as an important underlying-disease context. These are disease-associated risks, not outcomes attributed specifically to lamotrigine. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A separate narrative review concerns **pregnancy/postpartum in people with bipolar disorder** and discusses lamotrigine among several treatments, but the retrieved abstract does not provide lamotrigine-specific pregnancy outcome estimates. This population should not be combined with the epilepsy registry populations. [S25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593436/)

### Fetal or infant exposure

- **Pregnancy:** No numeric fetal concentration measurement was retrieved.
- **Maternal pharmacokinetics:** The selected label reports that pregnancy-related physiological changes may affect lamotrigine concentrations and therapeutic effect, with reports of lower concentrations during pregnancy and return toward pre-pregnancy concentrations after delivery. This is source-label context, not an individualized medication plan. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)
- **Lactation-only exposure evidence:** A naturalistic study of **60 mothers** assessed serum and breastmilk concentrations of several psychotropic medicines and child development during the first 12 months. Its mixed population included pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children; the abstract reported a high breastmilk concentration-by-dose ratio for lamotrigine but supplied no lamotrigine-specific numeric concentration. This finding should not be treated as a pregnancy fetal-exposure measurement. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Observed fetal or infant outcomes

For **first-trimester lamotrigine monotherapy**, the selected label reports:

- International Lamotrigine Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.2% of 1,558 infants**; 95% CI 1.6%–3.1%.
- NAAED Pregnancy Registry: major congenital malformations in **2.0% of 1,562 infants**.
- EURAP: major birth defects in **2.9% of 2,514 exposures**; 95% CI 2.3%–3.7%.

The label states that overall malformation frequencies were similar to general-population estimates, while the populations and comparators differed. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

**Isolated oral clefts:** The NAAED registry reported **3.2 per 1,000 among 2,200 early-pregnancy exposures**, described as threefold higher than unexposed healthy controls. The label states that this finding was not observed in other large international registries. A European case-control study reported an adjusted odds ratio of 1.45, 95% CI 0.8–2.63. Thus, this is an inconsistent, comparator-sensitive signal rather than a finding replicated across the cited registries. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

The label also describes analyses of fetal death, stillbirth, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth, and neurodevelopmental delay. It states that the available data did not suggest increased risk with lamotrigine monotherapy, but limits interpretation because outcome definitions, ascertainment methods, and comparator groups differed. [S10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=474fb321-575c-4b97-9dbe-080061652ee4)

A completed EUROCAT registry record reported that its earlier case-control analysis found no statistically significant increased oral-cleft risk compared with other defects; however, the record has **no posted results**, so the registry summary is not equivalent to a full published outcome report. The returned source population was congenital-anomaly registry records, with age information not established in the returned metadata. [S4](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01055327)

The 60-mother psychotropic-medication study reported no significant differences in birth outcomes or development through 12 months between exposed and nonexposed children, but it was not lamotrigine-only and its abstract does not establish a lamotrigine-specific effect. Its returned source population was mixed: pregnancy, lactation, neonates, and children. [S22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36103361/)

### Main limitations

- The PubMed search returned 690 records, but only a bounded set of abstracts was retrieved; it was not a systematic review. [S11](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=%28%28%22lamotrigine%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BSubstance+Name%5D+OR+%22lamotrigine%22%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29%29+AND+%28%22Pregnancy%22%5BMeSH+Terms%5D+OR+pregnan%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D+OR+gestation%2A%5BTitle%2FAbstract%5D%29&retmax=5&sort=relevance&retmode=json)
- Most retrieved literature was review or guideline material rather than lamotrigine-specific primary research.
- Pregnancy evidence is concentrated in epilepsy populations; bipolar-disorder evidence should be considered separately.
- Monotherapy versus polytherapy, gestational timing, comparator selection, seizure control, comorbidities, and other exposures may affect interpretation.
- The selected DailyMed label is one lamotrigine product label, not all formulations. Its pregnancy section was truncated in the broader extract; structured pregnancy data were available, but missing extracted sections are not proof that the information is absent elsewhere.
