## Sertraline during breastfeeding: LactMed evidence

LactMed’s record describes sertraline and its metabolite, norsertraline, in breast milk and reports infant-level measurements and clinical observations. The retrieved record was revised **August 15, 2026**; the extract is bounded, with the **Drug Levels** and **Effects in Breastfed Infants** sections truncated. [S44](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

### Measured exposure

Reported **breast-milk** concentrations included:

- Sertraline: mean **45 mcg/L** (range **7–207 mcg/L**) in a pooled analysis of 15 mothers. [S8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15169695/)
- In 26 women sampled over 24 hours: sertraline mean **129 mcg/L** (range **11–938 mcg/L**) and norsertraline mean **258 mcg/L** (range **20–1,498 mcg/L**). [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- In four mothers sampled at random times at two months postpartum: sertraline **26.4 mcg/L** and norsertraline **29 mcg/L**. [S6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- In one foremilk/hindmilk comparison, concentrations were **26 mcg/L** and **46 mcg/L**, respectively, with sampling 13 hours after a dose. [S44](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- In a case involving a preterm infant, randomly timed breast-milk averages were **201 mcg/L** for sertraline and **358 mcg/L** for norsertraline. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- Other reported sertraline milk concentrations included **29 mcg/L**, **58 mcg/L**, **<5 mcg/L**, **11.1–24.5 mcg/L**, **11.3 mcg/L** (range **3.6–35.7 mcg/L**), and **87 mcg/L** (range **12–288 mcg/L**). Sampling times varied or were not stated in some reports. [S44](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)

Infant-level information was retrieved using the source’s terminology of **infant serum**. In one report involving three breastfed infants, sertraline and norsertraline were undetectable in some infant-serum samples; another infant had detectable norsertraline but no detectable sertraline. In a separate group of 30 breastfed infants aged 6–13 weeks, 22 had undetectable infant-serum sertraline; eight had detectable levels averaging **7.9 mcg/L**. [S43](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9108810/) [S3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11483479/)

### Observed infant outcomes

- A study of 26 infants reported no detectable acute adverse reactions attributed to sertraline in breast milk. [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12590627/)
- Other small reports described no clinically observed adverse reactions in six infants and no adverse effects in seven 4-week-old infants. [S9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367050/) [S10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15367055/)
- One 4-month-old infant had benign neonatal sleep myoclonus and spontaneously resolving agitation. [S43](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9108810/)
- A retrospective study of 30 nursing mothers reported adverse effects in five infants; reported effects included insomnia, restlessness, constant crying and poor feeding. The retrieved LactMed passage is truncated, so additional details are missing. [S44](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/)
- A case report described a preterm infant who developed hyperthermia, muscle-tone regulation disorders and high-pitched crying. The source reported infant serum concentrations of sertraline and its metabolite and described the symptoms as attributed by the authors to serotonergic overstimulation associated with breast-milk exposure and reduced metabolism. [S19](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23249132/)
- One report of 25 breastfed infants described normal six-month weight gain and no abnormal effects reported by the mothers. [S6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12716242/)
- A small report involving 25 breastfed infants found no adverse reactions; the breastfeeding proportions were specified for some infants but not all. [S24](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25589155/)

These observations come from small studies, case reports and other reports summarized by LactMed. The extract does not establish a total study count, and reported absence of an outcome in a study does not establish that the outcome was absent from all infants.

### Effects on lactation and breast milk

A midwife’s observations described six mothers who reported decreased milk supply after starting sertraline; doses and other details were not reported. [S44](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501191/) A small study of serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor users, including one sertraline user, reported delayed onset of milk secretory activation compared with controls, but no statistically significant difference in feeding difficulties after day four. [S13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19965920/)

Broader studies of antidepressant or SSRI exposure reported differences in breastfeeding initiation or continuation, but some did not specify the antidepressant and others included multiple SSRIs; these findings do not isolate a sertraline-specific effect. [S17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22344850/) [S27](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28682318/) [S28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28984528/) [S37](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35170756/)
