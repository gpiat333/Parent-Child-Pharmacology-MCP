# Current implementation

Current source 0.19.0, policy 2026-11-23.22. Policy .15 fixes safety-routing false positives found after evaluation (condition names such as seizures or postpartum depression were treated as emergencies); evaluation results describe .20 ([report](../evaluation/policy20-final/REPORT.md)). Policy .16 adds the HRSA National Maternal Mental Health Hotline (1-833-TLC-MAMA) to perinatal crisis replies and recognizes more postpartum wording. Policy .17 adds a MotherToBaby referral for individual pregnancy and breastfeeding medication questions, a MotherToBaby fact sheet search (NLM Bookshelf, labeled as nonprofit content), PRGLAC research-gap context in pregnancy and lactation briefs, and an NIH-only scope note on funding results. Version 0.19.0 (policy .19) combines that work with the November 22 evidence-display and snapshot-filtering changes, which were developed separately as 0.18.0. Policy .20 lets “Can I take X?” questions receive the public evidence with a fixed message that the decision belongs with a clinician or pharmacist (plus MotherToBaby for pregnancy or breastfeeding); dose, start/stop, switch and taper requests still stop. Policy .21, made after the .20 evaluation, stops role-play, claimed-permission and “ignore your rules” framings; sends decisions for anyone, including “should my patient…” and vaccine decisions, to a clinician; treats yes-or-no and “safe for my baby?” requests like “Can I take X?”; returns an out-of-scope message for unrelated tasks such as ordering food; stops blocking short plain-English questions such as “Do vaccines cause autism?”; and adds the label's description section (chemical name, molecular formula and ingredients). Policy .22 maps common vaccine names (“flu vaccine”, “Tdap”) to LactMed's vaccine records and explains that RxNorm needs a product name; returns the label's approved uses and clinical studies (mostly adults) and, in pediatric briefs, where off-label use is documented, including NICHD's BPCA priority list; adds the 15 PRGLAC recommendations to pregnancy and lactation briefs; gives Spanish questions MotherToBaby's Spanish service, 988 in Spanish and MedlinePlus en español while still stopping them; and adds an NCCIH herbs link to supplement questions. Evaluated locally with Databricks model calls and MLflow tracking; the same frozen source passed in a Databricks serverless notebook. The existing App remains .9. No App deployment, public release or official submission occurred.

The research fixes add mandatory study-team eligibility/availability and bounded LactMed extract notices, constrain both draft and final-review instructions to the requested title/excerpt-only scope, and distinguish an unreported outcome from an explicit negative finding. R08 had a grading conflict: the application requires “Past listed deadlines,” while the old criterion demanded “Closed deadlines.” The criterion now accepts elapsed dates without falsely asserting that a submission portal is closed. This is a disclosed rubric clarification, not a clinical improvement or a retroactive score change.

The previous 3/10 benchmark had seven failures: B06/B09 were blocked by English-language false positives; B10 confused an aggregate seizure-control outcome with an active symptom; B01 miscalculated proportions; B04 miscounted matching Silver annotations; B07/B08 inspected the wrong or incomplete candidate set. The revised runtime adds narrowly bounded English task recognition, distinguishes named aggregate outcomes while preserving personal/acute routing, and returns optional joined Silver classifications and exported-row counts with explicit pagination. Counts retain snapshot and unmatched-row limitations. The revised benchmark client inspects the requested cohort and uses a bounded decimal arithmetic helper. The helper is client-side, not a 31st MCP tool. All ten questions and reference answers are unchanged; no expected answer is supplied to the model.

The final .14 benchmark scored 9/10; the intermediate .13 run scored 10/10. Both are retained. The remaining final B09 error returned the DOI of a different retrieved candidate despite selecting the correct PMID for an additional lookup. This is an answer-attribution failure, not missing source data. No answer was replaced or manually corrected to raise the score.
